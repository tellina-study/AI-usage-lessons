# What stays in the context after the answer

## Assertion

Reading is worth delegating when a short conclusion is what you need: a few places, a fact, a yes or no. If you are going to have to work with the whole volume you read — you will have to read it yourself, that is part of the work.

## Visual

- Delegating the reading makes sense when what has to be brought out of dozens of files is a short conclusion: a fact, a yes/no, a list of places a few lines long that the work then proceeds from. Forty files of searching, four files of editing — exactly this case.
- If you are going to have to work with the whole volume you read — a refactoring of those same forty files — you will have to read them in any case; doing that in your own context is then part of the work itself, because it is you who will be working with those contents next.

> What we set up for the sake of the context is decided by what will be left in the context after the answer, and not by how many files had to be opened. Intuition said: a separate worker will read faster. The test showed: forty files stay forty files and the same length of time, and two things change — whose context holds them and where the result comes back to on its own.

**About yourself.** Name to yourself a place in your own project where, before an edit, you have to search across dozens of files and what you find is three or four lines. Named it — there is your first candidate for delegation. If you are going to be editing all those same dozens of files, that is not the candidate.

## Speaker notes

Delegating the reading makes sense when what has to be brought out of dozens of files is a short conclusion: a fact, a yes or no, a list of places a few lines long that the work then proceeds from. Forty files of searching and four files of editing — exactly this case.

If you are going to have to work with the whole volume you read — a refactoring that touches those same forty files — you will have to read them in any case, and doing that in your own context is then part of the work itself: it is you who will be working with those contents next.

The boundary between the two situations runs along a single question: do you need the whole material further on, or the addresses inside it. The protagonist needed four places out of forty files, and the contents of the other thirty-six would never have been of use to him again. A refactoring of those same forty is a different matter: there the contents are the subject of the work, and no short conclusion comes out of them.

The rule this folds into: what we set up for the sake of the context is decided by what will be left in the context after the answer. The number of files opened has no bearing on that decision.

And the axis line the case stood for. Intuition said: a separate worker will read faster. The test showed: forty files stay forty files and the same length of time, and two things change — whose context holds them and where the result comes back to on its own.

The question about yourself, and it is worth carrying out to the end. Name a place in your own project where, before an edit, you have to search across dozens of files and what you find is three or four lines. Named it — that is your first candidate for delegation. Now test it against the second point: are you going to be editing those same dozens of files, or the lines you found? If the first — the candidate drops out, and it drops out rightly.

About the boundary from the previous screen: the same principle applies to it too. Connect unnecessary servers to a subagent and only the result will still come back, but the subagent's context will be taken up earlier and harder than it needs to be. The criterion stays in force; what this sharpens is the size of the saving itself.

Separately — the format of the subagent's answer, which is easy to take for a presentational detail. Asking a subagent to come back with a retelling of what it read instead of a conclusion is technically possible, and that is where the saving ends: a subagent told to retell forty files will return those same forty files into your context, only in its own words. Exactly the same arithmetic as with a second session: the longer what comes back, the less point there was in taking the reading away. The format of the subagent's answer is part of the criterion.

And the general shape of the rule beyond this case. A subagent is a means of managing what sits in the context; everything it gives and everything it costs is read off that one property. The next two cases of this rung will add two other reasons to set one up — work in several streams and dividing workers into zones of their own — and each of them is counted the same way: by what is left in the context once the subagent has closed.
