## Speaker notes

The tenth case — Toys R Us and Coca-Cola marketing failure. These are two related cases of 2024. Toys R Us — a well-known American toy retailer that went through bankruptcy in 2017 and was partly revived. In 2024 the company tried to make a prestige creative comeback via AI: at Cannes Lions 2024 — the largest festival of the advertising industry — a 66-second single clip was presented, entirely generated via Sora 2. The idea was nostalgia-driven: to return to the founder figure Charles Lazarus with an AI-generated reconstruction. The public reaction was devastating. Sentiment analysis showed a sharp swing. Before the clip's release: positive sentiment around the brand — twelve point two percent, negative — thirteen point five. After the release: positive collapsed to three point four percent, negative spiked to fifty-three point four. This is a structural negative shift of forty percentage points in the negative direction. Joe Russo, a well-known film director, publicly quoted: "AI ads fucking suck." Coca-Cola — a parallel case. Christmas 2024, Coca-Cola released an AI-generated reconstruction of the flagship "Holidays are coming" campaign — a re-run of the flagship 1995 campaign. The reaction was tepid to negative — mass criticism of the "soulless" nature of AI-generated content where the audience expected emotional human direction. Despite this, Coca-Cola repeated the AI-ad pattern in 2025 too. This is an institutional commitment to AI cost savings even at measurable brand damage. What these cases demonstrate. An AI ad is possible in principle. But a flagship seasonal campaign is territory where the audience expects human direction, and an AI shortcut there creates measurable brand damage. Lesson for the engineer: an AI ad is possible, but a flagship seasonal campaign without human direction equals brand damage. Brand-trust risk is measured by a sentiment reversal, not CTR. CTR in one campaign may be normal — this does not mean the brand suffered no long-term damage. Sentiment analysis is the second mandatory layer of measurement for an AI-marketing rollout. And most importantly — where a brand's heritage is flagship, adopting an AI corner-cut is a structural decision against brand capital. This is not just "save costs" — it is "spend brand capital."
