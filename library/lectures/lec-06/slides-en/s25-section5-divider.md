## Speaker notes

The previous parts showed how specific classes fail. This part is the concentrated core of the course's central idea: the criteria by which an engineer says "no" in advance, and which tool is more correct in each case.

There is one decisive rule: the higher the determinism of the problem and the cost of error, the less room there is for probabilistic AI. In this part we will unfold this into a table of criteria — a certification calculation, the safety factor, normative inspection against GOST, legal responsibility, the availability of an exact solver, safety of life, units at a seam between systems, a change to the force scheme — and for each criterion we will name why probabilistic AI structurally does not fit, and which tool is the right one. Then we will add concrete Russian normative boundaries: the ESKD barrier, Rostekhnadzor attestation, the requirements of Federal Law 187 and Federal Law 58, and the question of copyright. And we will close the part with a conceptual boundary that ties the spine together: the engineer sets the constraints and is responsible — the signature is a deontological, not a technical, responsibility; AI proposes the shape, but the human is responsible, verifies, and invents the function. It's important: this is not a ban on AI, but a designation of its place.
