# Visible content

## Title bar
Assembly is nearly free. So why does almost no one capture the value?

## Body
[Hero visual, split: an accelerating clock on the left / a leaking funnel on the right]

**Two facts, both true at once**
- Weeks of work → hours (Anthropic's internal experience)
- ~95% of organizations reportedly get zero return (MIT, summer 2025)

[Open question, bottom]
How can both facts be true at the same time?

## Speaker notes

Let's start not with a definition but with a paradox. Anthropic describes its own internal experience like this: tasks that used to take weeks of writing and reviewing code now take hours — an agent writes the implementation, it passes the tests, and code is nearly free. At the same time, in the summer of 2025, a research group at the MIT Media Lab published a report that media outlets amplified as "ninety-five percent of organizations get zero return from generative AI." Two facts are true at once and, at first glance, mutually exclusive: assembly is nearly free — and almost no one extracts value from it. How is that possible?

We won't resolve the paradox right now — it's the payoff of the whole lecture, and we'll come back to it at the very end. But let's fix its shape right away: assembly became nearly free, while turning assembly into real value did not. That exact mismatch is the subject of today's lecture. We'll walk the whole product lifecycle: from intent through research, design, build, measurement, operation — and at every phase we'll ask the same thing: what did AI make cheap here, and what stayed expensive and human.

Take thirty seconds: if building became nearly free, what became the product's real bottleneck?
