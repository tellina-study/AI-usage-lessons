## Visible slide text
**The physician came back after a year with no procedures** — an administrative post, illness, parental leave. Same diploma, authorization never revoked: there was nothing to revoke it for.

**The first procedure goes differently:** the scope moves slower, the hand takes longer to find the angle, he passes a fold by. No complications, the report is written, **and he doesn't know it.** A bad examination looks exactly like a good examination in which nothing was found.

**That is why three pillars keep him in form, and none of them asks the person himself:** volume of practice for the year, authorization from the organization, periodic re-certification.

**Russia:** periodic accreditation once every five years — 144 hours of study plus a portfolio. **The specialist writes the work report himself. There is no numeric threshold on the result — neither a finding rate nor a withdrawal time — in the requirements.**

**The model took over one action out of eight** — the withdrawal examination. **One was added: whether to trust the box.**

## Speaker notes

The physician came back after a year with no procedures: an administrative post, a long illness, parental leave, a transfer to another department. Same diploma, certificate still valid, authorization never revoked — there was nothing to revoke it for. The first procedure goes differently. The scope advances more slowly. The hand takes longer to find the angle for passing a bend. A fold that needs checking, the physician passes by.

And here is the reason this picture stands here: he himself does not know it. The procedure went without complications, the report is written, the patient is sent home. There is no feedback and there won't be: a growth that went unseen stays in the person until the next examination, and that is years away. A bad examination looks exactly like a good examination in which nothing was found. That is why the three pillars are built the way they are: a specialist's own sense of well-being is a useless instrument for measuring his form.

The room knows this picture from the other side. A skill not used for a year is lost — everyone who has gone back to a language or a subject area after a break knows this. There is one difference. In engineering work, the nearest build, test, or code review reports the loss of skill the same day; here the nearest instrument that reports it is the same patient's next visit, years later, and to a different physician.

Now the pillars — how this decision is made with no model at all. There are three of them, they work at the same time, and none of them asks the person himself.

Volume of practice: professional societies set a minimum number of procedures per year, below which a specialist is considered to have fallen out of form. Accreditation: authorization is issued for one kind of activity, issued by the organization that is responsible for that activity, and tied to the workplace and the procedure — it can be narrowed, suspended, not renewed. Re-certification: qualification is confirmed periodically, and confirmation presupposes both accumulated study and accumulated practice, because between two confirmations a specialist's condition changes, and in both directions.

The three pillars answer one question — is the person staying in form — and none of them turns to the person himself. The profession does not ask the physician whether he is in form: it counts on his behalf, by the detection rate and the withdrawal time.

Now how this is set up in Russia, because that is closer to the room than any foreign guidelines. A specialist's periodic accreditation takes place once every five years; the procedure is set by Order No. 709n of the Russian Ministry of Health, dated October 28, 2022. It requires accumulating 144 hours of continuing-education training — in one course or several — or at least 72 hours of continuing-education training plus informal education recorded on the Continuing Medical Education portal — and submitting a portfolio: a report on professional activity over those five years with information on education and length of service.

And here is what follows from this, and it has to be phrased precisely. Saying "the result of the work is not looked at in Russia" would be stronger than true: the portfolio does require a report on professional activity, and it does contain the results of the work performed in the job function. What is true is something else: the specialist draws up this report himself. There is no numeric threshold on the result — one that would be counted on his behalf and that would block renewal of authorization if not met — in the requirements: not a finding rate, not a withdrawal time. That is, a Russian endoscopist has no mandatory external numeric check on his own form — the very thing the whole section is built around.

An earlier version of this slide said "the result of the work — none at all": a statement stronger than the source supports — exactly the class of error we've been examining in vendors. Here, the detection rate is counted where a center decides on its own to count it, not because accreditation asks for it. So a Russian endoscopist is missing, as a mandatory matter, exactly the instrument the physician from the opening picture lacked: there is nowhere for him to learn of his own decline.

Now about the field of work itself, so that it's clear what the model intervenes in. The examination is made up of eight actions. Prepare the patient. Insert the scope to the cecum. Examine during withdrawal. Check behind the fold. Remove what's found. Send it for histology. Write up the report. Tally the physician's own metrics. The person does the first seven by hand; the profession does the eighth for him, per published guidelines, and he himself takes no part in that count.

Of these eight, the trained model took over one — the withdrawal examination, where a box appears in real time on the relevant part of the image. And one step was added: deciding whether to trust that box. Preparation, inserting the scope, the fold, removing what's found, histology, the report, and tallying metrics stayed with people and the profession.

And the three places where the model does not work are named by what is missing there. It cannot maintain skill — the direct opposite is measured, and that is the next slide's breakdown. It has nothing to show the physician that he would not have seen without highlighting, inside the procedure itself. It cannot measure his own result without the assistant, and neither can any procedure in any jurisdiction. The direction the tool is developing in is worth noting right away: from detection it is moving toward characterizing what is found, and further toward automatically tallying the physician's own metrics — that is, it is also becoming a measuring instrument for the person who uses it.

Only one question for the room is asked in this stage, and it is a prediction, not a poll: of the four measurement methods named in the previous slide, one measures the result of the work, and three measure behavior and volume. I ask the room to name which one measures the result, and to say why it is easiest to substitute with withdrawal time. The answer: the detection rate; it gets substituted because time is easier to hold to, and the rate cannot be raised by trying harder. There is deliberately no second question in this stage: two questions in a row read to the room as rhetoric, and they answer neither.
