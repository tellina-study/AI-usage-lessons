# 4. Space for AI

**A mirror of the whole lecture: it isn't that space is making AI smarter — AI is physically moving into space.**

In order:

**Starcloud** — the first data-center-class GPU in space
**Google Project Suncatcher** — a satellite with tensor processing units

## Speaker notes

This section closes out the four types of AI contribution: from autonomy of action, through remote control and thinking at the drafting board, to the question of where the computing infrastructure itself physically sits — a mirror of Section 1, where the machine moved into space to act where a human cannot be.

Two breakdowns in order: Starcloud-1 — the first data-center-class GPU, an Nvidia H100, in space, already serving real requests to the open Gemma model. Google Project Suncatcher — a refrigerator-sized satellite equipped with four tensor processing units, with a plan to grow the constellation to 81 spacecraft in a single laser-linked cluster.

Why does this section come last, closing the lecture, rather than somewhere in the middle? It depends logically on the material of all three previous sections at once: the demand for computing that this section serves is created by the growth in data and the growth in the number of systems broken down in the previous sections — rovers and excavators generate telemetry, teleoperated systems stream video, surrogate models and forecasts themselves require computing power. This section is the answer to the question "where is it physically cheaper to do all this computing work," which can only be asked once it has been shown how much of this work has appeared at all.
