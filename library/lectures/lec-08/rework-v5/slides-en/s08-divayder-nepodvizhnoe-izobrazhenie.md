# Section 1. Still Image
## concept art, illustration, key frame

The cheapest output unit — which is why it's the most mature practice, and the only modality where there are numbers on both sides of the ledger.

**Three cases:**

— The collapse of the per-image unit price

— T—Zh trained its own model on a home GPU

— Airbus's bionic partition — the win isn't from a trained model

*3 cases · 1 legal · 4 mentions*

## Speaker notes

This section opens the evening. It comes first by unit price, and importance has nothing to do
with it. A single image is the cheapest output unit of all five modalities — two to three orders
of magnitude cheaper than the next one. Everything else follows from this: the practice here is
the most mature, because people started trying it earliest and most cheaply; there are numbers on
both sides of the ledger, because the classical mode is measured too; and this is exactly the
place to take apart how the method works, at the depth it requires. Everything built into the
image repeats, with adjustments, in sound, video, and the 3D scene — there's no reason to take it
apart a second time in every section, so the lecture's load-bearing technical block sits here.

The three cases follow one form, and that form will repeat all evening. First comes the price of
both errors — the one where "we made it and it didn't fit," and the opposite one, "we didn't try
the variant that would have worked." Then the classical modes, counted in the same unit as the
model: without that, no "X times cheaper" is legible. Then the model next to them. And only
then — what moved downstream to the right, meaning what work, load, or risk showed up where it
hadn't been before. A win counted from zero instead of from the classical mode is never accepted
this evening.

The section's three cases have different completeness in their second step, and that's not a flaw
in the material — it's a substantive difference worth noting. In the first, both classical modes
are counted in the same unit as the model. In the second, the classical mode is named but has no
number of its own, and that will be stated outright on the slide, in a line, not left unsaid. In
the third, a second classical mode doesn't exist at all, and the reason is named — and this case
turns out to be the most instructive of the three, because there's no trained model in the loop
at all, and the win gets attributed to one anyway.

Besides the three cases, the section will include one legal case — the only detailed breakdown of
a court ruling all evening — and four mentions, each with a number. A mention without a number is
never put up this evening: a list of product names is a bibliography, and its place is in the
reading materials, not on the slide.
