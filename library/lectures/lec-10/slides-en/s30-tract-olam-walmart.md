## Speaker notes

Besides Cargill, several key platforms operate on the fourth rung in 2026.

Tract — a startup founded in 2023 by four anchor customers: ADM, Cargill, LDC, ofi — Olam Food Ingredients. It raised, in a 2024-2025 Series A round, eighteen point six million euros led by Icos Capital. The CEO since January 2025 — Allison Kopf. The main architectural feature: the leading commodity-market traders — direct competitors — jointly invest in shared infrastructure for compliance, scope-3 emissions reporting, and product-provenance tracking. This is a rare case in the industry when competitors share infrastructure.

The explanation — Tract is a data backbone, not agentic per se. It's an infrastructure layer on top of which agents work — for example, compliance agents at specific clients use Tract as a source of truth about suppliers. This is a critical distinction often missed in reviews: Tract is not "agentic", Tract is "shared data infrastructure for agentic applications at competing companies".

Olam Mindsprint — spun off from Olam International in 2024. A platform of three components. Farmsprint — plantation management. Procuresprint — agentic procurement: the agent selects a supplier by price, compliance, ETA automatically. Tradesprint — agents for commodity trading. In 2026 Wipro announced that the Mindsprint engagement is one of the largest strategic transformation partnerships of 2026. Procuresprint — a concrete example of agentic procurement: instead of a trader manually checking dozens of suppliers against hundreds of contract items, the agent compares them automatically and raises to a human only the nonstandard cases. This is AI in the sense of augmentation, not replacement — the human stays in the loop for final approval, the agent does the routine comparative work.

Walmart uses the Cropin Cloud platform to forecast yields across supplier farms in the US and South America — the partnership was announced in March 2025. The geography matters: US plus South American markets, not India — this is sometimes confused in reviews, because Cropin is an Indian company founded in Bangalore. The claimed effect — a reduction of food loss; the "-20%" figure slips into review materials but not into primary press releases with an explicit URL; it should be treated as a vendor-direction claim, not a peer-reviewed metric. This is an example of how the fourth rung — the supply chain — descends to the first via scope-3 visibility: Walmart wants to know how many tomatoes will come from a specific farm in three weeks.

Tesco — the British retailer uses AI demand forecasting since 2017; the claimed effect minus thirty percent food waste since 2017. Tesco is an example of the fifth rung, consumer and retail, but included here as an illustration that the AI methods of the fourth and fifth are close. More on L5 — in the next section after the environment.

The main misattribution warnings we'll use going forward: Tract is not agentic, Tract is a data backbone; Olam Procuresprint — the only explicitly agentic one in this four; Walmart × Cropin covers US + South America, not India.
