## Speaker notes

The structure of the lecture mirrors the ladder of five levels of AI penetration into agriculture, which we'll be climbing from field to shelf. Before each section there is a short divider showing where we are.

Section 1 — L1 Field. The lowest level: an open biological environment, maximum unpredictability. Here we'll cover See & Spray Ultimate as the canonical success case, the L1 vendor matrix, the foundation models of 2026, and then three failures: the collapse of vertical farming, the hallucinations of a general-purpose LLM in the role of an agronomist, and Plantix accuracy.

Section 2 — L2 Robot. A semi-controlled environment. Narrow specialization works: LaserWeeder, Saga UV-C, Tevel. The universal "autonomous farm robot" goes bankrupt: Monarch, FarmWise. Here too is the critical comparison of Cognitive Pilot and ITELMA (a Russian automotive-electronics maker) as an architectural choice within the AI domain.

Section 3 — L3 Animal. A semi-closed environment with measurements at the level of an individual animal. AI works more reliably here: SenseHub, CattleEye, DeLaval VMS. The limitations — the physical barn configuration, Holstein bias, and the economics of small farms.

Section 4 — L4 Supply chain. Controlled cargo flows plus fast feedback in basis points. This is the place where agentic AI leads in production deployment: Cargill CMAX, Tract, Olam Mindsprint. But federal policy is a tail risk: the cancellation of USDA Climate-Smart Commodities in April 2025 showed this.

Section 4-bis — Environment. Connectivity, vendor lock-in, regulation. This is a cross-cutting meta-level, without which none of the rungs work.

Section 5 — L5 Consumer plus payoff. The most mature layer, the only one where AI works reliably for established players. And the main consolidation — the five anti-AI criteria plus a callback to Plenty Compton.

The central question at the bottom — keep it in mind throughout the lecture.
