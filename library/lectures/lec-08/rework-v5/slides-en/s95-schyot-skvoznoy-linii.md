## Visible slide text

**Seven, where both sides are measured in the same unit:** image price · microdramas · the NBA finals spot · the Christmas campaign · a newsroom with machine-written articles · Russian outlets · dubbing.

**Three, where there is no baseline from the classical side, and it is named out loud:** how much the same banner would have cost from a staff designer · how many hours of voice banking the clinic's protocol requires · how much a retrofit for a planetarium dome would cost.

**Three, where the classical mode does not exist at all:** the partition — a shape optimization with no trained model · the actor's voice in the classical mode is either missing or belongs to someone else · for the scrolls there is exactly one alternative, "never to be read."

**Zero is a convenient baseline:** it always produces an impressive number and is always wrong — somebody did the work before the model. The borderline thirteenth case is named: for dubbing, the first classical mode is counted, the second (subtitles) is not.

## Speaker notes

The throughline of this lecture ran through every section: the win comes from devaluing the failed attempt. There is only one way to check a claim like that — by recounting, in how many of the analyzed cases the win is measured honestly, that is, against the classical mode. Zero is a convenient baseline: it always produces an impressive number and is always wrong, because somebody did the work before the model, and did it for known money.

The count is kept against the case registry and shown in full, so that it can be disputed.

Thirteen cases are analyzed in detail — these are the ones that went through all five steps of the analysis: the price of the error, the classical modes in the same unit, the model set next to them, the mechanism, and what moved rightward. Next to them stands one legal case that does not go into this denominator, because a court ruling has no classical mode in the same sense.

In seven cases the win is measured against the classical mode, meaning both sides are measured in the same unit. That is the price of a single image, where a commission, stock, and generation are compared in dollars per piece. The Chinese microdramas, where a live-action release and a generated one are compared in yuan per release. The spot for the NBA finals, where a reduction is named against an ordinary spot in the same slot. The Christmas campaign, where the team's headcount before and after stands side by side. The newsroom that published machine-written articles, where the counterfactual — how often a newsroom errs without the model — sits on the same frame. The Russian outlets, with their measurement on the same sample by the same method. And dubbing: there the first classical mode is measured in the same unit — fifty to four hundred fifty dollars per finished minute for a voice actor against 0.03–0.072 dollars for synthesis; the second mode, subtitles, is named but not counted, so the comparison there is incomplete, but the win in it is honest.

In three cases there is no baseline from the classical side, and that is said out loud right on the frame of the analysis itself. How much the same banner would have cost from a staff designer is not stated in the publication. How many hours of recording voice banking requires under a clinic's protocol — no source names it. How much a classical retrofit of an image for a spherical dome would have cost — it is not in the sources. These are honest gaps, and each one is named exactly where it sits.

In three more, the classical mode does not exist at all, and that is a property of the task itself: work that was never done before has nothing to be compared against. The bionic partition is built through shape optimization with no trained model — an analysis of misattribution: the win there is real, but it was delivered by a different technology than the one credited with it. The voice of an actor who lost speech has, in the classical mode, either nothing at all or belongs to someone else. For the scrolls charred by the eruption, there is exactly one alternative — "never to be read."

The sum: seven plus three plus three equals thirteen. The count checks out, and that matters: if it did not check out, it would mean some case got lost between the registry and the frames.

What this number says. Seven out of thirteen — more than half, and for an industry lecture on generative models that is a good result: in most of the analyzed cases the win stays a win after the counted classical mode is set next to it. The three cases where there is no classical mode at all are the most valuable in the set — these are tasks that were never solved before. A scroll that lay unread for two thousand years was read in full on June 25, 2026, and not a single letter in it was generated.

And a habit worth taking away in place of the number. Before agreeing with any claim about a model's win, ask it three questions in a row: what was it compared against — the classical mode or zero; in what units was each side counted; where is the denominator. Three questions close off almost every error in this field's industry surveys, including the ones that showed up in this very lecture's own material and were named out loud.
