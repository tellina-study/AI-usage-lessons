# An AI-run farm promised a revolution. 19 months — and it closed

## Assertion

Plenty Unlimited Compton — opened May 2023 with the mayor's delegation, closed December 2024. $940M lost, valuation $1.9B → <$15M (–99%). It closed not because of bad AI — because of LED thermodynamics.

## Speaker notes

Let's start with one date and one figure that we'll need in order to understand the other five sections of the lecture. In May 2023, in the city of Compton, California, there was a grand opening ceremony for the flagship of American vertical farming — the Plenty Unlimited greenhouse. The mayor's delegation, the company's CEO, reporters, a promise of four and a half million pounds of leafy greens a year. Marketing pitched it as the most technologically advanced farm in the world — algorithms optimizing light, nutrition, and climate at the level of an individual plant.

Nineteen months later, in December 2024, the facility was shut down. In March 2025, the company filed for Chapter 11. Total losses were about nine hundred and forty million dollars of raised capital; the company's valuation collapsed from one point nine billion dollars in January 2022 to less than fifteen million by early 2025 — a collapse of roughly ninety-nine percent. A whole direction of major venture-capital investment activity was shut down, one that SoftBank, Walmart, and Jeff Bezos through Bezos Expeditions had all been putting money into.

Here it's worth pausing and asking the question we'll be answering for the whole lecture: what exactly did AI do in this failure — and in what sense was it unable to do it? This is not a "AI is bad" story. The Plenty greenhouse's microclimate controller worked. Computer vision recognized plant growth stages. Yield-forecasting models were trained and updated. What did not work was the arithmetic of energy: LED lighting on a closed floor consumes roughly one hundred times more energy than the same plant gets from free sunlight in an open field. AI, however well it optimizes the light-and-heat regime, cannot close a hundredfold gap in the cost of the denominator of the business model. We'll return to this answer at the end of the lecture — but for now remember the contrast: an opening with the mayor in May 2023 and a shuttered site in December 2024. And the question: where in this story was AI useful, and where could it not help for structural reasons?
