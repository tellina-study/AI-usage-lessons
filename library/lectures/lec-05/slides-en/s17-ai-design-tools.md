## Speaker notes

A realistic team workflow for 2025-26: a product manager or designer describes a screen in a prompt to v0 by Vercel or Figma Make, and the generator pulls in the team's already-existing components rather than generic templates — seventy-two percent of designers use generative AI, ninety-one percent report an improvement specifically in quality. The generator produces two to four diverging directions in minutes — what used to take a day of manual wireframing. The human designer converges — picks a direction, discards the rest, fixes the details the model got wrong. The chosen direction goes through a real usability test with real users.

Other named tools: Google Stitch — a text-to-mockup generator; Uizard — a lighter-weight tier; bolt.new — a prompt turned into a full browser app, the canonical vibe-coding tool.

What's genuinely good here, without the hype: speed of the first draft, generation aware of the design system — less rework than naive text-to-UI, and democratization of a decent first pass — non-designers can now produce something presentable to start a conversation. The working practice, the clear formulation for the whole section: AI for divergence, human for convergence. Co-creative tools are appropriate for maximizing the volume and variety of early exploration. Convergence — deciding which problem is worth solving, and refining one direction to fully match the context — is explicitly named as the human strength no prompt replaces. A design system is the natural guardrail that keeps AI divergence from drifting off-brand.
