## Visible slide text

# Execution stopped being the scarce resource.

# What's scarce now — and in what units is it paid for?

## Speaker notes

This question is the axis of the whole evening, and it is phrased so that it cannot be answered
in one word. Its two halves work differently, and both are required.

The first half is a claim, not a question, and it has already been proven by the preceding
frames. Execution stopped being the scarce resource: getting an image, a minute of speech, or a
series episode came to cost anywhere from three cents to a few thousand dollars, where the
classic mode demanded anywhere from fifty dollars to a hundred thirty-seven thousand. This is
measured on both sides and in one unit. In the sixty years before this, the tool got cheaper
seven times, and each time the intermediary operation disappeared; this time something else
disappeared — the price of a failed trial. It follows directly that the bottleneck of production
has shifted. The question is where, exactly.

The second half is the actual question, and both of its words matter. "What's scarce now" is a
question about the subject. "In what units is it paid for" is a question about the ledger, and it
is not there for decoration. The answer "what's scarce now is creative vision" would be empty for
exactly the reason that creative vision is not measured in anything: it cannot be counted, it
cannot be put into a budget, and it cannot be compared against what it used to be. The course
treats such phrasings as empty for as long as they carry no measured unit beneath them, and the
requirement to name a unit is a defense against the good-sounding answer that says nothing.

So every breakdown of the evening has to name its own unit of account, and not one of them is
allowed to get away with general words. Sometimes it will be a hit rate — two-thirds of released
ad banners fail to deliver the needed response metric. Sometimes it will be the number of
generations per one usable frame: thirty to a hundred at the Russian editorial case, hundreds of
shots against a few dozen that made it in at the American one. Sometimes it will be minutes of
a person's viewing time: about half an hour per one usable illustration. Sometimes it will be the
status of a legal event out of five distinguishable ones. Sometimes it will be a catalog's share
against the share of money paid for that catalog, and the gap between the two running an order of
magnitude.

It's only worth stating in advance the form of the answer, not the answer itself. The answer will
turn out to be three addresses where the payment now goes, and each will be named by its own name
by the end of the evening: selection, rights, discernment. Selection — because trying became free
while choosing did not, and procedures for selection simply did not exist before: trials were few,
because a trial cost money. Rights — because what the model was trained on determines what can
even be released from its output, and three major jurisdictions answer this differently. 
Discernment — because a signature, a voice, a face, and provenance stopped confirming themselves
on their own.

Every breakdown of the evening will name its own address out loud. In the still-image section,
three detailed breakdowns will name selection, and the legal breakdown and the mentions will name
rights; discernment will not be heard once in this section, and that is a property of the
modality itself: a still picture carries no signature that confirms itself. It will appear where
such a signature exists — with voice — and will carry the whole conversation about recognition.

One more thing is worth knowing about this question, and it concerns how the evening is built.
It is said exactly twice: here, and at the very end, where it is answered by name. Between these
two points it is not repeated and not paraphrased — not in a section title, not in a conclusion,
not under one's breath. This is deliberate: a question repeated six times stops being a question
and becomes a catchphrase, and the room stops hearing it by the third time. Instead of repeating
the question, every breakdown is required to name its own unit — and it is exactly that chain of
named units that is the real path from the question to the answer.
