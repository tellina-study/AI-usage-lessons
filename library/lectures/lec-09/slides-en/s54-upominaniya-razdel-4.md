## Speaker notes

Two short mentions don't add a third detailed breakdown to this section — at the time the lecture was prepared, open sources turned up exactly two cases of data-center-class infrastructure in space meeting the three selection conditions — instead, they bring the scale of plans stated for the future back to the numbers already named at the start of the lecture.

Starcloud's FCC filing for 88,000 satellites — the company's plan, not a fact of today; already named in the Starcloud breakdown, repeated here as a general characteristic of the scale of plans stated for the future across this whole stage.

A reminder from the industry overview: the roughly 12x growth in the number of satellites over ten years, and the multi-fold growth in the volume of data over the same years, is the direct source of the computing demand that Section 4 describes: the more satellites there are, and the more data they produce, the more computing is needed to process it, and part of that demand is now physically moving out to orbit itself. This is the loop that closes the industry overview the lecture opened with: the growth in the number of satellites and the growth in the volume of data were named as the reason the question of autonomy in space became urgent right now — Sections 1–3 showed how that reason shows up in robot control, teleoperation, and design. This section shows the same reason from the other side: demand for computing is growing faster than ground-based power generation, and part of that demand is starting to physically move out to the very place the data itself is born.
