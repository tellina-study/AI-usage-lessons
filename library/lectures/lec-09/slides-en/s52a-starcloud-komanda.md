## Visible slide text

**Real people stand behind the satellite, not just an engineering render.** This exact team took the demonstrator from idea to a working H100 in orbit.

**NVIDIA's Inception program isn't a marketing detail.** As a participant in this startup program, Starcloud gets access to NVIDIA's hardware and expertise.

## Speaker notes

The photo shows the Starcloud company team, featured in an NVIDIA blog piece about its partnership with Inception-program startups. A photo of people, not an engineering render or a visualization of a future satellite constellation — it's worth showing it alongside the breakdown for exactly that reason: behind every number ("the first data-center-class H100 in space," "an 88,000-satellite filing with the FCC") stands a specific team of engineers, not an abstract corporation.

The adjacent breakdown has already named the gist of the case: Starcloud-1, 60 kilograms, launched November 2, 2025, carries a data-center-class Nvidia H100 GPU and is already serving real requests to the open Gemma model. NVIDIA, the maker of the chip itself, has a direct stake in the demonstrator's success — hence the piece on its own blog, which names the team and describes its work.

This is one of the rare moments in the lecture where you can clearly see, behind the technical breakdown, not a single chip in a vacuum but the result of a whole engineering team's work — solving problems of radiation hardening, heat rejection without convection, and servicing hardware with no human access — problems that simply don't exist when designing a ground-based data center.
