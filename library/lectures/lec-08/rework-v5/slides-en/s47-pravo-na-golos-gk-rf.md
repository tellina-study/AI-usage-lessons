## Speaker notes

The possibilities in this section are the least disputable in the lecture. Their price is exactly one thing, and it is legal: the technical object "a voice model" exists, while the legal object "a voice" does not exist in Russia.

The Russian Civil Code has no separate protection for voice. A person whose voice has been copied has, as of October 2026, three tools, and each misses the target. The first is personal-data protection, but this requires proving that the recording is their biometric data, which is a separate dispute with its own outcome. The second is neighboring rights over a specific phonogram — that is, over the recording that was actually made; these do not extend to the voice as such, and a copy built by a model is not the original phonogram. The third is the article on protecting honor and dignity, but it works only if the use is defamatory, and most synthesis cases defame no one. There is no direct norm on voice.

The attempt to introduce one is documented in full, and the statuses in it have to be kept strictly apart. Bill No. 718834-8 was submitted in September 2024 — a month after the lost case the next frame examines. It proposed a new Article 152.3 of the Russian Civil Code: protection of voice as a personal non-property right, by analogy with the existing protection of a person's image, covering voice imitation and real-time speech synthesis. The government did not support the draft: the negative position was reported in October 2024, and the decision of the government's legislative-drafting commission was issued on January 20, 2025. The arguments given: voice recordings are biometric personal data, and consent for processing them is already required; moral damages are recoverable under existing law; a voice recording is a phonogram, already protected by neighboring rights. As of October 2026, the draft has not been adopted. Saying "a law on voice is being prepared in Russia" would be wrong: submitted and not supported is a different status, and it means there is no movement.

The industry's position here is not anti-technology, and misrepresenting it would be dishonest. The Russian Voice-Over Union states its goal on its own website, word for word: they are fighting for the introduction of regulation on the use of voice synthesis, so that this technology becomes profitable for voice-over artists themselves, rather than the sunset of the profession. They are asking for a license, not a ban. This matters for an engineering audience: the side usually assumed to be the victim here is pushing for a mechanism that lets the technology be used legally and for pay.

For contrast — another jurisdiction where norms have been adopted. In Tennessee, the law adding voice to the right of publicity was signed on March 21, 2024, and has been in force since July 1, 2024. In California, the law on digital replicas of the deceased has been in force since January 1, 2025. Liability there is defined as the greater of two amounts: ten thousand dollars or actual damages incurred — meaning ten thousand dollars is a floor on the award, not a ceiling and not an either-or choice left to the violator. A mandatory caveat, without which the picture would look rosier than it is: no litigated cases under either law could be found. A norm in force and a norm tested in practice are different states, and the second does not exist here yet.

There is no federal law in the US. The relevant bill was unanimously approved by the Senate Judiciary Committee on June 18, 2026, but on September 30, 2026, when an attempt was made to pass it by unanimous consent, one senator objected, and it has not been passed by either chamber. This is a third status in one frame, and it must not be confused with the previous two: committee approval is not passage.

Five statuses the course requires you to tell apart show up in this section almost in full: a suit has been filed, a ruling has been issued, a law has been passed and is in force, a draft has been submitted and not supported, a draft has been approved by committee and not passed. An engineer needs this because whether you can plan work around a norm depends on its status. You can rely on a norm that is in force; you cannot rely on a submitted draft, and the timeline for its passage is unpredictable.

The practical conclusion that follows directly: as long as there is no direct norm, the one tool that actually works is the contract. Everything you want the right to do with a recorded voice has to be named in a document, in words. What happens when the word is missing is shown by the next frame.
