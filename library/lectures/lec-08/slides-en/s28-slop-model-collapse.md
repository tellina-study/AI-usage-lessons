## Speaker notes

The eighth case — slop and model collapse. This is not a landmark lawsuit; it is an illustration of a structural problem of data quality for AI training. Slop is a colloquial term that entered use thanks to Bender, Marcus, and other AI researchers in 2024. The academic synonym — low-quality synthetic content. Concrete documented examples. May 2024, Google AI Overviews — a feature added to Google Search to generate summary answers to queries. To the question "how to keep cheese from sliding off pizza," AI Overview answered "add ⅛ cup of non-toxic glue to the sauce." This was a joke from Reddit eleven years ago, and the model reproduced it as factual advice. In parallel — to the question "how many rocks should I eat per day," AI Overview answered "at least one small rock per day." The source — The Onion satire. These cases quickly went viral, and Google removed most AI Overview features within a few weeks. What this illustrates. The Google Gemini model on which AI Overview ran was trained on a huge corpus of web data that — without curation — included both Reddit jokes and The Onion satire alongside real information. Without the contextual awareness that distinguishes serious sources from jokes and satire, the model reproduced content as fact. In parallel — an academic publication. Shumailov and colleagues, Nature 2024, volume 631, pages 755-759. This work documents the phenomenon of model collapse — recursively training a model on synthetic data generated by a previous model leads to progressive degradation and a narrowing of output diversity. The authors call this phenomenon MAD — Model Autophagy Disorder. This means that for next-generation frontier models, curation of training data is important — you cannot simply scrape the web and train on it, because the web increasingly contains AI-generated content. Lesson for the engineer: source quality matters more than volume. A model trained on Reddit jokes without a filter loses to a model on a curated dataset — even if the curated one is ten times smaller. This explains why Adobe Firefly on Adobe Stock plus licensed data works better than purely web-scraped-data models on some categories. It also explains why data curation by vendors costs real money and why the "free goods" of web-scraped data is a false economy.
