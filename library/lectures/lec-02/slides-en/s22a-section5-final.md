## Speaker notes

We've gone through all four inference stages: tokenization, embeddings, attention, sampling. The black box from Lecture 1, which we then called the "inference model", is no longer black. Next comes the final section of the lecture, in which we assemble the picture and close the loop.

There are six slides in this section. First — a short recap: the same four stages are now laid out into a horizontal pipeline; one definition line under each stage. Then — the main practical takeaway of the lecture: explicit answers to the three "whys" from Lecture 1. Why a prompt with a role works better than an empty one. Why AI is bad at counting letters. Why the same request gives different answers. On each "why" we place an explicit reference to the mechanism we've just covered.

Then — three cross-cutting frames. The first — a "when not an LLM" decision tree: situations where classical ML, transparent methods, or a specialized small model work better than a large language model. The second — the conceptual difference between attention as a correlational mechanism and the human model of causality; a callback to Pearl's three-level ladder from Lecture 1. After that — the assignment for Seminar 2 and a brief teaser for Lecture 3 about agents, RAG, and MCP. And in the finale — Q&A with the room.
