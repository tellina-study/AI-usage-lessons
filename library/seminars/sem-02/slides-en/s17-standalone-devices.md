# A standalone device against a phone feature

## Assertion

$241M raised → assets sold for $116M → servers shut off within a year

## Visual

Humane AI Pin card: a screenless wearable badge, a laser projector that
displays an image onto your palm, voice control, price $699 plus a $24/month
subscription, sales started April 2024. A chronology next to it: ~$241M
raised → assets sold to HP for $116M (February 2025) → servers shut off
28.02.2025. A second, smaller card — Rabbit R1: ~100,000 units sold, an
estimated ~5% of buyers actively use it. + a photo of the device (designer to
check usage rights).

## Speaker notes

Another example of the same choice, but at a different scale — not a feature
inside an app, but an entire standalone physical device. The Humane AI Pin — a
screenless wearable badge with a laser projector that displays an image right
onto your palm, voice control, priced at 699 dollars plus a 24-dollar-a-month
subscription. Sales started in April 2024. The company raised around 241
million dollars in investment, and in February 2025 the company's assets — not
the whole company, specifically the assets — were sold to HP for 116 million
dollars. On February 28, 2025, the device's servers were shut off, and it
stopped working for everyone who had bought one. The Rabbit R1 is a similar
story: around 100,000 units sold, but by some estimates only around 5% of
buyers actively use it. The same organizational lesson as with Kite: a
standalone device or product competes not only with alternative technologies,
but with the user's habit of not switching to a new entry point when the
function they need already lives on the phone that's already in their pocket.
