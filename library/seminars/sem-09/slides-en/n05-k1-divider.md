# Case 1. Answer or Stay Silent

## Assertion

The training center's knowledge base doesn't cover everything people ask — but the process can already write an answer on its own.

## Visual

> A question arrives in the form: "Is there a free demo class before buying the course?" Olga's knowledge base has not a single item about demo classes — and the process is already prepared to answer on its own.

## Speaker notes

Last session the process learned to parse a request and tell Olga about it. Today it takes the next step: it writes the answer straight to the requester. And the very first question that comes into the form turns out to be about something the training center's knowledge base doesn't have at all.

Olga does have a knowledge base — ten items. That covers most of what people ask day to day: questions at a training center repeat, and they repeat within a narrow circle. But not everything. Demo classes aren't on that list, and nobody promised that the next question would necessarily land in one of the ten items — the knowledge base was written from what people had already asked; it says nothing about tomorrow's question.

As long as Olga was the one answering, an uncovered question was exactly not a problem: she simply saw that her knowledge base had nothing on it, and sorted it out herself — asked her manager, checked the contract, checked with the academic office. That step lived in her head and cost her nothing.

The moment the process starts answering, that step has to come from somewhere. The process has no built-in way to tell a question the knowledge base closes from one that only looks closed. And the answer it writes goes straight to whoever asked, bypassing Olga's own check.

That's the whole case: we need a decision that, on every incoming question, answers exactly one thing — does the knowledge base close this question or not. First we'll look at what stands behind that decision in Olga's own working day, then we'll build its first, most natural version, run it on the real rig, and see what it showed. Then we'll build a second version — and run it on the same set of questions.
