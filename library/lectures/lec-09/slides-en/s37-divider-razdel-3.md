# 3. Design and new capabilities

**Not autonomy of action — thinking at the drafting table, which did not exist before at all.**

In order:

**Surrogate models** — in Raptor engine design
**Claude/Anthropic** — rover route planning
**AIFS and AIGEFS** — AI weather forecast models
**Generative design** — NASA Goddard structures

## Speaker notes

This section closes the second half of the lecture by type of AI contribution — from action (Section 1) and remote control (Section 2) to thinking at the drafting table, which previously required only a human or a supercomputer.

Four breakdowns in order, of varying scale of impact: the Raptor surrogate model solves a task inside one engineering department of one company. Rover route planning by the Claude model is the task of one operational team (JPL), but the result is publicly checkable by the final distance traveled. AIFS and AIGEFS weather forecasting is a task whose result touches not one company, but entire weather services and, through them, millions of people. NASA Goddard's generative design of structures is a task already applied today to several real mission concepts of the agency, including a future flagship telescope.

In all four, the model does not replace the process — it changes how many options can be considered in the same amount of time. At the same time, not one of the four models does the entire job on its own, start to finish: the final check in every case is a separate, non-AI step. This is the third of the lecture's main honest boundaries, and it runs through the whole section the same way: the model expands the search space, it does not replace the check.
