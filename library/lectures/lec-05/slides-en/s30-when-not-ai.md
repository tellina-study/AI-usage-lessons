## Speaker notes

Let us assemble from five sections a through-line criterion «when AI is not needed or dangerous» — this is the answer to the question of applicability and risks. This is not five different warnings but one principle in five manifestations: the right choice of AI type is necessary but not sufficient; AI is dangerous where its output is wired to an action whose cost of error is not paid for by a harness.

The first manifestation — an irreversible automatic action without a gate, as at Zillow and Knight: a forecast or a signal that triggers an irreversible capital or trading action automatically in a nonstationary environment without a circuit breaker. The measure — a human gate, a narrow segment, live monitoring of the error. The second — a regulated decision without explainability, as at Apple Card: credit or denial without reason codes, without an appeal path, without a bias audit. The measure — an explainable model, a human on escalation, an audit of outcomes. The third — a financial fact without a grounding on a source, as at Air Canada: a fact binding on the client, generated freely. The measure — grounding, verifiability, a path to a human. The fourth — full replacement of people in service, as at Klarna: removing the human channel on the emotional and disputed tail for the sake of average efficiency. The measure — augmentation with guaranteed escalation. The fifth — price discrimination without a legal frame, as at Wendy's: an optimizer into whose goal the fairness constraint was mixed. The measure — price is decided by a human within a legal frame, and the optimizer works only inside the bounds.

Notice: in none of the five cases is the fix a better model. In all of them it is better judgment about what stands around the AI at the cost of the error. This is exactly the substantive answer to the central question: where it breaks is not in the AI type, which was chosen correctly, but in the absence of a harness proportional to the irreversibility and the regulated nature of the action.
