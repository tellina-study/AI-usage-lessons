## Speaker notes

This lecture stands on the foundation of the overview Module 1 and the first industry Lecture 4, so first let's fix what carries over ready-made and is not re-explained. From Lecture 2 we take the "which AI" tree: the distinction between classical machine learning, deep learning, and large language models, and the fact that a language model generates the next token of text. This distinction will underpin the entire "why not an LLM" argument in the sections on forecasting and scoring. From Lecture 4 we take the frame "the task determines the type of AI" and the principle that responsibility is not delegated. The contrast matters: there it was one type of AI in depth, here it is a palette of types for a palette of tasks.

Five types that we will go through: time-series forecasting, anomaly detection, classical tabular scoring, language models in support, recommender systems. As a sixth, through-going layer, we will cover computer vision in retail and in customer verification. Most of the value in finance and retail comes from something other than a language model, and applying an LLM everywhere would be an engineering mistake — this is the lecture's thesis in one phrase.

And an important methodological caveat. Four concepts this lecture introduces from scratch, because in the order of the course you see them here for the first time: the concepts of the false positive and false negative error and the confusion matrix, the mechanism of proxy bias, distribution shift. Lecture 7 will formally deepen this apparatus on medical material, but it comes after ours, so here this is a first, self-contained, but compact introduction — without the medical depth.
