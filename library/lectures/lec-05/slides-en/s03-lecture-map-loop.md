# Visible content

## Title bar
The lecture is a loop, not a list of six topics

## Body
[schema_cycle: 6 segments on a circle, arrows around it, entry point "Intent"]

1. **Discovery** — where the hypothesis comes from
2. **Design** — hypothesis → artifact
3. **Build / Launch** — artifact → product in production
4. **Measure / Experiment** — did it actually work
5. **Support / Operate** — the product lives 24/7
6. **Governance** — where to put capital next

[Return arrow: Governance → back to Discovery]

## Speaker notes

Here's the map of today's lecture, and it's drawn not as a list but as a circle, because that's essential. Six sections aren't six independent topics — they're six arrows of one loop. We start with intent — with Discovery, where a hypothesis about what to build at all is born. The hypothesis passes through Design, turning into a concrete artifact you can show a person. The artifact passes through Build and Launch, becoming a working product in production. Next — Measure: we check whether the change actually produced the effect we predicted. Support and Operate is what happens once a product is already living with real users around the clock. And finally Governance — the organizational level at which it's decided whether to keep investing, scale, or stop.

The key point that distinguishes this map from a simple list: the last arrow loops back into the first. The signal from operation and from governance decisions changes what we research next in Discovery. This is a closed cycle, not a one-time linear process — and that's exactly why at the end of the lecture we'll come back to the same circle, only with a full understanding of what happens on every arrow.
