# What to take away

## Assertion

Seven lessons, one per case — nothing new, just a compilation

## Visual

right:
"Vendor documents — study the data, don't take anyone's word for it."
"Personal data in logs — algorithms and small models are often faster and cheaper than big ones."
"Support assistant — an organizational constraint can settle the architecture just as well as technical ones."

## Speaker notes

Let's gather up what's already come up today — nothing new, just a
compilation. Vendor documents — study the data as thoroughly and broadly
as you can, don't take the client's word for it, two examples still
aren't the full picture. Personal data in logs — algorithms and small
specialized models often solve a text task faster and cheaper than a big
LLM. Support assistant — an organizational constraint like a closed API
can settle the architecture just as well as technical requirements.
Product descriptions — a button in an existing interface almost always
beats a standalone product. Meeting minutes — concrete new requirements
drive the architecture, not fashion for a technology, start simple. The
digest — lots of data sources doesn't mean you need RAG. And sales
calls — what looks hard to a human isn't necessarily hard for a model,
and data sensitivity is only uncovered by asking, not by assuming. That's
what's worth taking away from today's session.
