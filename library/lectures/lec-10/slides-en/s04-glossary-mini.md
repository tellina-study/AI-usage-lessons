# Glossary — the shared vocabulary of the whole lecture

## Assertion

Two working formulations and five supply-chain terms — the shared vocabulary of the whole lecture.

## Visual

A two-column layout. Each column is an Ocean rounded box. At the top of each block — a 32px Primary mid icon + the term name 18pt bold. Below it — a short definition 14pt regular.

**Left column — two working environment formulations:**

1. **Open-environment AI.** AI outside a controlled cycle: a real field, real weather, real biology. The environment changes uncontrollably, feedback comes over seasons. Course examples: L10 field, L9 drone in unknown terrain.
2. **Closed-loop AI.** AI inside a feedback-controlled cycle: a greenhouse, a factory, an operating room. The environment is controlled, feedback comes within minutes to hours. Course examples: medicine L7, factory L11, Cargill hedge L4.

**Right column — 5 L4 supply-chain terms:**

1. **Agentic AI.** An AI agent with an inference loop and use of external tools; performs multi-step tasks autonomously (plan→act→check→iterate).
2. **Basis points (bp).** Hundredths of a percent; 100 bp = 1%.
3. **Hedge slippage.** The gap between the expected price of a hedge and the price actually executed.
4. **Scope-3.** Scope-3 emissions — across the whole supply chain (suppliers + customers).
5. **AI-MRV.** AI systems for measurement, reporting, and verification of carbon credits / emissions.

## Speaker notes

Before diving into the material, let's fix two sets of definitions that will be used throughout the lecture.

The left column — two environment formulations that explain why the same class of AI models works in one environment and breaks in another. Open-environment — a real field, real weather, real biology; the environment changes uncontrollably, feedback comes over seasons. Closed-loop — a greenhouse, a factory, an operating room; the environment is controlled, feedback comes within minutes or hours. This is a working formulation of the course; it appeared in Lecture 7 on medicine and carries over into Lecture 11 on manufacturing as a load-bearing bridge. Most of the failures in this lecture are either open-environment cases, in which models validated in the lab break, or attempts to close the environment, after which it turns out that in a closed loop the blast radius of any malfunction is multiplied.

The right column — five supply-chain-level terms that appear in the course for the first time on the industry specifics of L4. Agentic AI is not a one-step answer; it's an agent with an inference loop, use of external tools, and multi-step autonomous tasks. Basis points — a unit of measure for small changes in finance: one hundred basis points is one percent. Hedge slippage — the gap between the expected price of a hedge and the price actually executed; for a large hedge, a difference of tens of basis points turns into tens of thousands of dollars. Scope-3 — emissions across the whole supply chain, not from the company's own operations; this is part of the EU CSRD mandatory reporting since 2024. AI-MRV — AI systems for measurement, reporting, and verification of carbon credits — that is, inference with large uncertainty that is often marketed as precise measurement.

Remember these definitions; we'll return to each of them explicitly in the corresponding sections.
