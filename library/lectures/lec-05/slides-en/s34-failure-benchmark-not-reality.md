# Visible content

## Title bar
Passing a format-A benchmark ≠ safe in a format-B production setting

## Body
[2 mini-cases: medicine 86.5% MedQA / law Lexis+17% Westlaw 33%]

**Medical**: Med-PaLM 2 — 86.5% MedQA, but a **separate adversarial safety set** is needed

**Legal**: *Mata v. Avianca* = **ChatGPT** (not Harvey) · Lexis+ 17% hallucinations · Westlaw 33%

[Gold callout]
A benchmark is evidence of performance only on that specific benchmark's task format

## Speaker notes

Google's Med-PaLM 2 scored eighty-six point five percent on MedQA — multiple-choice questions in the style of a medical licensing exam. A mandatory correction: the widely repeated claim that Med-PaLM recommended chemotherapy for a headache does not trace back to any primary source. The verifiable version is stronger: Google's researchers had to build a separate, harder adversarial set precisely because the exam-style benchmark doesn't surface open-ended clinical-safety failures at all.

In Mata v. Avianca, a lawyer filed a brief citing six entirely fabricated court cases generated by ChatGPT — not Harvey AI, a frequent attribution error. A rigorous, peer-reviewed study found: raw GPT-4 without retrieval erred eighty-eight percent of the time; the most accurate tool tested, Lexis+ AI, still erred seventeen percent of the time; Westlaw erred roughly thirty-three percent of the time, despite marketing itself as "hallucination-free."

The mechanism in both cases, tied to the Measure phase: a benchmark measured on a narrow task format doesn't transfer to the real user task — Goodhart's law again: the benchmark became a marketing target. This case carries three widely "well-known" failures that, once checked against the primary source, turn out to be distorted — and unpacking the distortions is more valuable than the cases themselves, because the lecture teaches fact-checking using its own material. The lesson: a named benchmark score is evidence only on that specific benchmark's format. The criterion: if a safety claim rests solely on a benchmark with a structurally different format, it is unsupported. The alternative: mandatory adversarial sets built specifically for the deployed task, scored by an independent methodology.
