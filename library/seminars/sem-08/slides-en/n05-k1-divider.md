# Case 1. Notifications

## Assertion

A request touches more than one topic — and the coordinator needs to know right away, without opening the table.

## Visual

> "Send me one line in Telegram: who wrote and about what — so I don't have to check the table for every request."

## Speaker notes

Olga is the administrator of the training center, and she has one specific, concrete request for the chain we built in the previous session. When a request touches on more than one topic at once, she wants to receive a message about it in Telegram, rather than having to go and check the results screen every single time a requester happened to mention something beyond the main question.

The request sounds simple enough, and granting it looks easy at first — the chain already knows everything it needs to know: the name, the main topic, the extra topics. All three values have already been extracted by this point and are sitting right there in the table. All that's left, it seems, is to assemble them into one readable message and send it off.

This is exactly where the case begins: how, precisely, to assemble that text out of three already-known values — that is today's question, and it turns out to be less obvious than it looks at first glance. Next comes a live scene from Olga's working day, showing what the first attempt actually did, and a little later, once we look at real runs, what that same attempt actually produced in practice.

Notice carefully what Olga did NOT ask for. She did not ask anyone to "make the notification smart" or to "let the model decide for itself what to write." She described only the result she wants to see on her own phone — one single line that immediately makes clear who wrote in and about what. How exactly the chain arrives at that line is not her concern at all, and that is worth keeping firmly in mind: the whole case from here on is about how different teams of engineers, standing at this very same spot, end up choosing different answers, even though the underlying need is one and the same for everyone, and every bit as concrete.

That need is exactly what we work with for the whole of this case — not some abstract "we need a notification," but this exact phrase, word for word, against which any proposed solution can be checked from here on. Remember its shape well: that is precisely how we will check every option that follows — not by eye, but by feeding it a real request and watching closely whether it produces exactly this line.
