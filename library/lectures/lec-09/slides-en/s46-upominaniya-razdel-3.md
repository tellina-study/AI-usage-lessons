## Speaker notes

The section's six mentions hold three different roles. The first two show that forecast models, trained on the same general principle as AIFS and AIGEFS, have already moved beyond a single research organization and work as a commercial product in various niches. The third — planetary defense — is a heavier mention with its own numbers: the same task that holds up the whole section, but on asteroid material rather than weather. The last three are short; they name adjacent directions this lecture deliberately does not break down in detail — not because they're unimportant, but because each one's engineering depth belongs to a different lecture in the course.

NVIDIA FourCastNet3 — the commercial use of weather-forecast models by the companies Eni, Southwest Power Pool, and Hitachi for wind-energy forecasting. Satellite + AI in agricultural insurance — operationally in use at commercial platforms in 2025–2026, speeding up crop-loss assessment from satellite data by up to 50%.

Planetary defense — NASA's Sentry-II and ATLAS, already-operating systems, not future plans. Sentry-II continuously scans the catalog of known asteroids and assesses impact probability on a horizon of 100 years ahead. ATLAS is a global network of inexpensive autonomous telescopes, where machine learning reduces the false-positive rate and speeds up the dispatch of warnings — the same type of task that holds up the LeoLabs breakdown: prioritizing and filtering signal out of noise. Honest calendar caveat: NEO Surveyor is delayed, launch no earlier than September 2027 — not to be confused with the already-operating Sentry-II and ATLAS.

Satellite imagery analysis of Earth, ML exoplanet search, and onboard anomaly diagnostics — three short mentions; the engineering depth of each belongs to the topic of a different lecture in the course.
