# He opened a session in his own repository and has not asked anything yet

## Assertion

The session is open, and he has not yet asked the agent a single question. Part of the context is already taken: four servers from the project file described themselves at start-up, and over the whole task one of them will be needed.

## Visual

## Speaker notes

The section's second case begins at a moment when nothing has happened yet. The session is open, not a single question has been asked of the agent — and part of the context is already taken.

It is not taken up by the conversation: there is no conversation yet. Nor by the project's files, although they are lying there too. Four connected servers described themselves at start-up — that is how the connection we assembled in the section's first case is built. The client, on starting up, asks every server what it can do, and puts what it receives into the context, before a person's first question and independently of it.

Over the whole task this person will reach for one server out of the four. The cost has been paid for all four.

The turn of the case is worth naming right away, because it argues directly with a widespread intuition. The intuition says: "I pay for what I use; a server the agent never reached for costs me nothing." The check says the opposite: the cost is paid by being connected. A server that was not reached for even once in a session costs the same as a working one. And it is paid anew in every session — every morning, at every opening.

The difference from the section's first case runs along what is being settled. The first settled what to connect, with what permissions, and what to prove with that the agent really does go through what is connected. This one is about the count: what the server's mere presence in the project costs while nobody is reaching for it, and what that cost is reduced with, without disconnecting what is needed.

From the slide about the standing cost in the first case, this case differs in scale and in composition. There the cost is named and measured on one server — as the price of the decision to connect. Here there are several servers, half of them were added to the project by another person, and the question stands differently: what to do about it when you cannot disconnect them and when the choice was not yours.

The objection "but the context is large" turns up here almost always and is dealt with concretely later in the case: how much exactly the definitions take up in the measured case, and at what moment a large context stops being enough.
