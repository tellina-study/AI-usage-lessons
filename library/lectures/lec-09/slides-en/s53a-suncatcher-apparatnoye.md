## Speaker notes

The photo shows the assembled Suncatcher satellite in the assembly facility before launch: deployed solar panels, a refrigerator-sized body, ground support equipment nearby. This is a photo of the real spacecraft, not a render of a future constellation and not an artist's illustration — the distinction is worth naming explicitly, because some of the publicly available material about the project is, in fact, renders.

Inside this body are four tensor processing units, Google's own design for machine learning. This exact spacecraft, launched on a Falcon 9 rocket on October 1, 2026, delivered the first recorded AI model response from a data-center-class TPU in orbit — the fact the whole case breakdown is built around.

The solar panels in the photo aren't a decorative element — they're the exact key constraint that gives the whole design its point: the continuity of sunlight in orbit, with no day-night cycle, which every ground-based power source is subject to. The company's plan is to grow the constellation of such spacecraft to 81 satellites, linked to each other by laser communication lines into a single cluster; what's visible in this photo is the first physical unit of that plan, not a mockup of it.
