# Two misses, opposite causes

## Assertion

The first was not inside at all. The second fit inside whole and crowded the work out.

## Visual

| WHEN AND WHAT | WHY IT CAME OUT THAT WAY |
|---|---|
| Monday. It answered by the repository — two of the client's requests were not in the answer. A missed deadline. | They are not inside at all. Let the context be twice the size — they still will not appear in it. |
| Wednesday, a working product. It read everything where the rule might be checked, assembled a correct list — and then started re-asking what had been explained at the start of the session. Half an hour. | Everything needed fit inside — and crowded out the very thing the session was opened for. |

> "The work has run past the agent's context — which do you set up first: bringing inside what is not inside, or carrying outside what does not fit inside?"

## Speaker notes


Both of this week's misses are on the screen side by side, and the job of this screen is to show their causes together.

Monday has already been described: the agent answered by the repository, and two of the client's requests were not in the answer. The cause is that they are not inside at all. Let the context be twice the size: those requests still will not appear in it, because they lie in another system.

Wednesday is a different project belonging to the same person: a product he works in every day, plans, members, two years of migrations. The task of the day is an ordinary one — set up a "Team" plan, twenty members instead of five. In the morning he spends some fifteen minutes explaining the task to the agent: what the plan is called, who can get it, what to do with old accounts, which cases to check. The edit is going to be made right here, in this session. But first a list is needed of the places where the limit on members is checked: miss one, and the new plan will pass the config and run into a check nobody remembers any more. The agent finds four such places, and to find them it opens more than forty files, some twenty minutes of reading. The list is correct. And when it comes to the edit itself, the agent re-asks what it was told that morning: what the plan is called and how many members are in it. Half an hour went on saying what had already been said and getting back to where they had stopped.

The causes are opposite. On Monday what was needed was not inside. On Wednesday everything needed did fit inside — and crowded out the very thing the session was opened for. "Does not fit" means two different states of affairs, and they are treated by moves that run toward each other: in one case you bring inside what is not there, in the other you carry outside what does not fit inside. The order of the whole class rests on that opposition.

Hence the question: the work has run past the agent's context — which do you set up first, bringing inside what is not inside, or carrying outside what does not fit inside? There are usually three answers to it, and all three are honest. "Depends on what is missing" is a correct first move: the question is precisely about how to tell one state from the other, and the criterion for that is assembled further on. "Data first — without it the agent simply will not solve the task": the grounds for that lie in the first section. "A separate worker first — it saves room": the grounds for that are in the second.

Choosing between the moves in a live project is not obligatory, both work. Each has its own cost: someone else's code and a permanent place in the context for the first, extra permissions and lost visibility of what was done for the second. The question is about which one you start with, when the resource and the attention are single.

And separately — about the guess that comes up on this screen more often than any other: in the second case a bigger context would have been enough. A large context pushes the boundary back and preserves the property that caused the miss: what has been read stays inside and shares the room with the work. Forty files fit whole — and that is exactly why the task left. The seminar will come back to that guess with a measurement.

The names of the two mechanisms are not needed here yet: they appear on the map next and are broken down each in its own section.
