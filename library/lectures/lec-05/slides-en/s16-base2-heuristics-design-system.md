## Speaker notes

Jakob Nielsen's usability heuristics are rules for expert review: not a substitute for testing with real users, but a fast, cheap way to catch problems before spending money on full-blown research. For an engineer, the metaphor "a linter for UX" is useful — static analysis before an early launch. Top five of the canonical ten: visibility of system status; matching the user's language rather than developer jargon; control and freedom — undo and a clear emergency exit; consistency and standards; error prevention — better to keep the user from making the mistake than to report it nicely after the fact.

A design system is a living, managed set of principles, tokens, components, and patterns that keeps a product consistent at scale. The key idea that will carry over into the AI section: a design system is a guardrail, a mechanism that keeps generative freedom inside an already-validated brand, which makes it the natural harness for AI interface generation.

Nielsen's own tough lesson, repeated in the industry like a mantra: "you are not the user" — anyone close to the project systematically mispredicts what will confuse a real person. A test with one user surfaces about thirty-one percent of usability problems on average; the classic five users find about eighty-five percent. This is the necessary counterweight for the next slide: however much AI speeds up prototype generation, putting the artifact in front of a real person and watching their real confusion has no AI substitute.
