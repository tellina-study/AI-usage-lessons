## Speaker notes

Since two thousand eighteen, Amazon positioned Just Walk Out as pure computer vision, without disclosing human involvement. An investigation found: about a thousand workers in India were reviewing video to label ambiguous transactions. The key number: in two thousand twenty-two, seven hundred of a thousand transactions required manual review against an internal target of only fifty of a thousand — the actual rate was roughly fourteen times the target. In April two thousand twenty-four, Amazon announced it was removing the technology from most Amazon Fresh stores in the US.

The mechanism: a real limitation of computer-vision accuracy across product variety, plus a governance failure — six years of marketing autonomous AI without disclosing the real scale of human labor. This is the "Wizard-of-Oz AI" pattern — hidden human labor disguised as autonomy. The bare fact "a thousand workers were involved" lets you be technically right — people annotated video, they weren't watching live. But the right counterfactual isn't whether people were involved at all, it's how far the actual intervention rate exceeded the target: a fourteen-fold overshoot.

Lesson: claims of autonomy must be verifiable and must include honest disclosure of the human-in-the-loop share. The hidden-human-cost criterion: if acceptable accuracy requires manual review at multiples of the target rate, the claimed autonomy has not been achieved. Now let's assemble all six phases into a single matrix: for each phase — what AI changes in cost and trust, what classic discipline stays, when AI-first breaks, and which failures illustrate it. The matrix isn't for memorizing — it's for applying: when you meet a new failure in the news, identify the phase, the broken classic discipline, and the criterion — that is exactly the trainable skill of recognition.
