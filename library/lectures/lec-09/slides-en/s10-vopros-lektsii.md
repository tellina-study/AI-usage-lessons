## Visible slide text

# Which decisions did space force us to invent —
and did they exist before it demanded them?

The answer — at the end of the lecture, named case by case for each of the four sections.

## Speaker notes

The material we'll gather over the next four sections answers one question, and it will be stated here for the second time — in the Wrap-up, right at the end, where the answer will be given named case by case for each of the four sections, not as one general phrase.

The question is this: which decisions did space force us to invent — and did they exist before it demanded them? This isn't a rhetorical question for mood — it will get a concrete, testable answer, and that answer will turn out to be neither a flat "space always invents" nor "space only applies what's ready", but different section by section: in two of the four sections we'll find a measured case of a solution born specifically by the frontier; in the other two, an honest transfer of a ready-made tool into a new configuration that was previously impossible. This 2:2 split isn't a predetermined target — it's what turned up as a consequence of honestly applying the case-selection rules to the material we gathered.

Keep this question in mind throughout the whole lecture: with every new case study it's worth asking — was this solution invented right here, under this specific pressure, or carried over ready-made from softer conditions? The answer to this question isn't just a nice phrasing — it's an engineering skill that carries over into any industry: the ability to tell a real innovation, forced by the conditions of the task, apart from applying an already-existing tool to a new place.
