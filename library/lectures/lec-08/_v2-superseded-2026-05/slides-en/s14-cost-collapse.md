# Cost collapse: 100×–10,000× by asset type

## Assertion

Cost collapse 100×–10,000× by asset type. But corporate commercially-safe is a separate $400M+ segment.

## Visual

On top, the assertion 24pt. Center — an Ocean rounded box with a horizontal bar chart (QuickChart): 4 asset types, 2 bars each (Before — long teal, After — short gold). 1 image: $50-200 → $0-0.25 (200×-10,000×). 50 product images: $1k-25k → $0-1.5 (>1,000×). 1 minute of 720p video: $1k-50k → $6 (150×-8,000×). Dub per minute per language: $50-500 → <$1 (50×-500×). To the right of the bar chart — a gold-tint callout: "BUT: Adobe Firefly $400M revenue 2024-25 — the middle segment (commercially safe) is a separate segment." ## Speaker notes

The main economic change that AI brought to the creative industry is a catastrophic collapse of cost-per-asset. This is not "AI is a little cheaper"; it is two-to-four orders of magnitude cheaper, depending on the asset type. Concrete numbers by the table. One illustration: before AI — fifty-to-two-hundred dollars from a freelance designer, fifty-to-five-hundred from a stock photo. With AI — zero to twenty-five cents. Multiplier — two hundred to ten thousand times cheaper. Fifty product lifestyle images: before AI — a thousand-to-twenty-five-thousand dollars for full photography. With AI — zero-to-one-and-a-half dollars. Multiplier — more than a thousand times. One minute of 720p video: before AI — a thousand-to-fifty-thousand dollars for the shoot and post-production. With AI on Sora 2 standard, sixty seconds at ten cents — about six dollars. Multiplier — a hundred fifty-to-eight-thousand times. A dub of a minute of video into one language: before AI — fifty-to-five-hundred dollars, voice actor plus studio. With AI on ElevenLabs — less than one dollar. Multiplier — fifty-to-five-hundred times. What this table does not mean. It does not mean that the creative industry will "lose a thousandfold of jobs." It means the following: the marginal cost of generating an artifact fell by two-to-four orders of magnitude, but the value-add from human direction, curation, brand alignment did not fall; and corporate commercially-safe generation still costs money. Adobe Firefly's four hundred million dollars in direct revenue in 2024-25 shows that a licensed corpus plus process plus integration is a full corporate SaaS stack, not "free goods." What this table does mean. Bottom-of-market — commodity creative — is a market disappearing as a separately professionally-priced category. AI generates it in minutes for zero-to-one-and-a-half dollars. Top-of-market — flagship campaign direction, original brand campaigns, complex multi-scene narrative — is a market where human direction remains mandatory. Adobe Firefly's four hundred million in revenue is empirical proof that the middle segment — commercially-safe industrial-quality generation — is a separate, fast-growing segment, not bottom-of-market.
