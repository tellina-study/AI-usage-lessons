# Voice cloning + dubbing into multiple languages

## Assertion

ElevenLabs: voice clone from 1 min of audio → 32+ languages. Dubbing $50-500/min → <$1/min. Production: Deutsche Telekom, Klarna.

## Speaker notes

By 2026, ElevenLabs became the de facto standard for voice cloning and AI dubbing. The capability: a voice clone from one minute of original audio, speaking in thirty-two languages and more, preserving the voice's characteristics — timbre, pace, emotional coloring. Dubbing Studio is a production-ready tool for localizing long-form video across twenty-nine languages. Before this product, a traditional dubbing studio required a voice actor to read in the target language, a sound engineer to mix, optionally a post-sync editor to lip-sync. The cost — fifty-to-five-hundred dollars per minute of dubbing into one language. ElevenLabs Dubbing Studio — less than one dollar per minute per language, minutes instead of weeks. Production in the corporate sector: Deutsche Telekom uses ElevenLabs for multilingual customer-service agents — one prerecorded voice sample of the CEO, cloned into thirty languages and more for regional communication. Klarna — for autonomous customer-service AI agents. The mini-failure block: the voice-cloning capability simultaneously opens a new class of legal risks. Scarlett Johansson v. OpenAI "Sky" in May 2024: OpenAI demonstrated a "Sky" voice in ChatGPT extremely similar to Johansson's voice. Johansson had earlier refused to voice ChatGPT when Sam Altman personally approached her. OpenAI removed the "Sky" voice within a week; formally, no lawsuit was filed. This is a de facto win for right of publicity: the voice-cloning capability obligates explicit consent even if technologically the voice "merely resembles." This case builds a bridge to the third section, where we will work through other voice and likeness cases from a separate angle.
