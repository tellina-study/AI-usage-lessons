## Speaker notes

The question the session opened with is standing in front of you in exactly the same words. The process wrote to a human — who can vouch that it's safe to send. Six case studies gave it one answer, and it's shorter than you might have expected at the start of the day.

You can vouch for what the process can show before it sends anything. A field that code fills in by its own rule. A value read from a trusted knowledge base. A second, independent measurement of the same number, checked against the first. All of that can be shown, recomputed, and discussed, and every one of them works the same way on any question that comes next. You cannot vouch for text the model wrote about itself — and that came out today on a success, not on a failure: in the easy case, the model honestly admitted it didn't know the answer, three times out of three. The trouble is that the exact same device, on a neighboring detail, answered confidently and wrongly six times out of fifteen. A self-report that worked looks no different, from the outside, than a self-report that's about to fail.

From this comes the practical conclusion, and it carries over to any orchestrator, including the one whose screens we looked at today. The decision that the send depends on gets pulled out of the answer's text and into its own field. Code reads that field. The value code computes with comes from a source the client never wrote. And wherever a number came in from a document or from the text of a request, a second, independent value of that same number gets placed next to it, along with a cross-check between the two.

Now, the file. It's the same workflow you left last session with, plus the refinement from today's first case study: the process answers on its own to a question from a known set, and for everything else it writes "please wait, a human will get back to you" — and that decision sits in a checkable field. And I'll say outright what's missing from the file, too. We saw the other five case studies today, built and measured, but they were built with separate teaching copies of the process, and bundling them into the delivered file isn't part of it. You can reproduce them at home; they don't come ready-made.

There's one ask for home, and it's a small one. Open your own version and ask it a question whose answer isn't in the knowledge base, but that has a neighboring item — about a deadline, an amount, a nearby condition. Watch two things: what the process answered the human, and what ended up in the checkable field at the same time. The gap between those two things is the whole of today's session, seen with your own hands.
