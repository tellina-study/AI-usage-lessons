# Kite and Copilot

## Assertion

One technology, different integration — and 17 months of head-to-head competition on the market

## Visual

A horizontal timeline with five points: "2014 — Kite founded" → "2019 — local
model execution" → "29.06.2021 — Copilot technical preview" → "21.06.2022 —
Copilot GA, $10/month" → "21.11.2022 — Kite shuts down." Between the third and
fifth points — a highlighted span labeled "~17 months of market overlap." User
counts sit next to the matching points: Kite ~500,000 users at peak; Copilot
~20M of GitHub's 100M+ developers, 4.7M paid subscribers, ~90% of the Fortune
100.

## Speaker notes

Look at the chronology. Kite — a standalone, independent product for
AI-powered code autocompletion — was founded in 2014, and from 2019 could run
the model locally on the developer's own machine. GitHub Copilot entered
technical preview on June 29, 2021, and became generally available on June 21,
2022, at 10 dollars a month. Kite shut down on November 21, 2022. Counting
from Copilot's technical preview to Kite's shutdown, that's around 17 months
where both products genuinely existed on the same market at the same time, and
the competition was real, not hypothetical. That said, the reason for the
shutdown should be stated carefully: Kite's own founder explained it not as a
win for one specific product, but as the fact that "developers don't pay for
standalone tools" — that's his statement about the market as a whole, not a
direct claim that "Copilot killed Kite." Kite had around 500,000 users at its
peak. Copilot has around 20 million users as of July 2025, out of more than
100 million developers on GitHub, 4.7 million paid subscribers as of January
2026, and it's used in roughly 90% of Fortune 100 companies. A comparable
technology, but built directly into the IDE and into the ecosystem developers
were already connected to.
