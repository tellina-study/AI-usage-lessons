# Where each action sits on the "decision in the model — decision in code" axis

## Assertion

Four actions line up on one axis — who ends up deciding, the model or code. The fifth stands apart.

## Visual

> The same axis the question's breakdown just named: on the left, the decision lives entirely inside the model; on the right, the decision is in code, the model is never called. Between them — two actions that split the work differently.

## Speaker notes

Before we break down each action one by one, look at all of them at once — on a single line. On the left, the option where the decision lives entirely inside the model: it gets the knowledge base and the question, decides on its own whether to answer or decline, and writes the text itself. On the right, the opposite end of the axis, where the model has no part in the decision at all: the question is compared against ready-made wordings by string match, and if it matches, a prepared answer responds.

Between these two ends stand two more actions, and both split the work between the model and code — but split it differently. One asks the model to name a knowledge-base item, and leaves the decision of whether to answer or hand off to a human to a simple condition in code. The other leaves the model a free text answer, but also asks it to name a number — how confident it is — and code decides by a threshold what goes to the client and what goes to Olga.

The fifth action doesn't sit on this line at all, and that's not an oversight. Searching for the needed item in an external store solves a different problem — the problem of a large knowledge base that doesn't fit into one call. It has its own axis: how many items are in the base. For a ten-item knowledge base that fits entirely in one call, this axis barely matters — which is exactly why the breakdown ahead sets this option aside separately, without a direct comparison to the other four.

Why look at placement before the "how it works — what it costs" table at all? Because position on this axis predicts exactly what the case is built around: where the "answer myself or hand off to a human" decision will end up once the action runs. The further left an action sits, the deeper the decision is buried inside the answer text — and the less chance Olga has to check it without reading the whole answer. The further right, the more formal the decision, but the less the action understands the meaning of the question. You'll see this link between axis position and what happens next in the breakdown on the next screen — now action by action, with cost and numbers.
