# Solution: Hash First, jev on the Expensive Step

## Assertion

The cheap mechanical step still goes first. The expensive step now runs the specialized service jev (20/20) instead of the cheap judge model (17/20) — the architecture is built and demonstrated next.

## Visual

> Figure: step 1 — the cheap mechanical pre-filter, takes on literal repeats for free. Step 2 — the meaning check, the measurement's choice is the specialized service (jev), more accurate and cheaper than the general-purpose model on this test. The node is built and live on the rig.

> **Honest caveat — right on the figure.** One measurement, on 20 pairs of one language and one topic (course enrollment). Generalizing beyond this set is untested — this is a boundary of the measurement, not a reason not to deploy the solution.

## Speaker notes

On the previous screen we measured five actions. From here — an architectural conclusion, and it isn't tied to any one specific tool: this formulation wouldn't change if the chain were built not in n8n but in anything else, and it doesn't name a single node.

The first step is a cheap, mechanical filter. It takes on everything that can be resolved without a model: all literal repeats, and resolves it for free and instantly. The classic vector approach doesn't go into this step — the measurement showed it isn't just a non-improvement over the simplest string comparison, it's measurably worse. That hasn't changed.

What changed is what runs on the second step. It used to be just the judge model — the only measured candidate for the expensive step. The measurement between it and a second candidate came out against the familiar choice: the specialized service jev — 20 out of 20, zero errors, and by public pricing even cheaper than the cheap judge model. By this test's results, the expensive step now runs it, not a general-purpose model — and that's no longer a thought for later, it's a built chain.

And right away, honestly, about the boundaries of this measurement: one test on 20 pairs of one language and one topic is a narrow basis for the conclusion "jev is always better than any judge model, everywhere". Nobody has checked generalizing beyond this specific set — a different language, a different topic, a different volume. The same discipline that kept us from taking jev on faith before keeps us from saying anything broader than the measurement showed now. But that's not a reason to delay deploying it here: on this set, for this task, the gap is clean and comfortable, and the solution is already live on the rig — over the next two screens you'll see the actual configured chain, not a draft diagram.

This is the same general move as in this session's first case: the model isn't removed, it's shifted to where it's actually needed — only here we also saw that even inside the "expensive" step there can be more than one candidate, and choosing between them is its own check, not something self-evident.

Now let's look at what this looks like not as a principle, but as configured nodes in an already-built chain.
