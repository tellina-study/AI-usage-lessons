# The result screen and the multi-topic request

## Assertion

Olga only sees that a request touched more than one topic if she opens the result screen herself — and she doesn't open it for every request in a row.

## Visual

> The training center, the same process that went live last session: form → field extraction → write to a table with topic, extra topics, and urgency → result screen. Olga, the administrator, works with this screen — twenty requests a day, and she clears most of them calmly straight off the result screen: the topic and urgency are already there, and who to hand it to is obvious at a glance.

1. Every few requests, the text doesn't come out shorter than usual: the person mentioned something beyond the main question — asked about payment, asked to reschedule a class, asked about the timetable. The "extra topics" field in the table catches this and records it honestly.
2. Olga doesn't open the result screen for every request right away — there are twenty a day, and opening each one the moment it's ready would be exactly the manual work the process was supposed to remove. She checks in batches, several times per shift.
3. If a multi-topic request arrives between checks, it sits in the table with the right fields — and looks no different from an ordinary request until Olga opens the screen herself and reads that exact row.
4. By evening, on a busy day, she admits honestly: she noticed some multi-topic requests not the moment they arrived, but at the next batch check — sometimes hours later.

> **What the training center pays for the delay.** A multi-topic request is almost always more urgent than an ordinary one: the person isn't just asking about the course, they also want to move a paid class to today, or clarify something that has to happen before the end of the day. A batch check every few hours turns exactly that urgency into an ordinary queue.

> Olga states her wish with total precision: "Send me one line in Telegram for every request like that — who wrote, on what topic, and what else they mentioned — so I don't have to go check the result screen because of one multi-topic request among twenty ordinary ones."

## Speaker notes

This is the same process we ended last session with: form, field extraction, write to the table, result screen. Nothing about how the process works changes — what changes is what Olga now expects from it.

Twenty requests a day, and most are about one topic: sign up for a course, reschedule a class, pay. For those, the result screen gives everything needed at once: the topic and urgency are already filled in, and who to hand it to is obvious at a glance.

But every few requests, the person mentions something beyond the main question — payment on top of signing up, rescheduling on top of a course question. The "extra topics" field in the table records this honestly. The problem isn't that the process missed it — it noticed and recorded it correctly. The problem is who sees it, and when.

Olga doesn't sit in front of the result screen all day. She has twenty requests and the ordinary work of a training center around them — she checks in batches, several times a shift. If a multi-topic request lands in the table between checks, it sits there with the right fields and looks no different from an ordinary one: the only way to notice it is to open the screen and read that specific row.

And a multi-topic request is almost always more urgent than a single-topic one. The person isn't just asking about the course — they also want to move a paid class to today, or clarify something that has to happen before day's end. A batch check every few hours turns exactly that urgency into an ordinary queue, and by evening Olga honestly admits: she didn't catch some of these requests right away.

Hence her request, stated with total precision, not "make the notifications smart": one line in Telegram for every multi-topic request — who wrote, on what topic, what else they mentioned — so she doesn't have to go check the result screen over one request among twenty ordinary ones.

How exactly to assemble that line from three already-known values is the question this case opens with.
