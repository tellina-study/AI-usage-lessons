## Speaker notes

The conclusion of the skill stage: with the assistant on, they find more, and that gain is measured by lottery and replicated; the assistant cannot maintain skill, tell the physician about a miss inside the procedure, or measure his result without itself.

And the part of the stage that touches the audience directly. The same phenomenon is measured by randomization outside the clinic, in education: a field study on about a thousand high-school students, in math, three groups — a control, free access to a model, and a model with teacher-set guardrails that cues instead of answering. During practice, free access gave plus 48 percent over the control, and the guardrailed model plus 127. But on an exam with no access to the model, the free-access group came out 17 percent worse than the control, while the guardrailed group came out about at the control's level. This reads as: practice with an unrestricted assistant creates an illusion of learning. And right alongside it, a remedy is measured: guardrails remove the effect, meaning this is cured by the design of access, not the amount of practice.

Alongside it, in the same genre of transfer, a randomized experiment on 216 members of chess clubs: free access to help gave plus 30 percent of a gain, against plus 64 where the system chose the moment to cue, and the free group played 24 percent fewer training games. The cost is paid by the right to decide for yourself when to ask for help. This is a working paper, not a peer-reviewed publication.

And an honest counterweight, without which the axis turns into a sermon: in a randomized experiment with sixty-nine novices who learned with an assistant and took a test without it a week later, deskilling was not confirmed. Medium strength, teenagers, entry-level tasks, a one-week horizon. This cannot be called a refutation of the clinical finding — just as the school experiment cannot be called a replication of it: a different study, different people, a different task. Agreement of sign in one direction and divergence in another mean exactly what they mean: the mechanism is similar, the magnitude does not transfer, and the measurement's horizon decides the outcome.

And what is completely absent. There is no replication of the clinical finding on real-world work, and this gap is named with a number: twenty-two separate search attempts — eighteen query phrasings and four targeted visits to pages; a number about the search, which says nothing at all about the subject itself. A longitudinal measurement of skill among those who learned with the assistant from the start and never knew any other way of working does not exist at all. Nobody has data about the people in this room — neither about skill that has dropped, nor about skill that held. The clinical finding does not fill that void: it was taken on specialists with nearly thirty years of experience, which for this audience still lies ahead.


Let's put the stage together by the same four questions, and close with our own gaps.

What works. With the assistant on, they find more: the gain is measured in randomized trials and replicated many times over, and hiding this half would mean doing exactly what vendors were examined for, only in reverse.

What doesn't. Maintain skill — here the direct opposite is measured. Give the physician feedback about a miss — there is none inside the procedure, and that is exactly why the drain is silent. Measure trust over months of real work — nobody has managed that: everything that exists was taken in a single session.

What happened to the work. Two forms from the glossary. The performer is the same, but got worse: the place, the volume, the order of steps, and the hand are unchanged; what changed is the condition of the hand. And a step appeared — twice: a mediator, translating the model's output into language the physician will trust, and targeted arbitration of disagreements.

What holds this in place. The detection rate and withdrawal time — both quantities are taken straight from ordinary work. And an honestly named empty spot: there is no procedure in any jurisdiction that measures a physician's result without the assistant.

There is one practical consequence, and it transfers beyond medicine: when you put an assistant in the loop, you have to measure both the result with it and the result without it, and not just once. The profession has an indicator for such a measurement, but no procedure — and demanding more from one observational study than it carries would be the same error as demanding work out of a claimed accuracy with no denominator.

**Sources.** [Budzyń et al., Lancet Gastroenterology & Hepatology 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-1253%2825%2900133-5)

About the decision flow — what changed in it besides the findings. A mediator role appeared: the model delivers an output, and a person translates it into language the physician will trust, and this role is new for the department. Alongside it is a measured relationship, and its weakness is named right here: trust in the output is higher among those who work with the model by hand — point estimates exist, statistical significance does not. Routine re-checking of every case has dropped away in the meantime, and targeted arbitration of disagreements has taken its place, meaning the structure of defense against error changed completely, rather than shrinking.

About the change in the structure of defense against error, folded into one line on the slide. Routine re-checking of every case and targeted arbitration of disagreements are not the same thing done cheaper — they are different defense mechanisms: the first catches errors uniformly and cheaply per unit, the second catches errors only where the two sides diverged, and each review there costs more than reading does. The shift from the first to the second changes not the volume of work, but which errors even enter the field of view of the check at all.
