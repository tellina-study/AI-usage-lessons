## Speaker notes

This is the lecture's title slide. It is short in content: the course, the lecture number, the topic, the duration, the date. The lecture is about what modern CAD systems call "AI" — generative design, instant "AI stress analysis," text-to-CAD, LLM copilots — and about why one word hides fundamentally different technologies, with different math, different guarantees, and different failure modes.

The template visually matches the other lectures of the course — this is part of the continuity the student is already used to. There is no additional content here; the slide's job is to mark the entry point into the lecture. The main debunking thesis will already sound on the next slide: an "organic"-looking part generally taken for the work of artificial intelligence was in fact produced by a deterministic numerical optimizer, with a mathematical formulation going back to 1904. The lecture is built in six parts: the foundation and taxonomy of six classes of AI, generative design as optimization ML, surrogate models and PINNs, generative AI and LLMs in CAD, criteria of non-applicability and responsibility, and, at the end, a synthesis in the form of a decision rule. The thread running through the whole lecture: before trusting a tool with a design decision, name its kind and ask about its guarantees.
