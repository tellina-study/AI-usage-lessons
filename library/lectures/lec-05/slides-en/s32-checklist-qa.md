[Top — Ocean rounded box, a checklist of 8 points]

**Before applying AI to a financial/retail task:**
1. Which AI type does the **structure of the task** dictate?
2. **Why NOT an LLM** here? (≥1 structural reason)
3. Can it be solved **without AI at all**? (a hard rule → code)
4. Is the action on the model's output **reversible**? (irreversible → gate)
5. How to **verify the fact** the AI produces? (class of error + principle)
6. Is the decision **regulated**? (reason codes / appeal / legal frame)
7. **Who is liable** and where is a human mandatory? (accountability BEFORE launch)
8. Is there **PII / financial data / biometrics**? (not into a public LLM; 152-FZ)

## Speaker notes

Five sections fold into a working checklist that is applied to a financial or retail task before choosing AI. Which AI type does the structure of the task dictate: a series of numbers over time — forecasting, norm and deviation — anomaly detection, a tabular regulated decision — explainable ML, dialogue — a language model, personalization — recommendations, vision — computer vision. Why not a language model here — state at least one structural reason; if you cannot, you may be taking a hammer to the wrong nail. Can it be solved without AI at all — a hard verifiable rule is solved by deterministic code. Is the action on the model's output reversible — the irreversible requires a human or deterministic gate. How to verify the fact the AI produces — recognize the class of possible error and name the principle of verification. Is the decision regulated — credit, price, a binding fact require reason codes, a legal frame, an appeal path. Who is liable and where is a human mandatory — name the point of accountability before launch. Is there personal or financial data, biometrics — if yes, not into a public cloud.

The assignment — Seminar 5. Teams in thirty minutes verify five real claims about AI in Russian banks and retail against the primary sources of the Bank of Russia and VCIOM, mark them true, partial, or false, and explain the type of error. The boundary is strict: the lecture teaches recognizing the class of error and naming the principle of verification — this is the level of understanding; the seminar — independently verifying, this is the level of application. The lecture deliberately does not do the seminar's work, it prepares for it.

This lecture is the second industry lecture. The unified pattern of the AI-type card, the matrix, and the checklist — this is a lens for all the subsequent industry lectures: in each industry the same question will return — here is a task, which AI type and why this one, where it breaks, and who is liable. Thank you for your attention. Now — your questions.
