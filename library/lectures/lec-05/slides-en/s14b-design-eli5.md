# Visible content

## Title bar
Design in plain terms

## Body
**What it is.** The phase where a hypothesis becomes a draft artifact: a screen sketch, a clickable prototype — something you can show a person and get a reaction to.

**Why.** A prototype is cheap — you won't mind throwing it away. Discovering a solution doesn't work on paper costs pennies; discovering the same thing after launch is very expensive.

**Mental model.** Two diamonds: first you widen and then narrow the search for the problem, then you do the same for the solution. A common mistake is jumping straight to a solution without checking you're solving the right problem.

## Speaker notes

Design in plain terms is the phase where an idea first takes a form you can touch. Not a finished product, but a cheap draft: a sketch, a mockup, a clickable prototype. The point of a draft is that you won't mind losing it: you make it precisely to show people and possibly throw it away right after.

Why is this a separate discipline and not just "draw it nicely"? Because design answers two different questions in sequence. First: are we even solving the right problem? Second: are we solving it correctly? The classical framework is "two diamonds": in the first diamond you first widen your view of the problem, then narrow it down to one clear statement; in the second diamond you do the same with the solution. The most common and most expensive beginner mistake is skipping the first diamond: falling in love with a solution right away, without making sure the problem is real.

One more idea from this phase that we'll come back to in a real failure: safety is built into the product here, in the design brief, not bolted on as a patch after something goes wrong. AI can generate dozens of interface variants in minutes — but choosing and testing on a real person stays your job.
