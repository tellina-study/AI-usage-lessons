## Speaker notes

The real painting is "Valley of Mexico from the Santa Isabel Mountain," 1875, by José María Velasco — a 19th-century Mexican landscape painter, and one of the most famous panoramas of the Valley of Mexico in the history of Latin American painting, now in the public domain.

On a similar text prompt — "a valley panorama, distant mountains, volcanoes on the horizon, warm evening light, 19th-century landscape painting style" — a modern image-generation model would produce a composition similar in spirit within a few seconds. We're not showing an actual generated file here — we're being upfront that this is a hypothetical example, not passing off our own description as a real result.

The lesson of this round: AI can imitate a recognizable style quickly and convincingly, but behind that speed there's no creation history, no artistic intent, no authenticity of the moment — the commercial or artistic value of the original isn't built on visual resemblance alone. Unlike technical text or code, the question here isn't about reliability, but about ethics and the authenticity of authorship.
