# What became checkable over the hour

## Assertion

Under two of the five cards there is now a file you can open and check with a command; under the other three there is no such file — their stages are still ahead.

## Visual

> Under two of the five cards there is now a file: it can be opened and checked with a command. Under the other three there is no such file.

> "The three bottom rows are not checked by anything today: all that was said about them is that their stages are ahead. There are no grounds for passing that off as a result."

## Speaker notes

Look at where the rows are filled in and where the empty slots stand. The difference between them is the result of the hour.

The hook. The barrier is up, and it prints its own holes: nine checks pass, five forms of silence it names in its own voice. What you can open is the settings file and the self-test — the branch and the commit are on the slide.

The skill. A procedure's fate is decided by its description, and the description sits in the file's frontmatter: visible without opening the body.

Under the three bottom rows there is no file, no command and no signal. Their stages are simply ahead. If someone moved their answer there a minute ago — I note it and do not refute it: there is nothing to check it with today, and the session ends on that uncertainty.

I close like this. In the working repository many of you already have a barrier up, and it is up with a broad matcher — it is just that nothing has checked it yet. These same two columns apply to it.

---

**Reference.**

*What the commands that check each of the two rows do.* For the hook — running the self-test from the repository root: it prints a table of checks where each check has an outcome, and the five rows with the limit are visible in it directly. For the skill — reading the frontmatter of the procedure's file: the description and the name are in its first lines, and the body is not read at all. Both checks take a minute and need nothing but a cloned repository.

*If asked why exactly two rows and not three.* Outbound access moved to the next session entirely by the course owner's decision. To declare a third row and not check it would be doing exactly what the whole hour was built against.

*If asked what to count as the session's result.* Not two files. The result is that a checkable question can be asked about each of them and answered with a command. A file you cannot ask such a question about is not in this table.

*If asked what to do next.* I answer in words, without an assignment: the next session takes the remaining three stages and closes them the same way.

*If "we already have a hook" comes up.* This case comes up more than any other. I ask on how many forms of the command it stays silent — and usually there is no answer, because nobody measured. That is the question worth taking away.

*Kept to myself.* I give no call to do anything: the session ends on what became checkable.
