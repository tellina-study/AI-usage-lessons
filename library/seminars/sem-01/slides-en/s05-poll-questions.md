## Speaker notes

A short warm-up before the statistics — getting a sense of how the group works with AI. Simple format: I ask a question, you answer out loud, and raise a hand if you'd like — this isn't a vote with a camera and a count, it's a live conversation, to get a picture of the group.

Four questions. First — which AI tool you use most often: ChatGPT, GigaChat or YandexGPT, GitHub Copilot / Claude / DeepSeek, or none at all. Second — how often: every day, several times a week, less often, never. Third — how much you trust AI's accuracy, on a scale from one to five. Fourth — whether there's been a case where AI clearly let you down.

We'll need these answers in the next block — we'll compare your group with outside research: whether you're higher or lower on trust, whether you use AI more or less often.

## Speaker notes

Now — a live poll. We vote by raising hands: I read out an option, you raise a hand if it applies to you, and the room's camera and the YOLO model — the same technology that counts people in frame in the demo in the course's first lecture — counts the raised hands in real time and shows the number on screen.

Important: this isn't anonymous — the result is visible to the whole room the moment the hands go up. But honesty here matters not because "no one will find out," but because we need a real, not a convenient, picture of the group — this is the material we'll compare with outside statistics in the next block.

Four questions. First — which AI tool you use most often: raise hands in turn for ChatGPT, for GigaChat or YandexGPT, for GitHub Copilot, Claude, or DeepSeek, and separately — who doesn't use one at all. Second — how often: every day, several times a week, several times a month, less often, or never. Third — how much you trust AI's accuracy, on a scale from one to five, raising a hand for each value in turn. Fourth — whether there's been a case where AI clearly let you down — yes, no, not sure.

After each round I say the number out loud and record it — these figures will be needed in the next block, when we compare your group with outside research.
