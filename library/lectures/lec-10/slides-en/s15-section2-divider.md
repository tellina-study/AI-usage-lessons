## Speaker notes

We climb to the second rung — L2, the level of autonomous machines and robots. This is a semi-controlled environment: the machine moves across the field, but its own physics — vibrations, GPS receiver, obstacle sensors, camera optics — gives partial control over what the machine sees and how it reacts. Logically it's a step between the first rung of the fully open field and the third rung of the semi-closed environment of a livestock farm.

And here one of the main lessons of AgTech 2026 emerges: specialization beats universality. Narrow specialized robots — LaserWeeder for weeds on specific crops, Saga Robotics Thorvald for UV-C night treatment of strawberries, Tevel Aerobotics for apple picking — work and scale. Universal "farm robots" — Monarch MK-V, FarmWise — go bankrupt.

In this section we'll examine four working specialization cases. Then three failures: the Monarch Tractor with 2025 lawsuits over "autonomous" with broken autonomy, the FarmWise wind-down plus Naïo recovery as the structural impossibility of a generic CV stack in a real field, and the drawn-out pilot phase of the strawberry-picking robot. In the finale — a critical comparison of Cognitive Pilot and ITELMA as an architectural choice within the AI domain, not as "AI vs. non-AI".
