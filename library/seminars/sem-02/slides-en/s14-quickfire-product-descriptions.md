# Product descriptions

## Assertion

"A separate AI service for product descriptions — good idea?"

## Visual

A card with a mockup of a separate new service — an input form, a "generate"
button. On the right — a question: "A separate service — good idea?"

## Visual — quote

"The director was at a retail conference, saw a competitor's product-description
generator — a standalone app, you type in the specs, you get text for the
product card. Wants something similar by the end of the month. The catalog is
big — around 12,000 SKUs, all of them are entered through the shared catalog
admin panel, and the outsourced copywriter already writes descriptions right
there. Separately, we should probably update the homepage banners, but that's a
different conversation. Also, another department is rolling out a new CRM in
parallel — not related to us."

## Speaker notes

Read the marketing quote in full. Direct signal: marketing specifically wants a
separate standalone service, modeled on the competitor's, catalog of 12,000
SKUs. There's an indirect signal worth pulling out explicitly in discussion:
the copywriter already writes descriptions right inside the shared catalog
admin panel — meaning marketing already has a single workspace where a button
could be inserted, and that changes the answer to "separate service or not"
even before the formal debrief. The homepage banners and the CRM rollout in
another department are decoy facts: both sound like "some other IT project at
the company," but neither affects the architecture for product descriptions.
The slide shows a mockup of a separate new service: an input form, a
"generate" button. Ask the room: is a separate service a good idea, or not?
What do you think, and why exactly — what in this setup actually determines
whether a new tool is worth building at all? During the debrief, ask: which
facts from the quote actually shaped your choice, and which didn't?
