# 17 lectures × 3 modules — from fundamentals to AI by industry

## Assertion

17 lectures × 3 modules — from fundamentals to AI by industry.

## Visual

fundamentals and familiar industries. Module 2 (lectures 7–12, MID): high-tech
manufacturing industries. Module 3 (lectures 13–17, DEEP): infocommunications,

of explanation each, round-2 order: choosing the right tool (not every AI fits
every task), common mistakes (and how to avoid them), human/AI accountability
(who's responsible for the result), information security (what can and can't
be shared with AI).

## Speaker notes

The course consists of seventeen lectures and seventeen seminars, split into three modules. Today's seminar comes before the first lecture — so on the semester map there's no "you are here" marker on a specific lecture, just a "today" marker before the start.

Module one — fundamentals and familiar industries, lectures one through six: the theoretical foundation and a look at the industries you encounter most often in practice — software development, finance and retail, engineering design, medicine, creative industries. Midterm one — at the seminar in week six.

Module two — high-tech industries in physical manufacturing, lectures seven through twelve: aerospace and defense, agriculture, manufacturing, automation and digital twins. Midterm two — at the seminar in week twelve.

Module three — infocommunications, science, extraction, synthesis, lectures thirteen through seventeen: logistics, telecom and cybersecurity, scientific research, oil and gas, and the final synthesis. Midterm three, the final one, at the seminar in week seventeen.

Separate from the structure — four cross-cutting themes that will come up in every lecture and every seminar regardless of industry: choosing the right tool for the task, common mistakes, dividing accountability between human and AI, and information security when working with AI (what can and can't be shared with a model). This isn't a one-off topic for a single session, but a thread that runs through the entire course — which is why it's drawn as a separate tier on the map, not as part of the lecture structure itself.
