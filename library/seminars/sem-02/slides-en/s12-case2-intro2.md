# Support assistant

## Assertion

"An off-the-shelf helpdesk with no open API — the system was bought 4 years ago"

## Visual

A quote card with the IT director's message. On the right — a question: "What do
we do?"

## Visual — quote

"We run an off-the-shelf helpdesk, there's no open API, no customization
options — we wrote to the vendor back in March, never got a reply. The system
was bought 4 years ago, and HR's and facilities' tickets run through it too.
The contract still has 2 years left. The support Telegram channel is a
separate story, it has its own simple bot integration, if that's ever
needed. Next quarter we're redesigning the corporate portal, but that's a
separate process, not related to the helpdesk."

## Speaker notes

Read the IT director's quote in full, without flagging the key words by
intonation. Direct signals, sitting inside the paragraph rather than in the
first sentence: no open API, no customization options, the system was bought 4
years ago, HR's and facilities' tickets run through it too, the contract with
the vendor still has 2 years left. There's an indirect signal worth pulling out
explicitly: the Telegram bot integrates more easily than the closed off-the-
shelf helpdesk — meaning the second integration point from the earlier quote
(operators on Telegram) now has a lower integration cost, and that changes the
balance between options, even if the audience doesn't bring up Telegram
themselves without a nudge. The corporate-portal redesign is a decoy fact: it
sounds like an IT initiative of the same kind, but it has nothing to do with
integrating into the helpdesk. Ask the room: what do we do? Notice I'm not
telling you whether this is good or bad for your plan — I'm just stating a
fact you'd only have learned if you'd asked IT yourself, ahead of time, before
you started designing a solution. Who wants to go first with what you'd
propose now? And for anyone who a minute ago suggested building the assistant
right into the interface — what do you think now that it's turned out the
interface itself is closed off to you entirely? During the debrief, ask: which
facts from the quote actually shaped your choice, and which didn't?
