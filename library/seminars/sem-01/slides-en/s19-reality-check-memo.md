## Speaker notes

Here's the "AI reality-check" memo — five points that will come in handy this very week, drawn from today's quiz.

First: a confident tone in an AI's answer doesn't mean the answer is factually correct. Second: AI doesn't know about events after its training cutoff date without access to web search. Third: an AI's answer isn't derived unambiguously from its training data — the model doesn't "look up" a ready-made chunk of text in the training set, it generates a continuation based on statistical patterns, and because sampling is random, the same question can get different answers. Fourth: AI sees text not as letters, but as tokens — chunks of several characters — so on tasks that require counting letter by letter (say, how many "r"s are in a word), the model can make mistakes, even though overall it works with language extremely well. Fifth: different AI models can give different answers to the same question — cross-checking with several sources is sensible for important decisions.

Take this sheet with you. These five points will be useful not only in this course, but in any work with AI tools.
