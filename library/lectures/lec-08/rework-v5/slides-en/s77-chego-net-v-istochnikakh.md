## Visible slide text

**This is information, not a gap.** Knowing that no measurement exists is more useful than a beautiful number of unknown origin: "a thousand times faster than an artist" would come from dividing a measured figure by a made-up one.

## Speaker notes

The cost layer in this section takes one honest line, and it's a substantive one.

A check across the whole collection of materials on cost gave zero substantive hits on the 3D topic. Four figures weren't found by name, and each of them would have closed its own place in the breakdown. The price of generating a 3D asset per unit of output would have closed the tally of a win in any 3D case. A modeler's rate for a comparable asset would have closed the classical mode against which that win would be measured. The cost of entry into real time — engine license, the cost of building a scene — would have closed the price of the modality as such. The share of the work a generated asset actually closes would have turned "eight seconds" into a meaningful figure. The fourth one probably doesn't exist in nature at all: the material conscientiously lists the remaining stages with no shares of any kind attached, and that's an enumeration, not an omission.

The conclusion from this is worth stating directly. We don't invent a base here. Multipliers of the form "a thousand times faster than an artist" come from dividing a measured number by an estimate pulled out of thin air, and the course hits this separately — this very lecture breaks down a case where two figures with different denominators, placed side by side, create a false pair. So the section presents its possibilities through demonstration and mechanism, and declares no tally of a win at all.

And one more consequence, now about the subject itself. The absence of public measurements for the modality is itself information about its maturity. For flat image, such measurements have existed since 2023; for dubbing, since 2024; for video, since 2025. For 3D, as of the end of 2026, there are none, and that's exactly what you'd expect from a technology that left the lab two years ago. It's more useful for a student to know that no measurements have been published yet than to get a beautiful number of unknown origin: the first lets you make an informed decision, the second creates false confidence.

Now the section's conclusion in full. Where the benefit is: reconstructing a scene from photographs has moved from a research project into an evening's work — forty-one minutes of training and a hundred thirty-four frames per second against forty-eight hours and six hundredths of a frame two years earlier, at unchanged quality. The search for the look has gotten cheaper, down to a few seconds per variant, and the win comes from the same source as in every section of the evening: the failed attempt got cheaper. Neural real-time graphics works at scale and invisibly, filling in fifteen out of every sixteen pixels.

Where the boundaries are: preparing for production stays with people, and that follows from how the method works, not from its immaturity — surface extraction by grid inevitably gives unstructured, dense meshes, and that's what the authors of a commercial generator themselves write. A generated object has no dimensions, and it doesn't replace a design system.

What load moved right: in the spherical-screen case — the boundary between restoration and invention, moved silently and signed by no one. In the asset-generation case — the acceptance stage: the decision "does this mesh go into production or back for rework" is now made many times a day by a person who didn't used to have that decision, because they used to build the mesh themselves.

And a short remark on how to read an absent number in general. The absence of a public measurement is information about a direction's maturity, not a defect in how the material was prepared. For flat image, such measurements have existed since 2023; for dubbing, since 2024; for video, since 2025; for 3D, as of the end of 2026, there are none. The technology left the lab two years ago, and that's exactly what to expect from it: first come demonstrations, then adoption, then — the people who did the accounting start publishing. Knowing that nobody has done the accounting yet is more useful than getting a beautiful number of unknown origin.
