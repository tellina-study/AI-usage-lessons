# Case 4. Image Search

## Assertion

We already know how to read one scan. Today — find a similar one among the past ones.

## Visual

> Two sessions ago showed the limits of handing a model a picture. Last session — that the model can read it. Today — the same storage-and-search task as in the three previous cases, just for scans and photos.

## Speaker notes

This is an extension of a line that runs through the two previous sessions; calling it a brand-new topic from scratch would be wrong. Two sessions ago showed the basic limits of handing a picture to a model — how much an attachment weighs and what happens when the limit is exceeded. Last session taught the process to read a photo attached to a request — say, a scan of a form or a receipt — and understand what's written on it. Both times, the conversation was about one specific picture that had just arrived.

Today's need is different: Olga's training center gets scans of forms and receipts in requests regularly, and a new request often looks like one of the past ones — same type of form, same supplier, same receipt format. We need to find a similar scan among the past ones: reading the current one is not enough here — the same way the three previous cases of this session found a similar text document.

This is the same storage-and-search task we've already been working through all session today, just applied to a different type of data. Let's see how honestly what we already know carries over.

The three previous cases of this session built a neat picture in advance, before this very breakdown: storing, cutting, and searching text are solved by similarly-spirited techniques, and each next technique sits neatly on top of the last. It's natural to expect the picture to fit into that same scheme just as smoothly — but that exact expectation is what today's run checks; it isn't taken on faith just because it held three times in a row.

This is specifically Olga's training center's task; there's no abstract organization here. Requests come in through the form, a photo of a document is often attached to them, and that document is almost never unique: there's no shortage of refund requests against the same contract with the same supplier over a year. Finding a similar past scan among already-processed requests isn't an abstract information-retrieval exercise — it's a direct working need that saves Olga time on cross-checking every time a new request arrives that looks familiar in form.
