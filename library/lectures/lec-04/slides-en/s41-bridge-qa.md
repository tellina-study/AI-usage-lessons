## Speaker notes

Let us fold the whole lecture into one load-bearing idea to leave with: AI changes the cost of writing code, but not the cost of understanding what to build and who is responsible for it. We went through the entire lifecycle and saw that AI touches each phase differently — somewhere it amplifies, somewhere it creates false confidence, somewhere it does not reach at all. And across all phases the conclusion is one: reliability comes not from the tool but from discipline — a chain of human-owned artifacts and human gates at the right points. The tool is secondary: the method decides, the tool executes.

Now about why this was the first industry lecture. The phase-centric map, the triangulation of independent measurements and the risk triad are not only about software development. They are a lens for all the subsequent industry lectures: in each industry there will be the same question in a new wrapper. And what transfers is precisely the method, not the tool list, because tools go out of date and the method does not. The method in four steps: decompose the industry's activity into phases; for each phase ask whether its complexity is accidental or essential; demand a baseline for every loud number and look at the systemic effect rather than the feeling; and separate the durable pattern from vendor hype with those five questions we covered. You applied this method throughout the lecture, and it is your working instrument for the whole course.

And now — I am ready for questions. Think: which of your own recent tasks would you run through today's checklist — and what would it tell you?
