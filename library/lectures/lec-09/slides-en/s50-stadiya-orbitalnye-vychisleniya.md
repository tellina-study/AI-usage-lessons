## Visible slide text

**A reversal of the whole lecture's logic.** In Sections 1–3, the question was "what part of a human's work can a model do next to the action itself." Here the question is different: not what the model can do, but **where it's physically cheaper to put it.**

## Speaker notes

Five parts repeat the same general scheme that already opened Section 1, Section 2, and Section 3: term, data and question, ways of doing it without automation, the decision being made, the alternative. This is the scheme's last use in the lecture, and the material it organizes here is the least like the previous three — not about action, not about remote control, not about design, but about the physical location of the computing infrastructure itself.

What this stage is. The industry term is orbital computing: placing AI computing infrastructure physically in space rather than on Earth. What data is collected and what question it answers. The data here isn't observation of the outside world — it's the internal power and cooling budget of the data center itself. The question is where it's physically cheaper to compute, if demand for AI computing is growing faster than ground-based power generation can keep up with it. How this is done on Earth without it. A ground-based data center is built next to a source of electricity and cooling — a river, a power grid, a suitable climate. What decision gets made. Put the computing in orbit, where the sun shines almost continuously and there's no competition for land or water for cooling. How this is handled without orbital placement. A ground-based data center, limited by the power grid's throughput and the climate of a particular region.

Why does this section come last, rather than somewhere in the middle? Because it depends logically on the material of all three previous sections at once. This section is the answer to the question "where is it physically cheaper to do all this computing work," which can only be asked once it has been shown how much of this work has already appeared, and keeps appearing.
