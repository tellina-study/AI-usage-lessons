## Speaker notes

The nine forms of work relocation — in full, in one table, with a tally under each form. Seven forms carry a number, two don't, and both dashes are named as dashes. The glossary was handed out by name at the start, with no numbers, and has held up every breakdown since: on every breakdown, instead of the image "the load moved rightward," there stood a named form with a number. The image lies wherever the work didn't move but doubled, disappeared, or stayed in place and dropped in quality — and nine lines are needed exactly so these cases don't blur into one.

The glossary splits by question. The first five measure the place and volume of the work: where it is now, and how much of it there is. The second four measure the hand, time, the consequence of an error, and the condition of the hand itself: who does the work, when, and what is paid for a miss. This split was announced on the axis at the start, and is only confirmed here: the form answers what happened to the work; the address answers at which human step it happened.

Let's go through the load-bearing lines. The load moved to another step — recording the visit: note time dropped by nine and a half percent against the control group, while active editing time did not change for either of the two tools. That is, typing got shorter and review stayed exactly the same — the work moved from typing to reading someone else's text, and this is the evening's clearest case of the first form.

A step appeared — arbitration on disagreement, where the model replaces the second reader, and this is the glossary's strongest line. Two National Health Service centers in England, fifty thousand women: without the model, 3.9 percent of reads went to arbitration in one center and 11.3 percent in the other; with the model, 9.4 percent and 13.8 percent. Almost threefold in the first and by a quarter in the second, and this spread is not noise — it is a difference in protocol. Alongside it, a Norwegian measurement on 441,000 cases: one primary read takes forty-eight seconds, arbitration on disagreement two minutes twelve — that is, one arbitration costs about as much time as roughly five reads.

The performer changed — two pillars here. The first: imaging the fundus at the visit instead of a trip to the ophthalmologist, with turnout rising from 22 out of 100 to 100. The second, domestic: a Russian assistant for colonoscopy takes over part of the search, and procedures with a finding become 472 per 1,000 against 413. This same case also carries the fourth form's second pillar: 1.23 false alarms per study where there used to be zero — the load grew for the same physician at the same spot. The fourth form's first pillar — the sepsis model: an alert comes in for 18 out of 100 hospitalizations, and 12 out of 100 raised alerts are confirmed, and the two are counted against different bases.

The load fell at the same spot — the volume of human reading in the Swedish program is lower by more than forty-four percent, with more cancers found, eighty thousand women. The flip side of this same form — narrowing of visual search under a false cue: the volume of work fell, and that turned out to be a cost, not a win.

The cost of error shifted — two pillars, and both about a subgroup. On dark skin, three models tell sick from healthy worse by nine to fifteen points out of 100, and the fix for that is the composition of the training set, not the algorithm. At an equal screening score, Black patients have more chronic conditions by 26 out of 100 — the score labeled as equally healthy people whose health actually differs. The average across the sample stayed decent in both cases; the subgroup pays.

The performer is the same, but got worse — two pillars, and both have already been voiced. Surgeons with a model's cue on septic shock: 0.712 against 0.820 without the cue. And the one measurement of erosion on real clinical work: for the same nineteen physicians, procedures with a finding became 224 per 1,000 against 284, minus sixty findings per thousand.

Now about the two forms with no tally, and papering over them with a similar case is forbidden. The removal of a step with no new object appearing has not come up once this lecture: three candidates were checked, and in each one a new step appeared in return. The second human reader left the workstation, but in return a step appeared — deciding whether this study needs a second read at all. Routine re-checking of every case went away, and arbitration on disagreement, counted in minutes, took its place. Typing shrank, and reviewing someone else's draft appeared. You cannot claim from this that "the model doesn't remove steps": such a claim would be broader than anything the lecture has measured. The form measuring the order of work has no tally for a different reason: the order in these breakdowns did get rearranged — for example, who reads first, the human or the model — but it was never counted as an order in a single case.

And the distinction the eighth form rests on, because without it you'd confuse it with its neighbors. In the fifth form, the hand changed; here the comparison is built inside the same nineteen physicians, and the measurement directly rules out a change of performer. In form four-a, the volume of the operation fell; here the volume is the same, and the finding rate fell — meaning these are different units of measurement. A quick check is the question of the denominator: form four-a has none at all, it's an absolute volume; the eighth has one, and it didn't change. The fifth and eighth stand on the same breakdown, and that is not a contradiction: colonoscopy with the assistant has two states of the loop — while the assistant is on, the model does part of the search, and when it's off, that same hand finds less than it found before the assistant arrived.

The question for the room is worth asking and waiting for an answer to: a form number from your own work gets named in a few seconds, and the named number then carries the rest of the conversation.
