# 4 criteria for refusing AI in a creative project

## Assertion

4 criteria for refusing AI: training-data license / output similarity / voice-likeness consent / brand-trust risk.

## Speaker notes

This is the central normative slide of the lecture. Here we translate the lessons of Section three into four practical criteria for refusing AI in a creative project. Criterion one — training-data license. If the tool does not have a documented licensed training corpus — you have legal debt in category one — Andersen and RIAA. Concretely: using Stable Diffusion for a commercial output creates latent class-action risk. Using Adobe Firefly — no, because Firefly is trained on Adobe Stock plus licensed content. This is not a choice of tool by "quality," it is a choice by structural compliance with requirements. Criterion two — output-similarity check. If outputs may reproduce protected content verbatim — you have liability in category two, NYT verbatim citation. If you build a product on a foundation model, you must have a technical control that tracks the similarity of the output to protected source content. Without it you bear the verbatim-citation risk personally. Criterion three — voice and likeness consent. If the product uses the voice or face of specific people — explicit consent is needed. ScarJo-class risk applies not only to celebrities — to any recognizable real people. The Korea schoolgirl crisis showed that even minors' selfies fall under this criterion. Criterion four — brand-trust risk. If you deploy AI for a flagship or historical campaign — Coca-Cola Christmas, a Sports Illustrated by-line, an NYT investigation — this is a structural expenditure of brand capital. The sentiment reversal is measurable, and its loss does not recover in one cycle. These four criteria are not "a list of AI prohibitions." They are diagnostic questions. If on any of the four you identify a risk, you have three options. Option A — do not use AI, use a non-AI alternative. Option B — mitigate the risk structurally: a licensing layer, an output-similarity check, a consent-acquisition infrastructure, brand-aware design. Option C — accept the risk explicitly, with a documented business decision and a calibrated mitigation. What you cannot do is accidentally take on a risk without knowing about it. This four-point diagnostic is your protective layer.
