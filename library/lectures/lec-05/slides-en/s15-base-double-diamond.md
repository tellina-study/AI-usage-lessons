# Visible content

## Title bar
Two diamonds: first the right problem, then the right solution

## Body
[2 diamonds: Discover→Define / Develop→Deliver, with a crossed-out anti-pattern arrow]

**Double Diamond** (Design Council UK)
- Diamond 1: Discover (divergence) → Define (convergence) — **the right problem**
- Diamond 2: Develop (divergence) → Deliver (convergence) — **the right solution**

[Anti-pattern, gold]
Common mistake: converging on a solution without checking the problem itself — skipping the first diamond

## Speaker notes

Design Thinking is a five-stage, human-centered methodology, canonically taught by the Stanford d.school. Double Diamond is the UK Design Council's framework, a visual model of two divergence-and-convergence cycles: the first diamond is Discover, broad exploration, then Define, synthesis into a concrete brief — about the right problem; the second diamond is Develop, generating many directions, then Deliver, testing and shipping one — about the right solution.

For this lecture's purposes we use Double Diamond as the section's load-bearing framework, because it's structurally equivalent to Design Thinking but shows the key idea more explicitly: two separate divergence-convergence cycles, not one. A common mistake for an engineer encountering the design process for the first time: instinctively converging on a solution before even checking the problem itself — skipping the first diamond and diving straight into the second. It's the same anti-pattern as "just write the code," skipping the question of whether we're even solving the right problem.

In practice the stages aren't linear — teams constantly loop back from Deliver to Define, and that's not a sign the process failed, it's the process working normally. Keep this framework in mind: when we get to this section's first failure, you'll see it happened exactly at the first-diamond point — in the decision of what we're even building and for whom — not in the technical implementation.
