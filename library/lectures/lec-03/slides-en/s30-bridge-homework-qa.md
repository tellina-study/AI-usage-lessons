## Speaker notes

Lecture 3 closes the course's overview module, which went in an ascending order: Lecture 1 — what AI is and how a prompt is built; Lecture 2 — why a prompt works: tokenization, embeddings, attention, sampling; Lecture 3 — how to assemble a system out of a model and how to choose which system to assemble. These three lectures are a common foundation; next the course moves to the industry lectures, where AI is applied to concrete subject tasks.

This lecture's framework — the ladder of complexity, the choice route, the eight-step checklist, the rule "when not AI," the agent starter kit, the rule of the human validator — is the base for all subsequent lectures, not material that ends with this lecture. In every industry topic the same question will repeat in a new wrapper: here is a subject task — which architecture and why exactly this one, and under what condition the answer would be different. Now you have the apparatus to answer it. A practical piece of advice for the rest of the course: at every industry lecture, hearing "here they apply RAG, an agent, or fine-tuning," mentally run the checklist — does the task requirement really raise you to this rung, or is it inertia.

Lecture 4 takes this apparatus and applies it to the first industry topic — AI in software development. There the question "which architecture and why" will meet you again, now on concrete tools and tasks of a developer: the same coding agents we examined through the harness frame — Claude Code, Aider, Cursor, OpenHands — will there be considered from the side of engineering development practice. The assignment — for Seminar 3. The paired seminar "Architectural choice: chat, agent, RAG, API — three cases" is the place for full mastery of the application learning goal. You will apply the eight-step checklist to three cases and for each formulate: the architecture, at least two reasons along the axes, at least one condition under which the choice would be different, and an explicit criterion "here it would be NOT so." Mini-apply task B from the previous slide is a warm-up before the seminar.
