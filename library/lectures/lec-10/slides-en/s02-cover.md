## Speaker notes

This is the title slide of the tenth lecture — the seventh industry lecture in our course. Agriculture is a unique testing ground for understanding where AI structurally works and where it structurally does not. Here, within a single production cycle, environments of different types coexist — an open field with biological unpredictability and a controlled logistics chain with digital traces. That's why in a single industry you can simultaneously see both a canonical AI success and a canonical AI failure — which gives the engineer one of the most useful mental tools: an understanding of why the same class of models works in one environment and breaks in another.

Unlike medicine, where the regulator is unified and the validation methodology strict, agriculture has a historical regulatory vacuum — ISO standards exist, but they are not as strict as DO-178C in aviation. This is part of the explanation for why the best AI success cases in agriculture are the ones where vendors invest in quality themselves, rather than where the regulator mandates it.

The tone of the lecture is operational: what exactly works in 2026, where it breaks, and what specific alternatives are available at each rung of the ladder we'll be climbing. After each section there are self-check questions.
