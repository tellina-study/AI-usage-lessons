Part 6 — Synthesis: a decision rule on a concrete problem.

## Speaker notes

Six classes of AI have been named and placed on the axis, three classes with the highest cost of error have been examined in depth along with their failures, the criteria of non-applicability have been assembled into a table, and the boundary of responsibility has been stated explicitly. Next comes the lecture's final, synthesizing part.

No new class and no new fact is introduced here. The part has a different job: to show that everything dissected so far assembles into a single working tool for thinking. First — a fully worked example: one engineering problem is run through the three anchor questions in two production scenarios, and it becomes visible how one question can split the justified answers apart. Then the decision rule is folded into a short mental checklist, next to which the full taxonomic matrix of six classes is presented — the one promised at the start of the lecture and deliberately held back until every cell could be filled with meaning.

The point of this part is not to memorize one more thing, but to carry away a procedure: on meeting a tool labeled "AI," name its kind, place it on the axis, check the completeness of the statement, ask about the exact normative tool, and remember that the signature and responsibility are not delegated. The lecture closes with an open discussion.
