# Case 1. Knowledge Base Storage

## Assertion

Every week a new item gets added to Olga's knowledge base — and one day ten lines in the prompt stop being enough.

## Visual

> The training center's knowledge base grows every week: a new regulation, an updated FAQ item, another disputed case worked through. Seminar 9's ten items were the starting point; from here the knowledge base only grows.

## Speaker notes

Last session the process learned to answer the requester on its own — from a knowledge base of ten items, written into the prompt in full. That worked without a hitch: ten lines of text don't load any model call noticeably.

But Olga's knowledge base didn't freeze at ten items. The training center is growing: every week brings a new regulation, an updated FAQ item, another disputed case worked through with the last cohort. Ten items was a snapshot of the base as of last session; from here it keeps growing on its own.

That's where today's whole case comes from. The training center wants the same thing it wanted last session: an accurate, cheap answer to the requester. The difference is that the base the process relies on no longer stands still. The most natural continuation of what already worked is simply to keep appending to the base in the same prompt, with no ceiling. That's what was done last session, and that's what Olga assumes will keep being done — until someone checks what happens when the prompt holds several hundred documents instead of ten items.

Now we'll work out what Olga needs from a solution like this, propose options, and test each one on real questions where the correct answer is already known.

At ten items, the idea of "just keep appending to the prompt" looks like the most sensible of all. The problem we'll measure today is different: the solution stops being sensible under certain conditions, and those conditions need to be known in advance, as a number — before the base grows and the process starts failing. That's exactly why the question last session ended on — at what base size does all this stop working — has a measurable answer: a specific threshold, and we're going to find it.
