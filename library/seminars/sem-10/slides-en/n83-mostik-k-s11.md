# What's Next

## Assertion

Knowledge outside the prompt and a checked answer are two different steps; the next one adds a third: the right to act.

## Visual

> **Can the process not just answer, but act on the company's behalf?**

The question the previous seminar opened with ("can the process answer the requester on its own") and the question today's opened with ("where does the process get its facts") together lead to the next step on the course's stepped arc: what happens when the process is given not just knowledge, but authority — the right not just to propose an answer, but to carry out an action on the company's behalf.

> And what today's session doesn't fix: this day's interlude — "found" ≠ "correct" — isn't closed off either by taking the base outside or by the right to act. That's a separate rung: quality evaluation and a kill switch, further along the arc.

## Speaker notes

Today's session closed the question it started with: where does the process get its facts, and why does it consider them true. The four cases gave it a full, measured answer, and the interlude about Google AI Overviews and the municipal chatbot added a warning to it that's worth carrying forward: even a perfectly built search doesn't guarantee that what's found is correct.

The question the previous seminar started with — can the process answer the requester on its own, without staying silent and without handing everything off to a human — and the question today's started with — where does the process get the facts it builds that answer on — together lead to the next step on the course's stepped arc. The next rung: what happens when the process is given not just knowledge, but authority — the right not just to propose an answer, but to carry out an action on the company's behalf. This is a separate, qualitatively new risk: a mistake in the text of an answer can be caught and never sent; a mistake in an action already taken cannot.

And I'll say one thing honestly, so as not to leave a false impression. Today's interlude — the finding that "relevant doesn't mean correct" — isn't closed off either by the fact that we took the knowledge base outside, or by the fact that the process will learn to act on its own behalf. That's a separate, still-later rung of the arc: evaluating the quality of what was found and the answer built on it, and a kill switch that keeps a wrong answer from reaching action. Knowledge outside the prompt is a necessary step, but not the last one.

Looking at this course's arc as a whole, you can see that every new rung doesn't cancel out the previous one, but does add it a risk the previous rung was never obligated to handle. A process that only answers with text can make a mistake — and that mistake will be seen either by the requester themselves or by whoever receives the message after them. A process given the right to act on the company's behalf makes mistakes with different consequences: an action, unlike text, can't always be rolled back. That's exactly why it makes sense to close off knowledge outside the prompt, which we worked through today, before giving the process authority — the foundation of reliable facts needs to be in place before anyone starts building the ability to act on top of it.
