## Visible slide text

# 2. Teleoperation

**A human is in control — but from a distance. Where does this stop working?**

In order:

**Dragon** — autonomous docking to the ISS
**Predictive latency compensation** — a trained model instead of a classical filter
**Astrobee** — ML-assisted piloting

## Speaker notes

This section is the second step of the chain: the degree of autonomy shown in Section 1 drops here, and we show where a human still holds control — just from a distance — before Section 3 moves away from autonomy itself to a different type of task altogether.

Three breakdowns in order: Dragon's autonomous docking to the ISS — automation gets a whole, explicitly carved-out piece of the task (the final 220 meters of approach), the human controls the rest himself. Predictive latency compensation — the trained model is not a supporting draft and not manual intervention, but the active mechanism itself, continuously predicting the scene's future state and compensating for the operator's perception of the delay. Astrobee — automation is built into every single control cycle, proposing a draft solution that a classical controller refines before trusting it.

The section opens not with a definition but with a scene — a deliberate break in form: the lecture's first substantive section was built on the form "term, general description, then breakdowns," and after that opening the form starts to become predictable. Here we deliberately break it.
