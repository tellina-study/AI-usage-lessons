# Reserve. Klarna — four forks in the road

## Assertion

The same case runs through all four choice types, plus one vendor KPI with no baseline

## Visual

Four rows: "AI or not" — routine, formalizable tickets. "Built-in or
custom" — built into the existing support channel. "Architecture" — chat
with escalation to a human. "Inference" — external cloud API with
customer data. At the bottom — a fact card: "the equivalent of 700
agents" / $40 million (2024) → "853 agents" / $60 million (Q3 2025), the
denominator never disclosed.

## Speaker notes

Klarna launched an OpenAI-based AI support assistant in February 2024,
right inside the existing support channel. In the first 30 days — 2.3
million chats, 67% of requests automated. Look at all four forks at once.
Routine tickets like order status or returns are a formalizable, repeated
task, a good candidate for automation. The assistant is built into an
already-existing support channel — the right choice by today's session's
logic. The architecture is chat with escalation to a human, not an
autonomous agent with no oversight. But the inference — an external cloud
API with support customer data — is a decision with a risk profile
comparable to our sales-calls case, except here they accepted it and left
it in production. Separately — the company claimed an effect "the
equivalent of 700 agents" and $40 million in 2024, and the
denominator — how many support agents the company had before the
launch — was never publicly disclosed. By Q3 2025 the metric had updated
to "853 agents" and $60 million in effect, and the denominator again
wasn't disclosed. In May 2025 the CEO, Sebastian Siemiatkowski, told
Bloomberg in an interview: "We went too far" — and the company went back
to hiring people, moving to a hybrid model. Which of the four forks do
you think Klarna got wrong the worst?
