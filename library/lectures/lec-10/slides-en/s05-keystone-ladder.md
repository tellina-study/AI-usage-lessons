# The AI ladder — from field to shelf. Each rung works differently

## Assertion

Five ladder levels. AI climbs from field to shelf — controllability rises, biological unpredictability falls, measurable ROI rises.

## Speaker notes

This is the keystone slide of the whole lecture, and it's worth examining closely — we'll come back to it between each of the following sections.

The keystone axis of this lecture is a ladder of five levels, along which AI climbs from field to shelf. From bottom to top, the degree of environmental control rises, biological unpredictability falls, and measurable return on investment rises.

L1 — Field. Open biological environment. Sun, rain, pathogens, dust — everything that is uncontrollable in an engineering sense. The canonical success is See & Spray Ultimate on five million acres. The canonical failure is the collapse of vertical farming to the tune of one point three seven billion dollars and more.

L2 — Robot, or machine. Semi-controlled: the machine moves across the field, but its own physics — vibrations, GPS receiver, camera optics — gives partial control over what the machine sees. Success — LaserWeeder G2 on two hundred fifty thousand acres. Failure — Monarch MK-V and the 2025 lawsuits over "autonomous" tractors that don't operate autonomously.

L3 — Animal. Semi-closed environment with measurements at the level of the individual animal. Success — SenseHub on two million cows, CattleEye, DeLaval VMS. Failure — Cainthus as an example of "partnership announced, deployment not verified".

L4 — Supply chain. Controlled cargo flows: containers, warehouses, exchanges, contracts. Here — gold accent — is the place of most active agentic AI in production deployment as of 2026. Cargill CMAX won the BIG AI Excellence Award in 2026. Failure — the cancellation of USDA Climate-Smart Commodities in April 2025, when $3.1 billion of program funding vanished by political decision.

L5 — Consumer, retail. Fully digitized environment: every SKU has a digital footprint, every transaction is logged. Walmart Eden ML since 2017, Tesco AI since 2017, X5 «Perekrestok» since 2020 — this is already world-class. There are essentially no failure cases here — this level reliably works for established players.

To the right of the ladder — an arrow with two labels. Upward rise controllability, ROI, predictability; downward falls biological unpredictability. That is the explanatory mechanism. And it's neither ideology nor a forecast; it's a 2026 observation. And it explains one seemingly paradoxical situation: venture investment in indoor farming and novel farming systems has collapsed — segment funding fell 53% year-over-year (AgFunder Global Report 2025: Q1-Q3 2025 $1.5B versus $3.2B for 2024). At the same time, investment in agentic AI for commodity trading and procurement is growing at double-digit rates. The explanation lies in the speed of feedback: at L4 traders measure the outcome of a hedge in basis points over minutes to hours; at L1 the farmer measures the result of a fertilizer over a season. The speed of feedback determines whether AI manages to pay off before the environment changes.

Each following section of this lecture is a motivated climb up one rung of the ladder. The axis does not "surface" somewhere in the middle; it opens here, at the very start, and returns as a callback in the finale.
