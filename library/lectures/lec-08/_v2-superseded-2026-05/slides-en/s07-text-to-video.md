## Speaker notes

By May 2026, text-to-video stopped being a demonstration technology. Three flagship models define the state of the industry. OpenAI Sora 2 — released in September 2026, maximum length of a single clip 25 seconds, resolution 1080p, synchronized audio, character cameos for multi-scene consistency. API price about ten cents per second at 720p, thirty-to-fifty cents at 1080p. Google Veo 3.1 — length four-to-eight seconds depending on tier, native audio, price five cents per second on Lite. Kuaishou Kling 3.0 — released February 5, 2026, up to fifteen seconds at native 4K and 60 fps; in blind tests on Video Arena, Kling 3.0 as of February 2026 takes first place with an ELO of 1243, ahead of Veo and Runway. What "production" means in the film industry. This is not replacement of the shoot, it is augmentation of pre-production and post-production. A concrete landmark — Lionsgate's partnership with Runway, announced in September 2024. Lionsgate provided Runway its IP corpus to train a custom model; the use — previsualization, storyboards, post-production backgrounds, VFX. On the quarterly earnings call in November 2024, Lionsgate Vice Chair Michael Burns confirmed that the AI pipeline saves millions and millions of dollars on pre/post-production. Critically — none of Lionsgate's statements say that AI replaced the shoot. Film production is still actors plus cameras plus locations; AI augments the stages before and after the shoot. An important mini-failure block for the entry into Section 3: 25 seconds is not a film. The cinematic pipeline is still assembled from short blocks. When Toys R Us tried to make a 66-second single Sora clip for an ad at Cannes Lions 2024, the result triggered a strong public backlash — we will work through this case in detail in the third section.
