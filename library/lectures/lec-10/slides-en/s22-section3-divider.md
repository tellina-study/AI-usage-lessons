## Speaker notes

We climb to the third rung — L3, the level of livestock. This is a semi-closed environment: the animals are kept in a barn or on pasture, the environment is partly controlled, but the individual variability of each animal — behavior, health, productivity — is high. At this level AI works more reliably than at the first and second rungs, for two structural reasons.

The first. The economics are concentrated. One cow gives milk worth three to five thousand dollars per year; the loss of one cow due to an undetected disease is a measurable loss. This creates direct and clear economics for AI sensors and analytics.

The second. Measurements at the individual-animal level are possible — via a tag, a camera, a collar. This turns an open-environment farm into an array of semi-closed-loop micro-environments: one animal is one loop. And on each loop AI works on a narrow task with measurable feedback.

In this section we'll examine four working cases of 2026: Allflex SenseHub with two million cows mounted; CattleEye, acquired by GEA in 2024; DeLaval VMS V310 with ninety-nine point eight percent attachment rate; Cargill Birdoo for broiler weight estimation. Then three anti-hype lessons in one block: Cainthus as an example of "partnership announced, no metrics", tie-stall barns as a physical configuration that breaks CV, and Holstein bias as an architectural asymmetry for Russia's local breeds. And at the end — the Russian parallel Connectome.ai plus the sanction uncertainty of the dairy stack.
