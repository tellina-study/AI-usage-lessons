## Speaker notes

Besides SenseHub, three other working cases on the third rung are worth examining, because they show different architectural approaches to similar tasks.

CattleEye — a different angle. A cow walks along a corridor exiting the milking parlour. A camera films its gait from above; the model estimates a lameness score from the biomechanics of movement — step symmetry, head-bob amplitude, rump drop. Lameness is one of the leading causes of reduced milk yield and premature culling; early detection, when the cow still walks but is already compensating for pain, gives the farmer two to three weeks to treat before the process becomes irreversible. The solution is cheap to deploy — CCTV plus cloud, without putting a collar on each animal. This is structurally different from SenseHub: SenseHub requires putting a collar on each animal, CattleEye doesn't. Sixty farms, eleven thousand cows directly; via the GEA channel — farms serving more than two hundred fifty thousand cows. Acquired by GEA in 2024 — an example of L3 market consolidation.

DeLaval VMS V310 — a robotic milking machine. The cow enters the station on its own when it feels a full udder — usually two or three times a day. A camera and a laser scanner find the teats; a robotic arm attaches the milking cups. A ninety-nine point eight percent attachment rate means: nine hundred ninety-eight of a thousand attempts are successful — the remaining two require manual intervention or a repeat attempt. This is a very narrow task in which AI plus mechanics give a deterministically better result than manual labor. The cow doesn't waste time in a milking queue, can milk more often, which increases the daily yield; the farmer is freed from night and morning milkings; the robot works twenty-four seven. The fifteen-percent growth in North American installations in 2025 reflects the economic logic on large dairy farms.

Cargill Birdoo — computer vision for estimating broiler weight in a poultry house without physical weighing. A camera above captures the bird's silhouette, the model estimates its mass from visual features. The claimed accuracy — more than ninety-five percent; savings of ten to thirty grams of feed per broiler thanks to the ability to dose more precisely: if the flock's average weight has already reached the target, switch from growth feed to finishing feed earlier. Americas-exclusive — this is a narrow niche in which the solution works and scales.

The common pattern of all three — augmentation, not replacement. AI recognizes signals, the human makes the decisions. A narrow task with measurable feedback. This is the structural success of the third rung, which explains why the L3 market in 2024-2025 went through consolidation — MSD absorbed Antelliq for three point eight five billion, GEA acquired CattleEye — rather than bankruptcies. The market is mature.
