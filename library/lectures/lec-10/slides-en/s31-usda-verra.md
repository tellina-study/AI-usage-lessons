## Speaker notes

Not all fourth-level cases work. Let's examine two parallel failure blocks: the USDA Climate-Smart cancellation and the Verra phantom credits.

The USDA Climate-Smart Commodities program. This is a US Department of Agriculture program launched under the Biden administration, a budget of three point one billion dollars, one hundred thirty-five projects, fourteen thousand farms, three point two million acres. The program supported the transition to climate-smart practices — no-till, cover crops, precision nitrogen — with AI-MRV to verify the effect on emissions reduction.

In April 2025 the Trump administration canceled the program, replacing it with a rebranded AMP — Advancing Markets for Producers — with a requirement to direct no less than sixty-five percent of federal funding straight to farmers. This is a substantially different direction: Climate-Smart funded the transition to sustainable practices with MRV verification; AMP — direct market payments to producers without establishing a link to climate outcomes.

What this means for AgTech business models. Dozens of AI-MRV startups — carbon-credit verification, soil carbon monitoring, sustainability metrics — built their business model on the flow from Climate-Smart projects. The cancellation of the program turned these startups' revenue projections into uncertainty in practically a single day. Some projects will survive under AMP, some won't.

The lesson: federal policy is a tail risk for the AgTech business model. When a business relies on public funding in one jurisdiction, a shift in the political climate can wipe out a revenue projection in a day. The alternative: unit economics over policy-tailwind plays. Businesses that hold up on the commercial side without relying on state funding survive. Investor sentiment in 2025-2026 has already shifted. This lesson applies to the Russian context in mirror form: the Russian state program "Digital Agriculture" 2019-2024 failed its goal of doubling productivity — agriculture in 2024 showed minus three point two percent; the new program "Agriculture of the Future" 2026-2030 is the next attempt with a risk of the same class.

The second fourth-level failure concerns AI-MRV for carbon credits. Verra — the world's largest verifier of voluntary carbon credits, the VCS standard. In January 2023 The Guardian, together with Die Zeit and SourceMaterial, published the results of a nine-month investigation: more than ninety-four percent of the rainforest offset credits issued through REDD+ projects under the Verra standard are phantom credits — that is, they don't correspond to the claimed emissions reductions. Pachama, one of the projects in this program — overestimation by eight times.

The link to AI-MRV. Carbon projects rely on AI systems to estimate the carbon stock in forests, soils, and agricultural systems. These systems use satellite imagery plus ML models to predict how much carbon is deposited in a given area and how much would have been deposited without the project's intervention — the baseline scenario. This is inference with large uncertainty, because the baseline scenario is hypothetical and has no ground truth; ML models are trained on a limited dataset and generalize poorly to new regions and ecosystem types; marketing these estimates as "precise measurement" creates a false impression of accuracy. The result — large-scale greenwashing: the issuance of carbon credits sold to corporations as proof of "net zero" commitments, while the actual emissions reduction is considerably smaller than claimed.

A very important caveat against misattribution. Verra phantom credits pertain to rainforest offset projects under a specific methodology. This doesn't mean that all AI-MRV are phantom; it doesn't mean the carbon-credit market as a whole is worthless. Agricultural soil-carbon is a different methodology: Climate Action Reserve, ACR, Verra VM0042 v2 — newer methodologies with improved validation. Indigo Ag — an example of a company using the Climate Action Reserve, not Verra — verified two million tons, a contract with Microsoft in 2026 for two point eight five million tons through a twelve-year agreement. Indigo Ag is not in the Verra scandal — it's a different standard, a different methodology. Don't make a cascade misattribution.

And the main anti-AI criterion from this slide — AP-seven. AI-MRV for carbon claims without direct measurement is a categorical anti-pattern. The alternative: direct soil sampling of a meaningful share of projects + transparent uncertainty bands; AI as a hypothesis, not as fact.
