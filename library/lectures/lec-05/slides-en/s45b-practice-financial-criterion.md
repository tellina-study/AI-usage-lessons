# Visible content

## Title bar
We write down the result at which the pilot gets closed — before we switch it on

## Body
[what it is → how it works → with AI and without → where it breaks]

**WHAT IT IS**

One sentence with numbers in it, written down in advance, by which a few weeks later you can see whether to carry on or to close. The nearest familiar analogue is **the acceptance criterion in a statement of work**, translated out of functionality and into money. A number named after the result explains the result and tests nothing any more.

**HOW IT WORKS**

1. **What we are improving** — and from what value to what value.
2. **What must not get worse while we do it** — one or two guardrail quantities.
3. **On what volume and over what period** we are testing.
4. **Who looks, and how often.** The entry is made before switch-on, with a date and a name on it: otherwise there is nothing by which to check the order of events in time.

[Labelled sample entry on the right: "Having the model handle tickets will bring the cost of one ticket down **from ₽42 to ₽15**, customer satisfaction will not drop **below 4.2 out of 5**, and the share of tickets handed to a human will not exceed **20%**. We test on **5,000 tickets over 6 weeks**. Watched by **the head of support, once a week**."]

**WITH AI AND WITHOUT**

| WITHOUT AI — apparatus half a century old | WITH AI — only the rhythm changes |
|---|---|
| A business case with a payback threshold and a decision at a gate. **There is nothing to invent afresh**, you take what is already there. | Cost moves daily, a quarterly rhythm cannot keep up with it, and that is why they look weekly. Meanwhile companies measure activity instead of money — hence the gap: almost everyone invests, **6%** see a measurable return. |

[Gold callout — WHERE IT BREAKS]
**The starting value is measured on your own process.** A vendor's promise will not stand in for it: somebody else's number describes somebody else's process. **If "we close it" is not among the possible outcomes, this is not a criterion:** a threshold you cannot fail to clear grants no permission. And **numbers that appeared after switch-on are an explanation of the result,** which is why the date of the entry and the name on it are checked before the numbers themselves.

## Speaker notes

An abstract "keep an eye on the payback" is of no use. Let us make the requirement concrete.

What it is. One sentence with numbers in it, written down in advance, by which a few weeks later you can see whether to carry on or to close. The nearest analogue you already know is the acceptance criterion in a statement of work, translated out of functionality and into money. And the defining property: it is written before the pilot is switched on. A number named after the result explains the result and tests nothing any more.

What a filled-in one looks like. Having the model handle tickets will bring the cost of one ticket down from forty-two roubles to fifteen, while customer satisfaction will not drop below four point two out of five, and the share of tickets handed to a human will not exceed twenty percent; we test on five thousand tickets over six weeks; the head of support looks at it, once a week. Notice how many different things sit inside one sentence: what we are improving and from what value to what value, what must not get worse while we do it, on what volume and over what period, and who is looking.

What is actually done with this today. Without artificial intelligence it has been done for half a century: a business case with a payback threshold and a decision at a gate — the apparatus is ready-made, there is nothing to invent afresh. With models exactly one thing changes: the rhythm. Cost moves daily, a quarterly rhythm cannot keep up with it, and that is why they look weekly. In practice, though, something else happens: companies measure activity — hours saved, tasks automated — instead of money in the profit and loss statement. Hence the gap: almost everyone invests, and a measurable return is seen by six percent.

Three boundaries. The starting value is measured on your own process. A vendor's promise will not stand in for it: somebody else's number describes somebody else's process, and the difference between the two is the very thing the pilot was started for. If "we close it" is not among the possible outcomes, there is no criterion, there is an intention: a threshold you cannot fail to clear grants no permission. And numbers that appeared after switch-on are an explanation of the result, which is why the date of the entry and the name on it are checked before the numbers themselves.
