# Visible content

## Title bar
McDonald's and IBM, 2021–2024: killing a pilot on 0.7% of the chain was right

## Body

**WHAT HAPPENED**
From late 2021 McDonald's and IBM tested voice order-taking at the drive-thru window: the customer speaks the order straight to the system, past the employee. It ran at about 100 restaurants out of nearly 13,800 in the US — 0.7% of the chain — for two and a half years. All that time clips of failures kept surfacing: bacon in ice cream, nine iced teas instead of one, orders mixed between adjacent lanes. In June 2024 the partnership ended. What was killed was the approach; the goal stayed — voice automation is still planned.

[Diagram] A large "0.7%" captioned "≈100 of ≈13,800 US restaurants · 2.5-year pilot" — scale and length side by side.

**ANALYSIS**
- The denominator is mandatory: "100 restaurants" without "of 13,800" reads as a large rollout; with it, as a narrow strip deliberately kept narrow
- The ceiling here is plain: speech recognition in noise, across accents, with other voices around. More time does not move such a ceiling
- A specific technical path was killed; the task stayed. Different decisions, and mixing them is costly
- Mirror of the previous case: there the pilot was skipped, here it was run honestly and its signal was read

[Gold callout]
The signal was read right: a long pilot on a small share still failing in public means the approach has a ceiling; a data shortage is not the cause. That is the continue-or-kill gate working.

## Speaker notes

You could not call this a failure. Here the continue-or-kill gate fired — the payoff of this section's base material, and it is worth taking apart what made it work.

The partnership began at the end of twenty twenty-one and ran for two and a half to three years, growing to about a hundred restaurants. Let us name the denominator straight away, because without it the figure reads wrong: a hundred restaurants is about zero point seven percent of the chain's nearly fourteen thousand US locations. Without the denominator it sounds like a large-scale rollout; with the denominator it is a narrow strip, deliberately kept narrow. All that time public failures accumulated — bacon in ice cream, nine iced teas instead of one, orders mixed between adjacent lanes. In June twenty twenty-four the partnership ended. Notice precisely what was killed: the approach. The goal stayed — voice automation of order-taking is still planned; what was killed was one specific technical path to it.

Why this is a signal read correctly. A pilot two and a half years long, held below one percent of locations and still regularly producing public failures, speaks of a structural ceiling in the approach, and a shortage of data does not explain it. The ceiling here is plain: speech-recognition accuracy in a noisy environment, across accents, with other voices around. Extra time does not move a ceiling like that, and the team read this from the accumulated signal. Fatigue had nothing to do with the decision — exactly the distinction the threshold is written down in advance to protect.

Compare it with the previous case, where the rollout went straight to the whole audience with no pilot at all. Two mirror-image errors around one axis: one project did not cross the gate after a long honest pilot, the other skipped the pilot entirely. Both are worth keeping.

And the alternative that was not discussed here, although it would have kept most of the benefit: a human in the loop — the system proposes the recognised order and the cashier confirms it on screen before it goes to the kitchen. That closes exactly the failure mode which, over years, never converged to an acceptable level.
