## Visible slide text

*How many of the 70,000 clips made it into the cut is not disclosed. "A thousand-fold" cannot be derived from this — there is nothing to divide by.*

*Defensible ratio of generations to a usable result: Toys "R" Us, 2024 — hundreds of shots per spot, a few dozen in the cut.*

**What got cheaper wasn't execution. What got cheaper was the failed attempt — and there is still no one to do the choosing.**

## Speaker notes

The sixty years from the previous frame fit into one line: the intermediary operation
disappeared. Seven times in a row, from 1961 to 2019, the same thing happened — the link where a
human mechanically carried an idea onto a medium was removed. The profession stayed, volume
grew, and an argument about authorship came up fresh every time. In 2022, a different link
disappeared: the price of a failed trial. The difference is one of kind, not of degree, and it
checks out three ways.

The first way is speed. Avid took fourteen years to fully replace classic film editing. The share
of fully generated tracks in Deezer's daily uploads grew from ten percent in January 2025 to more
than half by that same autumn — over eighteen months. Two transitions of the same class, a speed
difference of an order of magnitude.

The second way is direction. Every point in the previous chain made cheaper the execution of a
decision already made: the drawing is drawn, it needs transferring; the film is shot, it needs
splicing. Denoising makes cheaper the moment before the decision, when it is still unknown what
the result will be. Previously this stage was where you economized, because every trial cost
money. Now it is where you spend, because a trial costs a fraction of a cent, while choosing
among many trials costs a person's attention.

The third way is where the money went. After microstock, money moved toward "expensive and
exactly this": the market split, and the upper segment survived. After 2022, money is moving
toward selection: in the measured Russian case, producing one usable illustration takes thirty
to a hundred generated images and about half an hour of viewing, and two-thirds of the result
still fails to meet the needed metric. This is a new line item that appears in nobody's price
list.

Now to the row itself. Coca-Cola's 2025 holiday campaign is one and the same product, counted in
the classic mode and with a model, both by the company's own leadership. The classic mode was
named by Manolo Arroyo: a crew of more than fifty people and a year's schedule. With a model:
seventy thousand generated clips, five specialists on generation, thirty days. And then the
number the row exists for: the campaign as a whole still employed roughly the same hundred
people. People did not disappear. What changed was the number of trials gone through before
settling on what went to air.

The caveat without which this row cannot be shown sits right on the frame, and it matters more
than the number itself. How many clips made it into the final cut is not disclosed by any
source. The going line "seventy thousand clips turned into thirty seconds" is a conclusion the
press drew, not a fact anyone published. Without a denominator, no multiple can be derived from
this row at all — there is simply nothing to divide by. The one defensible ratio of generations
to a usable result in the public record comes from a different case — Toys "R" Us, June 2024:
hundreds of shots for a one-minute spot, of which a few dozen made it into the cut. A second
anchor is the "Air Head" team's own estimate: three hundred to one, shot footage to what made it
into a minute and a half, against classic documentary work's tens-to-one and scripted
advertising's roughly ten-to-one.

This caveat does not weaken the row — it sharpens it. The row presents one kind of measured fact
— the number of people in the campaign stayed the same, what changed was the number of trials —
and that fact alone is the content of the evening, presented as a number. The habit of asking
"what's the denominator" is worth carrying away: it is what checks any claim of the form "a
hundred times cheaper," and this lecture will need it more than once.

The second row of this frame is empty right now, and that is not an oversight. The place is held
for testimony from practice — an account of what this looks like from inside the work, not from
a press release. Such testimony is first-class material, and it is stronger than half the
published sources, if presented honestly: as named testimony with an explicit caveat that this is
testimony, not a measured case with both sides counted. If the row never fills, the frame still
works without it: the first row carries the whole weight on its own.

The line at the bottom is the conclusion the evening will return to at the very end. What got
cheaper wasn't execution. What got cheaper was the failed attempt. And there is still no one to
do the choosing, and the rest of the lecture is about what that costs.
