## Visible slide text

**You pay for selection, rights, and discernment:** an illustration is only 2.16 times cheaper, against three orders of magnitude of falling execution price — selection stayed expensive.

## Speaker notes

The question the evening opened with sounds for the second and last time. Its first half was a
claim, and by this minute it is proven: getting an image, a minute of speech, an episode of a
series, or a 3D object has come to cost from three cents to a few thousand dollars where the
classic mode required from fifty dollars to one hundred thirty-seven thousand. The second half is
the actual question, and both of its words matter: what is scarce now, and in what units is it
paid for. The demand to name a unit was not there for style. The answer "what's scarce now is
creative vision" would be empty for exactly this reason: creative vision is not measured in
anything — it cannot be counted, put into a budget, or compared with what came before.

The answer is assembled at three addresses, and not one of the three is a metaphor.

Selection. An attempt came to cost a fraction of a cent, and the choosing stayed human and did not
get one kopeck cheaper. The cleanest measurement of this is an ad spot for an NBA final: one
person, over two or three days, made three hundred to four hundred generations and selected
fifteen usable cuts. The ratio of four hundred to fifteen is ninety-six percent waste, and that is
exactly where the win comes from. The cheapness comes from a devalued miss: the model rarely hits,
and that has stopped mattering. Next to it stands a global brand's holiday campaign: seventy
thousand generated clips, five specialists on generation, thirty days — and roughly one hundred
people on the campaign before the switch and roughly one hundred after. What changed was the
number of attempts; the headcount stayed the same, and people moved into reviewing, culling, and
finishing. A sobering measure comes from a Russian marketplace, where both prices sit side by side
in the same period and the same units: a generated illustration is cheaper than an ordinary one by
a factor of 2.16, a portrait by 2.13, a video by 2.04, an article by 1.5. Only twofold, while the
price of execution itself fell by three orders of magnitude. What left the price of an order was
the execution; the selection, the approval, and the accountability for the result stayed in it.

Rights. Over three years, the dispute over the provenance of training data stopped being a
dispute and became a line item. The largest sum in the history of American copyright law is one
and a half billion dollars, and without a base it means nothing. Here is the base. When the
settlement was announced, the figure quoted was roughly five hundred thousand works, and one and a
half billion divided by that number gives roughly three thousand dollars per work. The final list
turned out more precise and smaller — four hundred eighty-two thousand four hundred sixty works —
and the same division gives about three thousand one hundred. This is one and the same quantity
with two different denominators, not a payout before and after deductions: three thousand one
hundred is the gross distribution per recognized work, out of which attorneys' fees and
administration costs are still subtracted. The court cut the requested fee from one hundred
eighty-seven and a half million to one hundred one and a half million, that is, to six and
eight-tenths percent of the fund, and after that deduction each work will see less than three
thousand dollars, not more. A deduction lowers the figure — and if somewhere it raises yours, the
denominators have been mixed up. The payment was for storing a pirated library; the same judge,
a year earlier, called training on legally purchased books strikingly transformative. This is a
settlement without an admission of guilt: money was paid, no rule was made. In music the same path
was walked faster than any court — the major rights holders settled and came back with a license;
the agreed rate runs from two-tenths to half a cent per generation against twenty to one hundred
dollars for a finished stock track. A third party at the same address is the right of whoever's
voice ended up inside the data, and here, under Russian law, there is no separate protection for
voice as such.

Discernment. A signature, a voice, and a face stopped confirming themselves on their own, and
confirmation became a separate technical task that someone pays for. A detector trained on one
family of models gives ninety-nine and nine-tenths percent correct calls on that same family and
fifty-one and nine-tenths percent on a model it has never seen; the second figure is a coin flip.
The quietest part of this tally shows up in newsrooms: where the share of machine-written articles
crossed a fifth, the number of experts quoted per thousand words fell by twenty-two percent against
the start of the twenty-twenties, while in newsrooms with almost no machine-written articles it
rose by six. The price of discernment here is expressed in people who stopped being called for
comment.
