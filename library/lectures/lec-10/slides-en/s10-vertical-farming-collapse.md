# Vertical farming — a failure not caused by bad AI

## Assertion

AppHarvest, Plenty, Bowery: $1.37B+ in losses, 14 bankruptcies in 2025. The controllers worked, CV recognized, ML predicted. What failed was the arithmetic of energy: LED ≈ 100× free sunlight.

## Speaker notes

Let's move to the first big failure block. Vertical farming is an attempt to build a closed-loop AI-managed farm: LEDs instead of the sun, a nutrient solution instead of soil, climate control instead of weather, AI optimization of all parameters. The idea of 2018-2021 looked convincing: eliminate weather, seasonality, field pathogens; place production in cities. The result of 2022-2026 is a collapse of the category. Around three billion dollars of lost venture capital, no fewer than fourteen bankruptcies in 2025 alone worth about one billion three hundred seventy million dollars, minus 53 percent of funding for the novel farming systems segment year-over-year per the AgFunder Global AgriFoodTech Investment Report 2025 (Q1-Q3 2025 $1.5B versus $3.2B for 2024).

Three anchor cases. AppHarvest — the flagship of American indoor farming, at its peak valued at about a billion dollars via a SPAC deal in 2021; on the board — Martha Stewart, J.D. Vance, David Lee. Debt at the moment of bankruptcy — three hundred forty-one million against current assets of one hundred ten million. The cause — a combination of high Kentucky heating OPEX in the winter period and the tomato-mosaic virus, which, once it penetrated the flagship site in Morehead, spread through the entire greenhouse loop in days. This is precisely the "closed-loop ↑ blast radius" phenomenon: in an open field the same virus would spread across hectares over weeks; in a closed loop it covers the entire production capacity.

Plenty is the company we started the lecture with. Raised capital of nine hundred forty million from SoftBank, Walmart, Bezos Expeditions. Compton, California — four and a half million pounds of leafy greens per year as planned, opened May 2023, closed December 2024. Nineteen months. The direct cause in their own words: there is a large gap between what consumers are willing to pay for leafy greens and the cost of vertical farming. Energy in California ate the AI optimization.

Bowery Farming — at its 2021 peak valued at two point three billion dollars. Ceased operations on November fourth, 2024 via an ABC rather than Chapter 11. Raised capital of more than seven hundred million; the Locust Grove facility of two hundred thousand square feet — the largest vertical farm in history — went into liquidation with thirty-two million of new equipment that was never even started. This is an especially telling signal: capex first, AI after — there was nothing left to catch up with, because the business model failed before the AI infrastructure managed to get going.

The main idea of the slide: ML worked in every one of these cases. The climate controllers worked; CV recognized; yield models trained. What did not work is the arithmetic of energy: LED lighting on a closed floor consumes roughly one hundred times more energy than the same plant receives from free sunlight. And this is not "bad AI" — it's a law of thermodynamics that ML does not optimize. We'll move to this math on the next slide.
