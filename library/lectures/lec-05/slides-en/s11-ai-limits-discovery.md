# Visible content

## Title bar
A synthetic conversation partner cannot produce disagreement

## Body
[An "agreement mirror" icon + 3 criterion cards]

**When a live interview is strictly better than AI synthesis**
1. The cost of a go/no-go mistake is high
2. A real past is needed, not a plausible one
3. Validation by commitment is needed, not opinion

[Gold callout]
AI summaries lose 20–40% of interview detail (Torres) when the "individually first" step is skipped

## Speaker notes

The fifth risk of discovery is ethical in nature, but it materializes as a concrete, measurable mechanism: an AI summary can miss twenty to forty percent of important interview detail when synthesis skips the "individually first" step and jumps straight to a cross-interview theme. This isn't a vague "AI sometimes makes mistakes" — it's a failure traceable to a specific step.

Why live interviews remain necessary — an argument of accumulation. The Mom Test's second rule — ask about specific things from the past — is exactly the rule a synthetic persona cannot satisfy, because it has no real past, only the ability to generate something plausible. A tendency to agree plus the absence of a real past is a cumulative failure: not just skewed toward "yes," but skewed toward "yes" about an event that never happened.

Let's make this testable through three operational criteria. First: the cost of a go/no-go mistake is high — you need a source capable of saying "no," and a synthetic one is by construction incapable. Second: you need a real past, not a plausible one — if the hypothesis concerns specific past behavior, a synthetic persona invents it rather than remembers it. Third: you need validation by a real commitment — a pre-order, a deposit, an intro — there's no AI substitute here at all. What clearly stays from the classic: the three Mom Test rules apply the same way no matter who asks the question; Blank's hypothesis-first discipline is the antidote to a one-click AI tree. The rule in short: the closer the output is to an irreversible decision about real people, the more strictly a real person is required; the further away, the more appropriate AI becomes as a draft accelerator.
