## Speaker notes

What should an engineering student do who has read the material and wants to work in this field? Below is a market landscape, information about the industry's segmentation, not a prescriptive career recommendation. Career decisions are individual and depend on a specific skill profile, inclinations, and circumstances.

Educational trajectories. Specialized technical universities offer master's programs in agro-IT, agro-engineering, digital agronomy. Specialized agricultural universities — programs in digital farming and animal science with digital technologies. These 2024-2026 programs have begun integrating ML courses into their curricula, which distinguishes them from programs of a decade ago. At the continuing-education level — online courses in satellite analytics, computer vision, ML for time series — a good supplement to a basic engineering education.

The L1-L2 segment in the international context covers large AgTech vendors with precision-farming and autonomy infrastructure. Examples — John Deere in the See & Spray and autonomous direction, Bayer Crop Science Digital, BASF Digital Farming in the xarvio advisory direction. As well as independent startups in specialized niches — Carbon Robotics in robotic weed control, Saga Robotics in UV-C strawberry treatment, Aigen in solar field robots. The skill profile for entry — computer vision, edge ML, sensor fusion, ROS, autonomous navigation.

The L3 segment in the international context includes dairy-equipment manufacturers with an AI stack — DeLaval, GEA, Lely; startups in CV for monitoring animal health and behavior — Cainthus as part of the Cargill portfolio, Connecterra IDA, CattleEye within GEA; companies in poultry CV like Cargill Birdoo. Skills — CV plus classical ML methods for animal biometrics, time-series analysis of sensor data.

The L4-L5 segment in the international context covers large commodity traders with agentic AI stacks — Cargill CMAX and CarVe, ADM, COFCO. The data infrastructure layer — Tract as an example of shared infrastructure for competing companies. Retail-supply ML — Walmart Eden, Tesco AI, Cropin. Skills — LLM and agentic architectures, RAG, finance and commodity trading basics, supply chain economics.

The Russian market has a segmentation distributed across levels. At L2 are Cognitive Pilot, ITELMA and Geoscan — autonomous machinery, UAV. At the R&D level agroholdings operate: EFKO in FoodTech, Rusagro Tech in digital agronomy. At L4-L5 — RSHB.digital, Magnit digital, X5 Tech — supply chain ML, demand forecasting. At L1 SaaS — ExactFarming. At L3 in narrow niches — Connectome.ai, a Skolkovo resident in the field of CV for monitoring calf births. Additional sources — the ecosystems of corporate R&D units of large banks and tech companies.

This is a market landscape, not a prescription. Each of these positions requires a specific skill profile, which an engineer obtains from the course as a whole plus specific industry preparation. Career decisions are individual.
