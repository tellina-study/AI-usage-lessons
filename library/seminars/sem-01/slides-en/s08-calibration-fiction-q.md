# Who wrote it — fiction: AI or human?

## Assertion

Who wrote it — fiction: AI or human?

## Visual

Round-2: removed the "hand+camera" voting badge in the corner (hand-raise
voting was already explained on s05, repeating the badge on 11 following
slides is redundant) and the AI/human pill buttons under the cards (we vote by
raising hands, not by clicking — the buttons created a false sense of
interactivity). 2 Ocean rounded box cards in a row, each a short
philosophical-reflective text on sleep and reality, with no author label, text
vertically centered in the card. Text A: "How strange — he died, and we live
on… Except I suspect that every time we go to sleep, we die in exactly the
same way. And the sun goes away forever, and the whole of history ends. And
then non-being gets bored with itself, and we wake up. And the world arises
again." Text B: a short AI-generated text in a similar reflective style on a
related theme.

## Speaker notes

The first category in the calibration game — fiction. Two short fragments on the theme of sleep and reality, unlabeled. Let's vote: which one was written by a human, which by AI.

Both texts are deliberately similar in theme and tone — the point isn't to find an obvious difference in subject matter, but to hear a difference in style, if one is even noticeable in such a short fragment.

After the vote — ask one or two students what tipped them off: the rhythm of the sentence, characteristic turns of phrase, a recognizable voice? For fiction, there's no objective, measurable criterion for "which is better" — this category is about style and recognition, not about productivity.
