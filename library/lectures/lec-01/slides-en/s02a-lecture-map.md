## Speaker notes

Before we go further — a quick navigation note. The lecture consists of five sections, which sequentially answer five different questions.

Section 0, which is where we are now, is the opening: a short demonstration, the instructor's introduction, and the central question of the course.

Section 1 is what AI is and why there are so many definitions. Here we will also walk through the seventy-year history of the discipline, including the prehistory before the term itself appeared, and pin down the key turning point of 2017.

Section 2 is where we are now, in 2022–2026. The figures for AI adoption in the world and in Russia, and an overview of breakthroughs from non-first-mover players.

Section 3 is the largest section of the lecture. Four ways to implement AI systems: model, chat, agent, application. We'll go through each one with a diagram and an example, and at the end of the section we'll get a practical tool for choosing the type of AI.

Section 4 is boundaries and safety. Where the data goes, what hallucinations are, three manifestations of a model fitting to the data, and the conceptual boundaries of AI as a class of technologies.

Section 5 is the conclusion, the semester map, and the preview of Lecture 2.
