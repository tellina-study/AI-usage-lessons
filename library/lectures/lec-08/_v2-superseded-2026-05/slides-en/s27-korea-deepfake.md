## Speaker notes

The seventh case — the Korea schoolgirl deepfake crisis. August 2024, South Korea. I want to note up front — this is sensitive content and I will speak about it carefully. The context. In August 2024 in South Korea a mass scandal with deepfake porn occurred. An investigation uncovered more than two hundred Telegram chats where schoolchildren and students created and exchanged deepfake porn made from selfie photos of female classmates and teachers. Between January and July 2024, six thousand five hundred takedown requests were carried out — four times more than for all of 2023. Seventy-four percent of suspects — teenagers aged ten-to-nineteen. Between 2021 and July 2024, seven hundred ninety-three cases were officially reported to law enforcement, but only sixteen cases reached prosecution. That is, the enforcement rate is under two percent. NPR and the Daily Star published detailed coverage. What this epidemic means structurally. We have two factors: first — a widely accessible AI capability for generating realistic synthetic content; second — a weak legal enforcement infrastructure for regulating this capability. The result — mass class harm to minors. This case became one of the triggers of EU initiatives to criminalize deepfake porn (entry into force by mid-2027). Lesson for the engineer: an accessible capability plus weak enforcement equals mass class harm. For consumer AI tools, a safety layer is mandatory before launch. NSFW-content detection, age verification, a complaint-handling process for abuse — these are not nice-to-haves, they are must-haves for consumer products with a generative component. If you launch a product that can generate the likeness of a real person from an uploaded photo — you have an obligation to build this protective infrastructure before launch, not after the first incident. This is not just a moral obligation — after EU 2027 and analogous laws in other jurisdictions, it will be a legal obligation with real fines and criminal liability for founders.
