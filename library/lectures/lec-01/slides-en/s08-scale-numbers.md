# AI became infrastructure in 3 years: 900 million users, 51% of developers daily, 46% of code from Copilot

## Assertion

AI became infrastructure in 3 years: 900M WAU, 51% of developers daily, 46% of Copilot code.

## Visual

A grid of four large metrics in Ocean rounded boxes with icons: 900M WAU (ChatGPT, February 2026), 51% professional daily (Stack Overflow 2025), 46% Copilot code (GitHub Octoverse 2025), $390.9B→$539.5B AI market 2025→2026 (Grand View Research 2026; methodological caveat in the caption — under Statista's narrow software-only definition the estimates are lower, around $244–260B). At the bottom, a gold band with a counter-fact: «90% of AI pilots in Russia never reach production — CNews / Vedomosti / Intellectual Analytics, March 2026».

## Speaker notes

By twenty-six, AI stopped being a separate product and became an infrastructure layer on which other products run. A few telling figures.

As of February 2026, ChatGPT has about nine hundred million weekly active users. That's weekly active users, not monthly: the monthly-activity figures are considerably higher. GitHub Copilot has more than twenty million users; among those who use it regularly, up to forty-six percent of the code is written by AI, and for the Java language — sixty-one percent.

The Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2025, a sample of forty-nine thousand-plus respondents from one hundred seventy-seven countries, shows: eighty-four percent of developers use or plan to use AI tools in their work. Fifty-one percent of professional developers work with AI daily. And at the same time forty-six percent do not trust the accuracy of AI answers to questions about code — up from thirty-one percent a year earlier.

An important caveat about trust. These two figures — fifty-one percent daily users and forty-six percent distrustful — at first glance seem contradictory. In fact they are consistent: the overall trend is rising adoption, while the pointwise assessment of code accuracy has become more critical, because developers have accumulated experience working with AI and have learned to see where it makes mistakes. This isn't «trust is falling», it's «trust is becoming more nuanced». A discipline of verification is the desirable behavior.

The market size — three hundred ninety point nine billion dollars in 2025, with a forecast of growth to five hundred thirty-nine and a half billion in 2026 (Grand View Research, 2026). By other methodologies — for example, Statista's narrow software-only definition — the estimates for 2025 are lower, around $244–260 billion. The spread reflects what to count as the AI market: sales of models, infrastructure, embedded AI, integration services.

And in parallel — the counter-fact. According to aggregated data from business publications, about ninety percent of AI pilots in Russia never reach full-fledged production deployment. The source of the estimate is a study by the consulting firm Intellectual Analytics (December 2025 – February 2026, published by Vedomosti in March), a sample of the fifty largest organizations in IT, industry, finance, the public sector, and transport/logistics. This is not a representative sample across the whole economy; it's a slice of the large players, where AI pilots are best documented. Even so, the combination of mass adoption and mass failure forms the central engineering problem of the course.
