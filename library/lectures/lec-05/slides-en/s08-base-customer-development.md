## Speaker notes

Customer Development is Steve Blank's methodology. The central idea is extremely concrete: there are no facts inside the building, so go outside. A startup is not a scaled-down copy of a large company executing a known plan — it's a temporary organization in search of a repeatable business model under extreme uncertainty. The plan can't be written in advance, because the plan depends on answers only customers know.

The method consists of four sequential steps, each ending in an explicit decision to continue or pivot. Discovery — turn the initial hypotheses into facts by going out to real people; this step is explicitly hypothesis-oriented — you write down what you believe before you walk out the door. Validation — check whether this can be sold not just to the first enthusiasts but to a wider circle. Creation — drive demand at scale. Building — transition the organization into formal functional departments.

It's important to distinguish Customer Development from Build-Measure-Learn, which we discussed in the keystone: BML is the engine, Customer Development is the map of the terrain the engine drives across. First understand what you're testing and with whom, then how fast to spin the test cycle. And here we also introduce a device we'll reuse in every following section: the falsifiable hypothesis — a written statement "we believe that X, we'll test it via Y, by date Z we'll have a yes-or-no answer." The format works because it turns vague confidence into a testable commitment with a date. A known limitation of the method: it's written for startups starting from zero, searching for a business model, not for refining a feature inside a mature product — applying the whole ceremony to a minor feature tweak is excessive, exactly as you wouldn't run an A/B test to fix a typo.
