## Speaker notes

Next we enter the first rung of the ladder — L1 «Field». This is the lowest and hardest rung for AI: an open biological environment with maximal unpredictability. Sun, rain, pathogens, the individual peculiarities of a plot — everything that is uncontrollable in an engineering sense. And it's precisely here that the band of successful AI application is the narrowest.

In this section we'll examine, first, the canonical success case of L1 — John Deere See & Spray Ultimate. What works in it and why. Second, the L1 vendor matrix — five of the largest platforms with different operating modes and an important anti-hype caveat: brand is not the same as mode. Third, the foundation models of 2026 — TerraMind, Prithvi-EO 2.0 — and the vendor-concentration risks tied to them.

Then three failures that set the boundaries. The first — the collapse of the vertical-farming category to the tune of more than one point three seven billion dollars; we'll examine it through a five-why analysis and the thermodynamic gap in the cost of LEDs versus free sunlight. The second — the hallucinations of a generic LLM in the role of an agronomist; the controlled experiment by Tzachor et al. in Nature Food (November 2023, press coverage 2024-05). The third — Plantix's accuracy and why a ten-to-fifteen percent misdiagnosis rate at the scale of ten million downloads is a structural systemic risk.

At the end of the section — two anti-AI criteria: the first about the law of thermodynamics mattering more than ML, the second about the generic LLM as a categorical anti-pattern for advisory. And the Russian parallel — the main vivid lesson of L1 political risk, which we'll return to in Section 4-bis.
