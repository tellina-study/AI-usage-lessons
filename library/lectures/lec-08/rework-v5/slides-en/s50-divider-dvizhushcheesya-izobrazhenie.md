## Visible slide text

**Section 3. Moving Image**

The same mechanism as the still image, and two orders of magnitude more expensive: five seconds of high-definition video — **249,240 tokens** against **4,096** for a 1024×1024 image. That is why industrial scale is visible here with the naked eye.

**Three breakdowns in order:** Chinese micro-dramas · Kalshi, NBA Finals, June 2025 · Coca-Cola's "Holidays Are Coming."

**Six mentions, each with a number.**

## Speaker notes

The third modality of the evening is built more intricately than the previous two: the production chain here is longer, there are more stages, and the unit of output is more expensive than both sound and the still image — and measurably so.

A 1024-by-1024-pixel image is 4,096 tokens. Five seconds of 720p video is 111,600 tokens — twenty-seven times more. Five seconds in 1080p is 249,240 tokens — sixty-one times more. And these tokens have to be run through twenty to fifty denoising steps, each with full attention and no cache at all. This is the most expensive of the three modalities covered so far, and that is exactly why the levers of cost reduction are most clearly visible here.

The section has a feature the previous two don't: an excess of material. All the Western cases are one-offs — one ad, one film, one scene — while the first breakdown covers a whole industry that switched to generative production as its primary method in a single year, with a full accounting of the economics. Such a combination is rare, and the order of the breakdowns is chosen so that scale moves from the industry level to the individual: first the market, then a two-thousand-dollar ad, then a campaign where the room's expectation diverges from the result.

The section's three breakdowns are named here in order and match their own slide titles word for word. Six mentions follow, each with a number and a baseline.
