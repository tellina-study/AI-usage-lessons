## Speaker notes

This is the introductory lecture of the course "AI across industries." We will build an overall map: what AI is as a class of technologies, what state it is in as of 2026, what ways of implementing AI systems exist in engineering practice, and where these systems have their boundaries.

By 2026 AI has ceased to be a separate product and has become an infrastructure layer that invisibly runs in dozens of services you use every day. Voice input on your smartphone, traffic-aware navigation, face unlock, the spam filter in your email, recommendations in a music service, autocomplete in the browser — all of this is AI, which most users have stopped perceiving as AI.

The goal of the lecture is not to introduce you to every possible AI tool (that is technically impossible, the market changes faster than textbooks come out), but to give you a working vocabulary: how a model differs from a chat, how a chat differs from an agent, and how to figure out in thirty seconds which type of AI fits a specific task. By the end of the lecture you will have a simple tool — two diagnostic questions plus a quadrant — which we will keep applying in all the other lectures of the course.
