# AGI forecasts — 4 speakers, 4 different incentives

## Assertion

AGI forecasts — 4 speakers, 4 different incentives.

## Speaker notes

All existing production AI systems are narrow AI: they're optimized for specific tasks or narrow domains. AGI — Artificial General Intelligence — is a hypothetical AI with a human level of cognitive abilities across a broad range of areas. This is an open question that's actively debated in the public arena with mutually exclusive forecasts.

When you see a specific AGI forecast, ask what material stake the speaker has in that forecast. The higher the confidence in imminent AGI, the more often you need to check who wants what from the market after such a statement. This doesn't mean the speakers are insincere — it means AGI forecasts are made in a public arena in which every leader is simultaneously building a company and shaping investors' expectations.

A rundown of the four public figures. Sam Altman, CEO of OpenAI: "we know how to build AGI; the beginning of the transition to superintelligence", January 2026. The material stake — hundred-billion-dollar funding rounds, IPO context; high AGI confidence supports the company's valuation.

Dario Amodei, CEO of Anthropic: "AGI in 2-3 years; AI will replace software developers within a year; Nobel level in two years", Davos 2026. The material stake — competitive pressure from OpenAI; his own 2026 funding round.

Demis Hassabis, CEO of Google DeepMind: "AGI by 2029–2030, three-to-four years; the window narrowed over 2026", Axios and Google I/O, May 2026 — the theme developed in the essay "A Framework for Frontier AI" from July 14, 2026. The material stake — a leader in the research community; more trust with a cautious stance; Google doesn't need AGI hype for its core business.

Yann LeCun, founder of AMI Labs, ex-Chief AI Scientist of Meta: "the LLM paradigm won't lead to AGI; a new architecture is needed, world models, JEPA", consistently. The material stake — a round of about a billion dollars in March 2026 on an alternative path; a counter-consensus stance is advantageous for differentiating a startup.

Notice the pattern — not one of the four speakers holds a neutrally scientific stance. Every forecast reflects either a commercial interest or market positioning. A useful exercise when reading any public interview of a top manager of an AI company: to ask yourself the question — what decision would they want the market to make on the basis of this statement.
