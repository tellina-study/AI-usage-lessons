# The idea of a neural network is 13 years older than the term «artificial intelligence» itself

## Assertion

The idea of a neural network is 13 years older than the term «artificial intelligence» itself.

## Visual

Between them a gold arrow/bracket labeled «13 years».

On the right side — a photo sidebar (it does not turn the slide into an overloaded one, a compact column ~2" wide): a real 1959 photo (Wikimedia Commons, file «Lettvin Pitts.jpg», CC BY-SA 3.0) — Walter Pitts (right) with his colleague Jerome Lettvin demonstrating a frog experiment at MIT. This is the only genuinely available photo of Pitts with a clean license; no solo portrait from 1943 exists in the public domain.

At the bottom, a short takeaway phrase: «The connectionist idea is older than the term AI itself».

## Speaker notes

Even before the term «artificial intelligence» itself appeared, the neurophysiologist Warren McCulloch and the logician Walter Pitts published a paper formalizing the neuron as a logic element: a network of such simple binary elements is theoretically capable of computing any function expressible in terms of logic. The paper came out in 1943 — thirteen years earlier than the 1956 Dartmouth conference, where the term «AI» would first appear.

The McCulloch–Pitts formal neuron was not an engineering system in the modern sense and did not solve applied problems, but it anticipated the connectionist tradition — the line of thought from which, decades later, artificial neural networks and, ultimately, architectures like the Transformer would grow.

It's useful to keep this date in mind as a reminder: the idea of a «neural network» at the theoretical level is older than the term «artificial intelligence» itself. Next we'll look at the history starting from 1956 — from the moment the term officially appeared.
