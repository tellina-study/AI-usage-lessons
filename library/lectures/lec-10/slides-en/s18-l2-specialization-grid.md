## Speaker notes

Beyond the LaserWeeder, four specialized platforms operate in L2 in 2026 — each in its own narrow niche.

Solinftec Solix — an autonomous field robot from the Brazilian company Solinftec, deployed in the US with two-hundred-forty-three-percent year-over-year expansion in 2025. As of the end of 2025 — more than one hundred robots in Illinois, Indiana, Kansas, Iowa, Wisconsin, Texas. Vendor self-report — up to ninety-eight percent reduction in herbicide volume. This number is from the company, not an independent validation; it should be treated as a marketing caveat. Twenty-four-seven operation on solar power plus self-refilling spray.

Saga Robotics Thorvald — a robot for UV-C night treatment of strawberries against powdery mildew. And here is a critically important caveat that marketing often omits: Saga Robotics covers about twenty percent of the tabletop-strawberry market in the UK — but this is UV treatment at night, not harvesting. Strawberry picking is still manual. Per the company's data, eight point four million pounds raised in 2024-25, one hundred fifty-plus robot units deployed, ninety-seven percent uptime, more than two hundred thousand autonomous kilometers, the goal being thirty percent of the UK market by 2026. This is an exemplary niche win: one task — UV-C at night, one crop — tabletop strawberry, one geography — the UK, a well-measurable result — reduced fungicide use.

Tevel Aerobotics — flying apple pickers. A narrow orchard-crop niche, where the fruit stays on the tree after ripening and requires manual removal. The technology — tethered drones with robotic arms picking individual fruits. Not an "autonomous orchard"; a concrete replacement for the manual picking of individual fruits in a situation where H-2A — agricultural-worker visas in the US — is becoming more expensive and less predictable. Apple picking is a simpler task than strawberry picking, because apples hang higher and are easier to grasp — more on this in the strawberry-economics case.

AGCO PTx Trimble Outrun — a retrofit autonomy mode for a mixed equipment fleet: Fendt, Massey Ferguson, John Deere, CNH. An alternative to the "closed" John Deere AutoTrac system. A demonstration at AGCO Tech Day 2025; the goal — a full autonomous crop cycle by 2030. This is a typical "emerging" segment: not yet in production deployment, but the retrofit-vs-closed-system architectural choice is fundamentally important — more on this in Section 4-bis on vendor lock-in.

The common pattern of all four working stories is specialized niches with one clearly defined task and measurable ROI. None of them claims to be a "universal farm robot". This contradicts the marketing of 2018-2022, when the main investment thesis in AgTech robotics was precisely a "universal assistant for the farm". On the second rung the universal mode doesn't work; the specialization mode works. The explanation of this pattern lies in the failures we'll examine on the following slides.
