## Speaker notes

The Russian layer of the first rung is structured differently from the American Corn Belt, and understanding this asymmetry is part of the engineering skill.

The three key working solutions are ExactFarming, AgroSignal, and Progress Agro group. ExactFarming — a Russian SaaS for farm management, serving twelve thousand seven hundred farms and nine point eight million hectares as of 2024. AgroSignal — monitoring of fuel consumption and equipment movement, without AI capabilities in the proper sense of the word. This is an example of automation, not AI, and an important example of how digitalization in the industry does not always mean machine learning. Progress Agro group in Krasnodar Krai deployed variable-rate nitrogen application on two thousand eight hundred hectares with plus five percent wheat profitability. This is an internal company measurement, not peer-reviewed, but a public metric with a concrete scale.

Russia's agricultural digitalization index — twenty-seven point two out of one hundred versus seventy-five point five for the USA. This is a structural gap, not a matter of propaganda. It's explained by three factors: the small size of farms outside agroholdings, cheap labor relative to the USA, and sanction restrictions after 2022 that reinforced dependence on a limited set of domestic or gray-imported solutions.

And most important — the main vivid lesson of political risk on the first rung. Climate FieldView left Russia in 2022 together with the exit of Bayer Crop Science from the Russian market. Russian agroholdings that had invested in FieldView in 2018-2021 lost access to the platform with its dashboards, recommendations, and satellite analytics all at once. This is AP-six — the vendor lock-in trap. Every AI platform is a vendor control surface, and a subscription does not make the farmer the owner of the data and decisions. We'll return to this lesson in Section 4-bis, where we'll examine the double optic of vendor lock-in through the Melitopol remote-brick and FTC v. Deere.

The lesson for an engineer not tied to the Russian context: this same class of risk applies to any farm in any peripheral country dependent on a cloud-AI vendor from another jurisdiction. The Russian case is a natural experiment illustrating what happens when an imported AI stack becomes unavailable.
