# Speed: days → seconds

## Assertion

Speed collapse: concept art days → seconds; dubbing weeks → minutes. The iteration cycle becomes 10-100× denser.

## Speaker notes

The cost collapse is accompanied by a speed collapse. This is a second economic factor, separate from cost. A concept art draft — for game-dev, a film, an ad — traditionally takes days from a freelance designer or an in-house concept artist. Via AI — five-to-sixty seconds prompt-to-pixel in Midjourney, Imagen 4, Flux 2. A B-roll frame — filler for documentary or marketing video — traditionally takes hours of shooting plus post-production, or ten-to-a-hundred dollars per minute via stock footage providers. Via AI — five-to-sixty seconds via Veo 3.1 or Sora 2. Dubbing long-form video into a target language — traditionally weeks of studio work. Via AI on ElevenLabs Dubbing Studio — minutes. Concept exploration in design — three-to-five variants of a main idea for a client presentation — traditionally half a week of design. Via AI — minutes for dozens of variants. The engineering lesson. The speed collapse changes cycle time at every stage of the creative pipeline. Earlier the client saw the first concept in a week; now — in minutes. This is not just savings — it is a change of the work pattern. The iteration cycle becomes ten-to-a-hundred times denser. This, in turn, requires new skills from the human in the loop: the ability to formulate "pass/fail" criteria faster, because more new variants appear. If earlier one concept was discussed for half a day, now in half a day one can review fifty concepts and narrow the pool to three. This is a different mode of work — not "choose one from the finished set" but "quickly filter out the unsuitable."
