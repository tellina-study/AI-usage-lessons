## Visible slide text

# 1. Autonomous robots in space

**The machine decides and acts on the spot — with no dispatcher at every step.**

In order:

**AEGIS** — autonomous selection of scientific targets on Mars
**SPOC** — deep learning for labeling Martian soil
**COROB-X** — three robots in a lava tube
**LeoLabs** — radar prioritization and collision warnings

## Speaker notes

This section opens the chain of four types of AI contribution promised by the map of the evening, and holds the lecture's maximum autonomy: later on we'll show a lesser degree of autonomy (Section 2) or shift sideways to other kinds of task (Sections 3–4).

Four case studies in order: AEGIS — a rover decides for itself what to photograph, raising target-selection accuracy from 24% to 93%. SPOC — a deep network labels soil type in an image with 98% accuracy, already helping rover drivers today, and potentially the rover itself in the future. COROB-X — three robots build a map of a lunar lava tube on their own, somewhere too dangerous for a human to descend. LeoLabs — a commercial radar network decides on its own which object from a growing catalog of orbital objects to observe next, and issues up to 400% more close-approach warnings.

Before we get to these four cases, the section first names the stage as a whole — what kind of work this is, what data gets captured, how a decision gets made without automation — and separately breaks down where a classical, deterministic algorithm manages on its own, and where a trained model is needed. This is the section where the line between automation and learning is the most visible on the surface of any of the lecture's four sections: geometry goes to the classical approach, recognizing an object against noise goes to the model.
