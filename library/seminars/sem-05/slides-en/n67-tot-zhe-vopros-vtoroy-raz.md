# The same question — a second time

## Assertion

The same opening question and the same five cards — a second time in the session; the room answers again, and it is said out loud whose answer changed and what exactly changed it.

## Visual

> This question was already asked at the start of the session; the answer was not voiced then.
> In this repository — with its submission form, its tests and its twenty-four lines of instructions — which of the five would you set up first? Whose answer changed — and what changed it?

`hook` · `skill` · `MCP` · `subagent` · `process`

## Speaker notes

An hour ago this question was already asked, and you kept your answer to yourself. The cards are the same five and in the same order: not one of them became the right one over the hour.

The question is the same, character for character, and there are as many cards. Only one thing changed over this hour: under two of the five there is now a file you can open.

Hands first: whose answer changed. Then two or three voices — what exactly changed it. I do not name my own answer and do not judge anyone else's.

---

**Reference.**

*What usually changes the answer, and what to say to each.* Most often people move **away from the hook** — after the barrier showed them, in front of them, how many forms of the command it stays silent on. That movement is honest, and I confirm it: the mechanism closes one path, the class stays open. The second common one is people coming **to the process**: if a mechanism has to be checked as well, you need an order with the check written into it. I accept that too. The third is staying **on the hook** with the caveat "but with a narrow matcher"; that is the conclusion the stage was leading to, and I say it out loud.

*If the answer shifted to the skill.* I ask which signal they see at home. If the instruction file has grown too large, the signal is real, and that is the right answer for their project.

*If the room stays silent.* I remind them what appeared over the hour: two files in the repository and two filled-in rows. I prompt no further: a pause does the work here.

*If "so which answer is right" comes up.* I answer that there is none today either: the choice depends on which signal has already sounded in the project. The full breakdown of all five comes at the end of the next session, when the remaining three are closed.

*What I do not do here.* I do not sum up — that is on the next slide. I do not ask anyone to do anything after the session. I do not judge whose answer "grew".
