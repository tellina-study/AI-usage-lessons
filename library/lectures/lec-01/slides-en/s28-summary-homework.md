## Speaker notes

Lecture 1 covered four content topics and arrived at a single practical tool. If there's one thing worth taking away from the lecture, it's the main question: where AI works, where it doesn't, and how to tell.

Three main takeaways.

First. AI is a spectrum of technologies, not a monolith. A specific AI tool has coordinates along two axes — task type and modality — and belongs to one of four implementation types: model, chat, agent, application. A competent discussion of AI in an engineering context starts with explicit classification.

Second. Choosing the type of AI is an engineering skill, and you have a tool for it. Two diagnostic questions and a 2×2 quadrant — simple, but systematically violated. Most of the corporate pilots that were rolled back never asked these questions before deployment; fashion and convenience picked the type of tool instead of an engineering analysis of the task.

Third. AI amplifies the human, but goal-setting, critical judgment, and responsibility remain ours. Every category of error we broke down — hallucinations, bias, sycophancy, distribution shift, data leaks — requires a human verification loop.

That is exactly the main question of the lecture, spelled out in full: where AI works, where it doesn't, and how to tell.
