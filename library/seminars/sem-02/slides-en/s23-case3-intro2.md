# Meeting minutes

## Assertion

"It came up at the standup today: what did we decide about vendor X back in May?"

## Visual

A quote card with a line from a month into using the system. Next to it — a
growing archive of minutes (a stack-of-documents icon). On the right — the
question: "Does this change the architecture?"

## Visual — quote

"This happened at the standup today, kind of awkward to retell. We were
discussing the quarterly budget, and someone from finance asks: 'Wait,
what did we even decide about vendor X back in May, weren't there some
agreements about a discount?' Dead silence. The minutes pile up in a
shared folder on the drive, named more or less by date, and searching
through them by hand is quite the experience, especially if all you
remember is the topic and not the date. The folder itself is growing by
about half a gigabyte a quarter. By the way, they also discussed the
summer vacation schedule at that same meeting, and separately, the
redesign of the corporate portal, but that's a different story."

## Speaker notes

Read the line out in full, without leaning on the key part with your
intonation. The direct signal is dissolved in the story about the
standup: minutes pile up in a shared folder, and the question "what did we
decide about vendor X in May" gets searched by topic and rough date, not
by an exact document — this is no longer one meeting, it's a lot of
accumulated minutes. The indirect signal is worth spelling out explicitly:
the rate at which the folder is growing (half a gigabyte a quarter) isn't
just "the archive is already big" — it's evidence that the base will keep
growing, so the solution needs to be designed for a growing volume, not
the current one. The vacation schedule and the portal redesign are noise
twins — both came up in that same line, but neither has anything to do
with searching the minutes. Question to the room: does this change the
architecture? What exactly changed compared to what we were discussing a
minute ago? Give yourselves a second to match this new request against
the solution you proposed for a single meeting. Who wants to go first? And
a separate question for those of you who a minute ago were arguing for a
single model call: who's changed their mind now — and why exactly did this
one detail make you reconsider your answer? When you break it down, ask:
which facts in the line affected your choice, and which didn't?
