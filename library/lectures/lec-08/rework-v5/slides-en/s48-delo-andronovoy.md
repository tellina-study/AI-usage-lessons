## Speaker notes

This is the only Russian case on a synthesized voice to reach a ruling, and it can be traced from start to finish — which is why it is worth examining directly, rather than retelling rumors about a dozen similar situations.

In 2019, voice actress Alyona Andronova recorded about 600 pages of text for a bank. By her account, the contract contains no word "synthesis" and no mention of neural networks; she was told the recordings were needed for internal purposes — a call center and a voice assistant. This is the load-bearing detail of the entire case, and it has to be kept exact: this is about a use that the document made no provision for at all; there was also no prohibition there that could have been violated.

In 2022, her voice turned up in third-party advertising — erotic-chat ads, calls to donate; the voice proved available for synthesis on third-party platforms. Because of this she lost part of her work: clients who recognized the familiar timbre in that context stopped hiring her.

On April 7, 2023, the bank offered compensation of about two months' pay in response, on condition of a full waiver of claims. The offer was declined. On August 30, 2023, a lawsuit was filed with the Savyolovsky District Court of Moscow: nearly ₽6 million, removal of the voice from public availability, and protection of honor and dignity. The same day, a petition was launched that gathered about 26,000 signatures.

On August 12, 2024, a ruling was issued in Case No. 2-3580/2024: the claim was denied. The court held that the exclusive rights to the recordings, including the right to process them with a neural network, including by third parties, had passed under the contract and that the necessary consents had been obtained. The court bypassed the question of protecting voice as an intangible good, declining to rule on it — meaning the question the case was actually interesting for went unresolved not because it was answered in the negative, but because it was never reached. The ruling was appealed; the outcome of the appeal could not be established from open sources, and that state has to be named as it is, not guessed at.

This has to be read as follows. The court resolved the dispute as a contract dispute. In Russia, a voice exists in court exactly to the extent it is described on paper signed five years earlier — at a time when the word "synthesis" did not yet exist in this industry. The gap that lost the case, as of October 2026, is not closed: the bill that would have closed it was submitted a month after the ruling and was not supported by the government.

The outcome is stated directly, and this matters for the form of the breakdown: a breakdown with no outcome is half a breakdown. The temptation to tell this as a story of injustice and stop at the lawsuit is strong, and that is indeed how it is most often retold. But a filed lawsuit, on its own, proves nothing; the ruling proves something, and here the ruling is negative.

For an engineer, this yields a practical rule, and it is broader than the topic of voice: a contract for recording material that may be used to train a model has to name that use in words. The absence of the word "synthesis" in the 2019 document cost a person their profession in its former form, and a ₽6 million lawsuit. This applies to any material a person hands over to an organization: voice recordings, photographs, text, code, labeling. The question to ask when signing is this: is the use I do not expect named in the document, in words.

And the other side of the same rule, addressed to whoever drafts the contract. The wording "including the right to process, including by third parties" looked, in 2019, like a standard boilerplate clause, and it is exactly what decided the case five years later. A broad, general formula in a contract looks like it is providing for flexibility, and it actually shifts the entire future risk onto whoever signs it. Good-faith practice is to name uses explicitly rather than hide them under a general formula — and the industry asking for a license instead of a ban is asking for exactly that.
