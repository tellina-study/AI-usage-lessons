## Speaker notes

L5 is the highest rung of the ladder and the most mature layer of agriculture-AI. This is the level of consumer retail, where a fully digitized environment gives AI models ideal conditions: every product SKU has a digital footprint, every transaction is logged in real time, every decision — procurement, pricing, shelf placement — has direct and measurable feedback in hours to days.

The canonical L5 success cases of 2026. Walmart's Eden ML algorithm — a system for assessing the quality of fresh produce on inbound shipments and predicting shelf life. It's been in production since 2017. This is inference on images of inbound pallets plus an ML forecast of spoilage speed. Per Walmart's claims, using Eden plus Cropin's yield forecast leads to reduced food waste; the exact "minus twenty percent" figure slips into review materials, but this is a vendor self-report, not a peer-reviewed metric.

Tesco AI demand forecast — the British retailer uses ML for demand forecasting since 2017. The claimed effect — minus thirty percent food waste since 2017. This is public reporting in Tesco's annual reports.

X5 «Perekrestok» — the Russian retailer deployed an ML demand-forecasting system since 2020. It uses more than two hundred factors: historical demand, weather, day of week, local events, competitor prices, promotions, warehouse stock. The categories — produce and dairy. A fully in-house ML team. Per X5 Tech data, the forecast accuracy is world-class. This is the very parity with world leaders we discussed in Section 4: X5 in production for six years, Tesco and Walmart for nine years, a gap of about three years that keeps narrowing.

Magnit F&R — the fourth canonical L5 success case, and for it it's important to distinguish two modules. The Forecasting module is deployed in production at 46 distribution centers since January 2026 — this is the network level and de facto parity with world leaders in this narrow task (Habr Magnit 2026). The Replenishment module — a pilot at 3 DCs with a plan to scale to 10-20 by early 2027. This is a hybrid status: half of the F&R stack at world level, the second half in the pilot phase. The correct formulation is a modular split, not a blanket "Magnit at world level" and not "Magnit at pilot".

And this level works reliably for world leaders, for X5, and in Magnit's Forecasting module. It's the only level where "AI everywhere" is not hype but production reality for established players. But — and this is a critical anti-hype caveat — most of this layer is not specific to agriculture, but represents general retail-supply ML. ML demand forecasting in grocery retail is the same ML demand forecasting as in fashion retail or in FMCG non-food. And this is an important observation: L5 successes don't prove "AI readiness of the whole agriculture chain" — they prove the maturity of retail-AI as a separate discipline.

Why is this rung in an agriculture-AI course? Three answers. First — it shows "mature ML": what happens when the environment is fully digitized and feedback arrives in seconds to minutes; it's a contrasting backdrop for understanding why L1-L2 work differently. Second — L5 success doesn't prove agriculture readiness; critical for anti-hype: the "AI revolution in agriculture" usually relies on L5 metrics that pertain to retail-AI, not field tasks. Third — L5 descends into L1 via scope-3 visibility: Walmart Eden plus Cropin plus Walmart-supplier farms form a cross-level pattern where an L5 player penetrates into suppliers' L1 data. This is the place where L4-L5 architectures reach beyond their domain.
