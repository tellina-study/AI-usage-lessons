## Speaker notes

A real example from Russia. Since March 2024 Sberbank has made credit decisions on individuals almost entirely with AI; the scoring model takes into account up to five thousand parameters of the client; the bank estimated the additional effect from applying AI across all lines of business at roughly three hundred and fifty billion rubles for 2023. Per Bank of Russia materials, the degree of AI autonomy in scoring individuals and small businesses at systemically important banks is approaching one hundred percent, with issuance and review largely automated. An important detail for the through-line theme of «human or AI»: per the same document, over eighty percent of financial institutions that consistently apply AI give the client the ability to decline AI processing and switch to an employee. This is not cosmetics but an engineering-and-ethical principle: automating a decision must not close off the human channel.

This is worth sharpening. One hundred percent of decisions being made by AI in Russia does not mean that a black-box neural network decides the client's fate uncontrolled. It means a high degree of pipeline automation on interpretable models with reason codes, regulatory oversight by the Bank of Russia, and a preserved client right to a human. The AI type is tabular ML, chosen precisely because of its explainability and auditability, not despite them. This illustrates the lecture's thesis at the scale of the country's largest bank: the right type of AI is dictated by the task and the regulation, not by trendiness. When you hear «the bank automated one hundred percent of decisions», the right engineering question is not «isn't that scary» but «what harness is built around that automation»: reason codes, oversight, the right to a human, a bias audit.
