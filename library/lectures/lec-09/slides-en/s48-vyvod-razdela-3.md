## Visible slide text

A general- or special-purpose model expands how many options can be considered per unit of time — or, for generative design, expands the space of possible options itself. **It does not replace the final check in any of the four cases.**

**Bridge to Section 4:** if a model can work from anywhere, with no physical tie to a location — where is it physically most advantageous to place it?

## Speaker notes

A general- or special-purpose model expands how many options can be considered per unit of time, or — in the case of generative design — expands the space of possible options itself, without replacing the final engineering check in any of the four breakdowns. This is the common conclusion for all four breakdowns in the section, and it directly contradicts the simplified reading "the model now does the engineering work instead of the human": the model does part of the work, the final word stays with the classical computation, simulation, or test.

For the Raptor surrogate model, all three selection conditions are met, although the confirmation here is weaker than in many other breakdowns: SpaceX does not publish exact numbers on either side. For Claude/Anthropic, the first condition is met literally, the second unconditionally, the third is column b with an honest caveat. For AIFS/AIGEFS, all three conditions are met with the most solid confirmation in the section: remove the model and you're back to hours of computation on a supercomputer, directly confirmed by official publications. For generative design, the first condition is met in its strongest form among the four breakdowns — without the search algorithm over the space of shapes, the engineer would not have considered the variants the algorithm considered; this is not "the same thing, faster," but "a differently structured process."

Further along the cycle — the reverse question: if a model can perform this work from anywhere, with no physical tie to any specific place on Earth, then where is it physically most advantageous to place it? Section 4 answers this question literally.
