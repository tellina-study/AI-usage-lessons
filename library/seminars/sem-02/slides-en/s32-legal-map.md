# Sales calls

## Assertion

"Wait. Which service are you sending these recordings to?"

## Visual

At the top — a scene card: a lawyer at a prototype demo hears a
recording where a client dictates their passport details for a delivery
contract, and reacts. Below — three mini legal-map cards. **RF**: 152-FZ
(the base personal-data law) + as of 05/30/2025 (420-FZ) — first breach:
a flat fine of RUB 3-5 million; repeat breach: a turnover-based fine of
1-3% of annual revenue (minimum RUB 20 million, maximum RUB 500 million).
**EU**: GDPR — up to €20 million or 4% of global annual turnover
(whichever is greater); the record fine — Meta, €1.2 billion, 2023.
**US**: no single federal law; state-level (California — CCPA/CPRA) and
sector-specific (e.g., HIPAA for medical data) laws. At the bottom — a
takeaway line: "every major jurisdiction is seriously fighting to protect
personal data."

## Speaker notes

A lawyer walked into a prototype demo and heard a recording: a client
dictating their passport details for a delivery contract. "Wait. Which
service are you sending these recordings to?" None of the engineers had
thought about that in advance — everyone was only listening for needs and
objections, because that's exactly what the sales manager had asked for.
Look at the legal map. In Russia the base personal-data law is 152-FZ,
and as of May 30, 2025, the 420-FZ amendments are in force: for a first
breach — a flat fine of 3 to 5 million rubles, for a repeat breach — a
turnover-based fine of 1 to 3% of the company's annual revenue, minimum
20 million, maximum 500 million rubles. In the European Union, under
GDPR — up to 20 million euros or 4% of global annual turnover, whichever
is greater; the record fine went to Meta — 1.2 billion euros in 2023. In
the US there's no single federal law protecting personal data — there are
state laws, for example California's CCPA and CPRA, and sector-specific
ones, like HIPAA for medical data. One takeaway: every major jurisdiction
is seriously fighting to protect personal data, and this isn't an
abstract risk, it's concrete amounts written into the laws of different
countries.
