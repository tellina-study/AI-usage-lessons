## Speaker notes

The photo shows a demonstration involving two astronauts at the LUNA facility, an analog complex of the European Space Agency and the German Aerospace Center (DLR) in Cologne. This needs to be said honestly and right away: despite the name, the photo shows Earth — a facility covered in lunar regolith simulant, built precisely to test hardware and procedures under lunar-like conditions before they ever actually fly.

The neighboring breakdown has already named the status of predictive latency compensation directly: a research stage, not deployed practice, confirmed at lunar analog facilities, not on a real mission. This photo is a literal illustration of that caveat: a lunar-like setting, real people in real spacesuits or simulation suits, but under Earth's sky and Earth's gravity.

The difference between the facility and the actual Moon is not a formality. The facility has no real multi-second communication delay produced by the distance to the Moon — it has to be artificially simulated, along with the rest of the conditions of a lunar operation. That is why the confirmation here is weaker than for Dragon or Astrobee, which routinely operate under real conditions: the confirmed effect (reducing task time by up to 26%) was measured under lunar-approximating conditions, not on the Moon itself.
