## Requirements
Requirements — the first artifact, before any code

## Tag
Strong phase · spec-driven · 1 failure

## Narrative bridge
The first phase of the cycle turns fuzzy intent into a specification. The leading thing here is not the tool but the discipline: between intent and code a human-reviewed artifact is placed, and the decision "what exactly we need" stays human.

## Speaker notes

We begin to go through the lifecycle by phase, and the first is requirements. This is the phase that turns fuzzy human intent "I want a system that does X" into a structured artifact — a specification. The phase's input is intent, the output is the specification, the plan, the decomposition into tasks that the next phase reads.

The leading thing in this phase is not the tool but the spec-driven discipline: the first artifact of development is not code but the specification, which the human wrote, possibly with the help of AI, read, and accepted. AI is strong here, but strong narrowly: it amplifies the structuring and completeness of expressing intent, while the intent itself — the decision of what to build — remains the human's essential complexity. Tools like Spec-Kit or plan mode are a secondary illustration of what this discipline is executed with. In this section we will examine the practice itself, the three recommendations of the methods, and one characteristic failure of this phase. Keep two questions of the phase in mind: where AI amplifies — and where the human is required.
