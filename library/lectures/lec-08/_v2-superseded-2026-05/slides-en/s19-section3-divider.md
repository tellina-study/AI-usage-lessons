## Speaker notes

We finish the second section on changes to the economics and move into Section three — the main failure budget of this lecture. This is the longest section: twenty-four minutes, twelve cases plus the taxonomy. Each case is structured identically: assertion, evidence from verified sources, implication, and an explicit "Lesson for the engineer" in an Ocean rounded box. This is deliberate — without an explicit lesson a case stays "an interesting story," and we need the student to carry away a transferable principle from each one. The twelve cases cover four landmark copyright lawsuits — NYT v. OpenAI, Getty v. Stability, Andersen v. Stability, RIAA v. Suno, plus Thomson Reuters v. Ross as the first US ruling rejecting "fair use"; then deepfakes — the Arup CFO scam for twenty-five point six million dollars and the Korea schoolgirl crisis; then content failures — slop, Sports Illustrated fake authors, Coca-Cola and Toys R Us marketing failure; and the section closes with a consolidated displacement view. After the twelfth case you should be able to see the pattern: AI failure in the creative field is not one type of risk but a family of different ones, and the engineer must be able to distinguish which class of risk applies to their specific task.
