# Lecture map — 6 sections + Q&A

## Assertion

The lecture is structured as 6 sections: ADDED → CHANGED → BROKE → where AI is not needed → what an engineer should do → Q&A.

## Visual

A large horizontal roadmap of 6 cards: Section 0 (opening + keystone), Section 1 (ADDED — new capabilities), Section 2 (CHANGED — pipeline and economics), Section 3 (BROKE — failure budget), Section 4 (where AI is not needed), Section 5 (what an engineer should do). Each card has its number 0-5 large at the top, a 2-3-word title, and one line — what is inside. The current position (Section 0) is highlighted gold; future ones — light teal-tint. Below the roadmap — a Q&A tag separately. Above — an anchor phrase "6 sections · 1 checklist on the way out."

## Speaker notes

The further structure of the lecture is six sections plus Q&A. Right now we are in Section zero, which has just begun: opening, central question, keystone axis. The next three sections are structured along this keystone axis: Section one — what AI ADDED, new capabilities that did not exist before; Section two — what AI CHANGED in existing pipelines and economics; Section three — what AI BROKE, the main failure budget with concrete landmark cases. After that, Section four switches the register from diagnostic to normative: where an engineer is wise to say "AI is not needed here," by what criteria. Section five gives a checklist — the practical deliverable a student takes with them. And the lecture closes with Q&A. On the way out — one checklist of five questions, applicable to any future creative task with an AI component. Section three, as the main failure budget, takes up nearly a third of the lecture — this is structurally important for the course's educational goal. This map slide will stay in memory as a landmark: if at some point you are unsure where we are and where we are going, recall these six cards.
