## Visible slide text

**Not an illustration, but the task's own material.** It was from frames like this one from the front camera that Claude stitched together a route out of 10-meter segments and placed waypoints.

**The date is not an arbitrary number.** Sol 1707, December 8, 2025 — the day of the first of two drives that together covered 456 meters.

## Speaker notes

The photo shows a frame from the front hazard camera of the Perseverance rover, taken on the 1707th Martian sol, December 8, 2025. This is not a stock illustration of the Jezero crater terrain "in general," but a specific photo from a specific day on which, according to Anthropic and NASA JPL, Claude analyzed HiRISE orbital imagery and terrain data and planned a route across exactly this stretch of surface.

The neighboring breakdown already named the result: a 456-meter route, covered over two sols (the 8th and 10th of December), and the fact that the final check of the plan was performed not by Claude, but by JPL's digital twin, which ran the plan through more than 500,000 telemetry variables for compatibility with the flight software. This photo is the material Claude actually worked with: rocks, terrain, shadows, all of which had to be accounted for when stitching together a safe route out of short segments.

It's worth saying explicitly why this exact frame is more valuable for the breakdown than a generic illustration of the Martian surface could be: this is the only case in the entire lecture where an image is available from literally the day and event named in the breakdown, not just a thematically similar photo of the same place or instrument.
