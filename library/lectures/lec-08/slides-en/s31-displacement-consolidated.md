## Speaker notes

The eleventh case — the last in the section on AI BROKE. This is not a separate case story; it is a consolidated view of displacement, which we already partly addressed in Section 2. Here we focus on the critical inference: displacement is not a temporary shock but a structural shift, and the engineer must understand this before deploying an AI tool into the creative industry. Three key stats. First — minus seventeen point zero one percent of work in the Upwork graphic-design category after the release of the main GenAI image tools. This is no longer statistical noise — it is a structural displacement of bottom-segment graphic-design freelance. Second — income compression. AI detection in forty percent of copywriter jobs paying ten-to-nineteen dollars per hour, versus under ten percent in jobs at sixty-plus dollars per hour. Wage compression from the bottom. AI tools wash out the bottom segment of work, leaving high-value specialized roles untouched. Third — Hollywood's institutional response. SAG-AFTRA plus WGA signed a four-year extension with AMPTP in 2026. The AI clauses won — Digital Replicas, requiring informed consent plus compensation for using a real performer's likeness; Synthetic Performers regulation; on the WGA side — disclosure for AI-generated material. What these clauses do not cover — bottom-segment freelance, B-roll actors, extras, commodity voiceover. This market keeps shrinking. Industry consolidation as a response. Getty plus Shutterstock merger, three point seven billion dollars in January 2025 — defensive consolidation. This is an institutional acknowledgment that separately, stock providers cannot withstand the cost collapse. Lesson for the engineer. Displacement — structural, not a temporary shock. Clauses help only in the top segment of protected roles; bottom-segment wage compression remains. If you develop an AI tool that deploys into the creative industry, analyze before launch which class of workers your tool will displace. If it is bottom-segment commodity work — this is already happening, and it is "legal" in the sense of labor law but creates ESG plus regulatory pressure. If it is top-segment creative direction — this will not work, because it is precisely this zone that is protected by the value-add of human curation. Understanding this distinction is part of engineering maturity in 2026.
