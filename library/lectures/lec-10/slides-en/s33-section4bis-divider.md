## Speaker notes

This section is a cross-cutting meta-level. It doesn't describe another rung of the ladder; it describes the general environmental conditions without which none of the rungs works. All three subsections — connectivity, vendor lock-in, regulation — are strictly a failure perspective.

Connectivity. Eighteen percent of American farms without internet at all; one hundred twenty-three thousand flights with GNSS interference in the first four months of 2025; Starlink banned in Russia from April thirtieth, 2026 for half a year. This is AP-five: cloud-first for an off-grid farm is an architectural error. The alternative — edge ML and TinyML.

Vendor lock-in. January 2025 — the FTC lawsuit against John Deere over decade-long repair restrictions. May 2022 — the Melitopol remote-brick: Deere remotely disabled twenty-seven pieces of equipment that had moved from occupied Zaporizhzhia Oblast to Chechnya. December 2025 — the FCC ban on DJI ag-drones: eighty percent of the US ag-spray drone fleet under threat. And the most important idea of the section — the double optic. One and the same mechanism — geo-locking plus VIN-locking — yields two opposite interpretations: an anti-theft success on one side, a vendor control surface on the other. An AI security feature today is an AI control surface tomorrow.

Regulation. The EU AI Act high-risk classification for autonomous agricultural machinery since February 2025, plus AI literacy for operators, plus a liability cascade. The USDA AI Strategy is formal. Russia's program "Agriculture of the Future" 2026-2030 is declarative — the successor of "Digital Agriculture", which failed its goal of doubling productivity.

And these are the three anti-AI criteria that complete the five for consolidation: AP-five about connectivity, AP-six about vendor lock-in, AP-seven about AI-MRV without direct measurement — the last already introduced in the section on Verra.
