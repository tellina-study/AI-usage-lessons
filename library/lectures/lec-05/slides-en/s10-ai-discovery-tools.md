## Speaker notes

Now — what AI changes in this phase, concretely and by tool. Desk research — a review of market reports, complaints, tickets — down to a single hour. Perplexity, especially its Deep Research mode, is widely recognized as the best tool for this task: the service plans a research trajectory, reads sources, and returns a report with links in minutes. Practitioners report roughly a fifty-percent time reduction. Mandatory practice, not an option: verify important data directly against the source before using it in a decision — links exist precisely so verification is possible, but the tool doesn't remove the verification step itself.

Interview synthesis: Dovetail is specifically built for cross-interview synthesis with a "quote anchored to its source" design — this protects against drift into an unverifiable summary. AI is genuinely strong at clustering — grouping hundreds of tickets into recurring pain themes — and at building a reference dataset, a curated set of "query → correct answer" pairs, which we'll come back to in sections three and four as a formal launch artifact and an evaluation set.

Synthetic users as pre-research is a narrow, legitimate area: piloting a discussion guide, drafting proto-personas, generating hypotheses. None of these outputs should be treated as evidence for a "ship or not" decision. Telling detail: ninety-seven percent of researchers use AI, but only about eight percent trust AI-generated participants as a data source — the gap is recognized by the profession itself. Teresa Torres, one of the leading discovery practitioners, uses AI to draft a first pass, but the two-step synthesis rule holds without exception, and she explicitly advises against synthetic interviews — because the team's own thinking is the whole point of the process.
