## Visible slide text
**For physicians the same check begins with the cost of a miss. A bad examination looks like a good one in which nothing was found** — the physician has no signal of his own.

**Both gains are of the same order as the assistant's, or larger, and neither is free:** three minutes of the physician's time per procedure, and a second person in the room.

**The baselines differ:** 271 per 1,000 for the first order of work, 393 for the second — different hospitals and patients. The gains do not add up.

## Speaker notes

The withdrawal examination is the procedure step where a finding either gets seen, or doesn't. The cost of a miss is named here before any accuracy, and the first thing to understand about this stage is: feedback arrives years later. A missed growth stays in the person until the next examination — this is exactly how an interval cancer arises, found between examinations. Seeing too much costs removal, sometimes bleeding, histology, and a repeat visit. Neither the physician nor the model set the asymmetry between these two costs: a health authority set it, when it decided who to call in for examination, and how often.

About the unit the whole section counts in, and this has to be said once, here. The quantity the profession uses to measure an endoscopist is called the adenoma detection rate (ADR): the share of screening procedures in which the physician found at least one precancerous growth, out of all his screening procedures. On the section's slides it is converted into a number of procedures per thousand — that way it can be added and compared aloud without confusing it with a share of patients. Two hundred seventy-one procedures with a finding out of a thousand means: nothing was found in the remaining seven hundred twenty-nine, and part of those "nothings" is not the absence of an adenoma but an adenoma that was missed. The rate is built cleverly, and that is its strength: a growth that requires looking behind a fold lands in the denominator regardless of whether the physician looked, and lands in the numerator only if he did. So it measures behavior, not reporting about it, and it cannot be raised by trying harder in how you write it up.

Why this spot is built more dangerously than the others. In engineering work, the nearest build or check reports a miss the same day. Here, the nearest measuring instrument is the same patient's next visit, years later, and to a different physician. A poorly performed examination does not feel bad in that minute, or a month later, and the person has no signal of his own about a decline at all. That is why the procedure counts the discipline on his behalf — he himself cannot find out.

Now the second step of the breakdown's form, the one whose omission is where breakdowns go astray. A model compared against nothing always wins, and that win means nothing: wherever the assistant arrives, two orders of work were already operating there, and both are measured in the same unit.

The first mode — discipline of withdrawal time. Withdrawal time was extended from six minutes to nine. A randomized trial, 1,027 patients, 514 against 513, time timed by stopwatch: procedures with a finding became 366 per 1,000 against 271, that is, the detection rate rose from 27.1 to 36.6 percent at a significance level of 0.001. A separate observation also gives a lever on the mode: each additional minute of withdrawal adds about six percent to the odds of finding an adenoma, measured in a window from six to thirteen minutes.

The second mode — a second observer in the room: alongside the physician, a nurse also watches the same screen. A pooled analysis of three randomized trials on 1,676 patients: from 393 procedures per 1,000 up to 457, that is, from 39.3 to 45.7 percent, relative risk 1.16 at an interval of 1.04 to 1.3. A second pooled analysis, across eleven trials and 8,278 patients, gives 1.15, from 1.05 to 1.26. The reviewers' own caveat is named together with the number: all the included trials carry a high risk of systematic bias, and there is no gain at all in this same review for polyp detection or for advanced findings. And here is my own caveat: part of the effect may be the effect of being observed while observing, and these trials could not separate one from the other.

A caveat about the baselines. The starting rates for the two modes differ — 271 procedures per 1,000 for the first and 393 for the second, different populations and different centers — so the two gains are not compared against each other. Each is compared with the assistant's gain in its own body of work, not with the other; adding plus ninety-five and plus sixty-four into "plus one hundred fifty-nine" is not valid in any form.

The conclusion this step stands apart for: both classical modes deliver a gain of the same order as the assistant, or larger, and neither is free — the first costs three minutes of the physician's time per procedure, the second costs the presence of a second person.

And nobody has measured a drain on skill for these two modes: there is no measurement. To say "they don't drain skill" would be claiming more than has been measured — exactly what this lecture examines vendors for.
