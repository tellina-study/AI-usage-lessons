## Speaker notes

Let's formulate the central question that the lecture will return to in every section — five explicit points of return. Finance and retail are the industries of the deepest AI adoption; the engineer's question here is not "can AI be applied", but for which task which type of AI, why exactly that one rather than a language model everywhere, and where this specific type breaks.

The first part of the question — "which type and why exactly that one" — is analyzed in each of the five substantive sections through a unified card pattern. This is a five-step frame through which we analyze each type in the same way: what is the task, what type of AI and why exactly that one, a real example from Russia and the world, where it breaks with a documented failure, and what the correct alternative is with a criterion for inapplicability. The second part of the question — "where it breaks" — is through-going: each section ends with an analysis of a real failure of exactly this type of AI, with a lesson learned and a criterion.

An important disclaimer. This unified card pattern is a didactic construct of the lecture, an analog of the ladder of autonomy from Lecture 4: a way to organize the material so that five different types do not turn into a list of technologies, but come together into an apparatus of judgment. It is not an industry standard. The map of five types that we will go through, plus the through-going layer of computer vision — it does not need to be understood all at once. Each type is analyzed by one and the same scheme, and by the end of the lecture the scheme will become your working tool for making the choice. The answer will not be "AI is good" and not "AI is bad", but an apparatus with which, for a specific task, you name the appropriate type, justify the choice, and see in advance the point of mandatory human control.
