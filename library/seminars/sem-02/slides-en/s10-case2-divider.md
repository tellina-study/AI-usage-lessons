# Build it in, or build something separate?

## Assertion

The second choice: should the solution live inside an existing interface, or on its own

## Visual

Large title naming the choice on a neutral background + a background illustration
(designer to choose) + a single comment line with no spoiler.

## Speaker notes

The second question today: build the solution into what people already use every
day, or build a separate product for it. On paper, both options look
reasonable. A built-in solution promises seamlessness; a separate product
promises freedom — its own interface, its own development pace, no constraints
from someone else's system. And each option has a hidden cost that's almost
never visible in a demo or on a pitch slide — it shows up later, in real
operation, once actual people start using the solution in their actual
workflow, not the prototype's authors at a showcase. That's exactly why these
decisions can't be made on the beauty of the concept alone — you need to dig
into the specific circumstances of the company and the task. Let's move to the
setup — it comes from someone who's accountable for the outcome, not for how
nice the idea looks on a slide.
