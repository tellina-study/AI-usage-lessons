## Speaker notes

We looked at the bracket and established that it was designed not by generative AI but by a deterministic optimizer. Next comes the first of the six parts: the foundation and the taxonomy.

In this part we first establish one distinction that runs through the whole lecture as its load-bearing axis: deterministic optimization solves an equation set by the engineer, while a trained generative model guesses the answer from learned examples and contains no physical law inside it. Without this distinction, all further discussion of where AI fails would sound like "it sometimes makes mistakes"; with it, it becomes clear why the errors of some classes are structurally inevitable, and of others are not.

Then we introduce the six classes of AI found in engineering design, placed on one axis from deterministic to probabilistic. For now this is only a skeleton — one axis and six labels with short definitions; the full attribute matrix will be assembled by the end of the lecture, once every one of its cells has been filled with meaning. This part is needed so that further on we don't confuse topology optimization with a generative neural network, or a surrogate with a solver — these are different classes with different guarantees.
