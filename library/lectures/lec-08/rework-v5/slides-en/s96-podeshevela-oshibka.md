## Speaker notes

For sixty years the tool of creative production got cheaper seven times, and each time the same thing happened: the intermediary operation disappeared — the link where a human mechanically carried an idea onto a medium. Ink tracing disappeared when an animator's drawing started being transferred onto celluloid directly. The prepress shop disappeared when retouching moved from dedicated systems to an ordinary workstation. Physically splicing film disappeared when editing became nonlinear. A retake for the sake of one note disappeared. Shooting on commission for a catalog disappeared when microstock appeared. Hand-animating a face from reference disappeared. A location trip for a backdrop disappeared when the backdrop started being rendered in a game engine right on set. The profession, meanwhile, stayed each time and moved onto the new tool.

In 2022, something different got cheaper. A first approximation of an idea came to cost fractions of a cent, and the industry's entire win comes from that. Making seventy thousand variants and picking one became cheaper than making one and hitting the mark. That is exactly why execution was the last thing to be rebuilt, while the first thing to be rebuilt was selection — a stage that barely existed before as a separate piece of work, because there was almost nothing to look at: when a trial cost money, few were made.

Both halves of the evening follow from this, and both are honest. Both have to be said: a lecture that stops at the first one teaches trust instead of judgment.

The first half. Those who learned to choose, won. One person, with three to four hundred generations and two days, puts together a spot for the National Basketball Association finals. Five specialists, in thirty days, deliver a global brand's Christmas campaign. An entire short-drama industry switches to a new production method in a single year and releases one episode every thirty-six seconds. A person whose illness took away their speech gets their voice back, minute by minute, from old recordings. A scroll that could not be unrolled without destroying it is read in full — and not a single letter in it was generated.

The second half. Those who used to be recognized for free, now pay. Voice, face, signature, and manner stopped confirming themselves, and confirmation started to cost money and the verifier's labor. A recognizer that is flawless on a familiar model gives a coin flip on an unfamiliar one. In newsrooms where the share of machine-written articles passed one-fifth, the number of named experts per thousand words dropped by twenty-two percent — these are people who stopped being asked to comment, and nobody explained to them why. A voice actress who recorded six hundred pages under a contract that never used the word "synthesis" heard her own voice in someone else's ad and found no statute to defend it under. That is the bill for the cheap mistake, and it is not split evenly.

The eighth round of getting cheaper differs from the previous seven in kind, not in size. Each of the seven removed an operation — a concrete action a person did by hand and stopped doing. The profession stayed whole through each of them: the same artist moved from ink to a tablet, the same editor from film to a nonlinear station, and the craft moved along with them. The eighth removed the price of a miss. The operation itself stayed in place, and the profession did not move over whole — it split in two. Those whose value lies in choosing got a tool that multiplies the number of variants by an order to an order and a half, and immediately became more needed. Those whose value lay in being recognized for free — a voice people trusted, a signature nobody checked, a face that vouched for itself — gained nothing and got a bill.

What an engineer should take away from this is one working idea, applicable far beyond the creative industries, and it was worth sitting through the whole evening for. When the price of some step in a piece of work drops to nearly zero, the work doesn't disappear — it moves onto the neighboring step that didn't get cheaper. Finding that neighboring step ahead of time is exactly the professional skill. In 2022, that step turned out to be selection. Next time it will be something else, and you will look for it the same way: by asking what exactly came to cost fractions of a cent, and who now does the work that used to happen on its own.
