## Speaker notes

Section four is about where AI breaks, errs, and is dangerous. This isn't a chamber of horrors — it's an inventory of the classes of problems that systematically arise in AI systems and that an engineer must be able to anticipate. The topic is your zone of responsibility, not a separate question for AI safety specialists: you've already made the decision to build AI in, and you and your team — not "the model" — will answer for an incident: a data leak, a wrong decision about a customer, an erroneous automated verdict.

On top of that, AI errs systematically and predictably: bias, hallucinations, sycophancy, distribution shift recur from system to system — these aren't random bugs but properties of the technology. And, finally, the boundary of "what AI can't do" is also your zone of responsibility: beyond it you need a human decision, verification, a fallback scenario, and without an understanding of this boundary a system can't be considered designed.

The section runs through seven topics, arranged from practical engineering safety — what you can break or lose right now — to the conceptual boundaries of AI as a class of technologies — what AI fundamentally can't do.
