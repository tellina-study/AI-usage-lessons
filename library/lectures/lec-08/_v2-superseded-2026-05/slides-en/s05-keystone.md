# AI added → changed → broke

## Assertion

AI ADDED → CHANGED → BROKE. Three tenses of one process — each generation of creative tools passes through them in months.

## Visual

Large heading "AI added → changed → broke" 44pt deep blue. Below the heading — 3 horizontal bands of equal height (3 tenses), each in its own Ocean-palette color: ADDED (mid blue), CHANGED (light teal), BROKE (gold-tinted outline). In each band — iconography on the left + a 1-phrase essence + 1 example from the lecture on the right. The ADDED example — "Sora 2: 25 sec text-to-video"; for CHANGED — "Cost collapse 100×–10,000×"; for BROKE — "RIAA v. Suno, Arup deepfake $25.6M." Below, in small type — anchor "This is the axis of three tenses of one process, not three parallel categories."

## Speaker notes

This is the key conceptual axis of the whole lecture. The structure is built on one axis: AI ADDED — what AI CHANGED — what AI BROKE. These are three tenses of one process, not three parallel categories. Each class of creative tool, as it appears, sequentially passes through all three tenses in months. ADDED — the moment a new capability appears. Sora 2 added text-to-video with synchronized audio. Midjourney added character preservation across generations. ElevenLabs added voice cloning from a minimal sample. CHANGED — the moment a new capability penetrates an existing pipeline and changes its economics. Cost-per-asset falls a hundred to ten thousand times over. Generation speed collapses from days to seconds. New professions appear — prompt engineer, AI director — and at the same time old ones are washed out. BROKE — the moment a capability creates a new class of failures and legal debt. Sony Music sues Suno. The New York Times sues OpenAI over twenty million logs. An Arup CFO loses twenty-five million dollars to a deepfake. Why exactly this axis: for an industry lecture about creative tools there is a very strong temptation to read it as "top tools of 2026" — a Sora 2 vs Veo 3.1 vs Kling 3.0 review. Such a structure turns the lecture into a marketing sales brief. The "added-changed-broke" axis keeps a critical view: each tool class first appears as a capability, then changes the economics, and at the same time creates legal and reputational debt. This is the axis of three tenses of one process, and that is why it leads directly to thirty percent of content about failures and limitations without trampling over a tools review.
