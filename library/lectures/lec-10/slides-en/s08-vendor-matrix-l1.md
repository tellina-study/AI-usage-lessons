# L1 vendor matrix — five platforms, different modes

## Assertion

xarvio / FieldView / Cropwise / Granular / Taranis — five L1 platforms with different geographies, different operating modes, and different business models. Brand ≠ mode. «250M acres FieldView» = subscriptions, not «AI optimizes every acre».

## Speaker notes

Beyond See & Spray, five more large platforms operate on the first rung. But the operating mode of each is worth understanding separately, because marketing of the "AI advisory" type conceals substantial differences — and an engineer evaluating a vendor is obligated to distinguish them.

The first is BASF xarvio FIELD MANAGER. More than one hundred thirty thousand farmers, twenty million hectares under management, more than one hundred countries. The operating mode is rule-based agronomic recommendations plus classical ML for optimization; not deep learning end-to-end. The most interesting development — in October 2025, BASF Japan launched the world's first outcome-based rice yield guarantee via xarvio with the Humus AI service. This is a fundamentally new model of AgTech-vendor responsibility: the vendor guarantees an output metric, not an input. This is the gold point of the map — the only example of an outcome-based contract at L1 in 2026.

The second is Climate FieldView from Bayer. More than two hundred fifty million subscribed acres in twenty-three countries; in the US that's more than half of all corn, soybean, and cotton acreage. And here is the critical anti-hype caveat: two hundred fifty million acres are subscriptions to the platform, not "AI optimizes every acre". Most of the functionality is visualization, weather forecasting, seed-hybrid recommendations; deep learning is applied in sub-tasks, but that is not the main operating mode. Plus vendor lock-in — the recommendations are tied to Bayer and Pioneer hybrids. And there is a political lesson: Climate FieldView left Russia in twenty twenty-two, and Russian agroholdings that had invested in the platform lost access all at once. We'll return to this in Section 4-bis.

The third is Cropwise from Syngenta. An open platform with a developer API, announced in October 2025; third parties develop agro applications on top. An intermediate position between a closed system and open source.

The fourth is Granular Insights, Corteva. A narrow focus on prescription planning; three-meter satellite imagery plus variable-rate prescriptions. Less functionality than FieldView, but within its niche — it works.

The fifth is Taranis. A premium product for large farms; high-resolution aerial imagery plus deep learning for disease and insect detection. Not for smallholders.

The main lesson of this slide — this is a family of products with different modes and different business models. "L1 AI platform" as a single category is marketing packaging; real analysis requires deconstructing "brand → operating mode → business model → geography". This is the first practical skill we'll train on concrete cases in this section.
